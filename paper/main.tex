\begin{abstract}
\flag{[PROPOSED, awaiting approval]} Most glioma surveillance datasets lack treatment dates, centres document
progression differently, cohorts are small and the hardest scans are few. We built PRISM, a modular system that
detects the scan at which progression is documented through separate image and treatment-timeline pathways, attaches
a confidence to each scan and writes a report with a local language model. On MU-Glioma-Post the timeline pathway
reached an AUC of 0.82, above the image pathway. Fitted once and applied unchanged, the models reached 0.77 on
LUMIERE; on UCSF-ALPTDG, single-scan measurements reached 0.80 and the image pathway with change from the previous
scan 0.73, against 0.86 when trained there. An audit showed the scores rested mainly on the treatment timeline at
MU-Glioma-Post and on enhancing tumour volume at UCSF-ALPTDG. Conformal sets for the combined score met their
pre-specified coverage at all three centres, and after one revision every report passed a deterministic check
against its record.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Introduction 1); refs taal2008, brandes2008.
After surgery and chemoradiotherapy, patients with glioma are followed with MRI every few months, and at each scan
the team must decide whether the tumour has progressed. The decision is hardest in the months after radiotherapy,
when treatment can increase contrast enhancement without tumour growth. This pseudoprogression is most common in the
first weeks after chemoradiotherapy and more frequent when the MGMT promoter is methylated
\citep{taal2008,brandes2008}. In routine care, progression is therefore documented from the images together with
symptoms and the course of treatment, and the date on which it is recorded depends on when the patient was scanned,
so a model trained on such records can learn from the schedule as well as from the images.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine C1--C5); SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md rows 1.7, 6.3; refs an2021, varoquaux2018, yu2022.
Models for this task meet five challenges that come from the data rather than from any one method. First, most
public surveillance datasets hold images but few treatment dates, so a model that needs the treatment timeline cannot
be applied to them. Second, centres document progression in different ways, from dated clinical decisions to expert
ratings of each scan, so a model can score well at two centres for different reasons. Third, cohorts are small, and
at this size the choice of one test split can move an AUC by more than a change of model \citep{an2021,varoquaux2018}.
Fourth, the situations in which the decision is hardest, early after radiotherapy or when enhancement grows, hold few
cases in any one cohort, so accuracy there is rarely measured. Fifth, tumour volumes from automatic segmentation are
noisy, so the clinician needs to see what was measured; and patient records often cannot leave the hospital, so any
report must be written inside it. External tests address the second and third challenges. In a recent systematic
review of automated response assessment, 3 of 20 studies used external test data \citep{shi2025}, and deep learning
models in radiology usually lose some accuracy on external data \citep{yu2022}.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (question).
We asked whether a system could give the clinician, at each post-treatment scan, the image evidence and the
treatment-timeline evidence separately, transfer to other centres without refitting, and say how far each can be
trusted there. We also wanted it to explain each score in a short report written inside the hospital.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim); NUMBERS.md sections 3--7.
PRISM is a modular system built for this. We developed it on MU-Glioma-Post (MU) and tested it on LUMIERE and
UCSF-ALPTDG (UCSF) with models fitted once and external tests planned in advance. It scores each scan through two
pathways, one from clinical data and the treatment timeline and one from measurements of the segmented tumour, and
shows a combined score beside them. It attaches a confidence made of a conformal prediction set, a statement of
whether the pathways agree and a reliability card, and writes a report with a local language model whose every
sentence is checked against the scan's record. On MU both pathways detected the scan at which progression was
documented; the models fitted there reached an AUC of 0.77 at LUMIERE, and the image measurements transferred to
UCSF. An audit of the scores showed that they rested mainly on the patient's position in treatment at MU and on the
enhancing tumour volume at UCSF. The conformal sets for the combined score met their pre-specified coverage at all
three centres, and after one revision of the prompt, following a failure at one cohort, the reports were faithful to
their records.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:2}

\subsection{PRISM}\label{sec:2.1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine table); SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 1.
PRISM handles each scan in five modules (Figure~\ref{fig:1}). The timeline pathway and the image pathway keep the two
kinds of evidence apart, so that the image pathway can run where no treatment dates exist and the audit can show which
evidence a score used. Segmentation masks supply the measurements and the images the clinician sees. The confidence
addresses the rare situations, and the report presents all of it without the record leaving the hospital. The third
challenge, small cohorts, is handled by how PRISM is evaluated (Section~\ref{sec:2.3}).

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, B1, B0; External test, Tbase); UCSF_RESULTS.md deviation 3; NUMBERS.md 2.1.
The timeline pathway scores a scan from clinical data and the patient's position in treatment. On MU its inputs are
age, sex, IDH mutation and MGMT promoter methylation, which alone form the clinical-only reference model, together
with the days since diagnosis and since surgery, the start, end and dose of radiotherapy, chemotherapy and the visit
index, each taken as of the scan day. Where a cohort records no treatment dates, PRISM uses a reduced timeline that
keeps the clinical inputs, the days since surgery and a flag for the first weeks after radiotherapy. At LUMIERE the
flag is the rater's note that the scan was less than three months after radiotherapy; at UCSF it is approximated from
the start of radiotherapy. Both pathways are ridge logistic regressions, with every fitted step inside the training
data.

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (EXPLORATORY pure imaging); UCSF_IMAGING.md; SPLIT_VARIANCE.md; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md Deviations 1.
The image pathway scores a scan from simple measurements of the segmented tumour. On MU it uses 27 inputs: the log
volumes of six tumour compartments, including enhancing tumour, necrosis, the surrounding FLAIR hyperintensity and the
resection cavity, with shape and relative intensity features; the log ratio of each volume to the previous scan; and
the ratio of the enhancing and total lesion volumes to the smallest volume in the patient's history. It holds no
dates, intervals or treatment flags. A single-scan version keeps only the 19 measurements of the current scan. At
LUMIERE, whose masks have three labels, the pathway uses three volumes and their change from the previous scan, and at
UCSF the history is the one earlier scan in the dataset. We defined these inputs after finding that the planned
imaging model contained inputs that encode the scan schedule (Section~\ref{sec:3.3}), so the image pathway on MU is
exploratory; its use at UCSF and in the system tests was planned. The combined score is one ridge model fitted on both
sets of inputs. At LUMIERE and UCSF its inputs are the reduced timeline with harmonised volumes, their change and the
interval since the previous scan, so there it holds one schedule input, that interval.

% Sources: SEG_FIGURE.md; UCSF_IMAGING.md; NUMBERS.md mismatch 6; refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
The measurements come from the segmentation masks supplied with each cohort: the automatic masks released with MU
\citep{mahmoud2025}, the DeepBraTumIA masks of LUMIERE \citep{suter2022} and the masks provided with UCSF
\citep{fields2024}. PRISM renders the report's figure from the same masks by code, and the language model receives
only the record, not the image.

% Sources: SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 2; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md Specification and "cost of the split"; RELIABILITY_CARD.md;
% refs angelopoulos2023, mitchell2019.
Each scan's confidence has three parts, a conformal set, a pathway-agreement statement and a reliability card. The
conformal set is a patient-level split conformal prediction set at the 80\% level \citep{angelopoulos2023}. It holds
one outcome when the score is clear and both outcomes when it is not, and it should contain the true outcome for at
least 80\% of scans. The set comes from the combined model refitted on 70\% of the MU patients and calibrated on the
remaining 30\%; on MU this split is made inside each training fold, and for the external cohorts one MU-calibrated
set was applied unchanged. Because of the refit, the set's model ranks scans slightly differently from the displayed
combined score (Supplementary Section~\todo{S24, system results, to be added}). The two pathways agree when both
scores lie on the same side of that pathway's median score over the cohort's evaluation scans; the rule uses scores
only, never labels. The reliability card looks up the scan's situation, its phase of care crossed with the change in
enhancing tumour since the previous scan, in a table of results measured in each cohort, in the spirit of a model
card \citep{mitchell2019}. It states the situation's tier: tested, with AUCs and intervals, when the situation holds
at least 10 progression and 10 non-progression scans; few cases, with counts only, when it holds fewer; and not
tested when it holds none. The agreement rule and the card were defined after the cohort results had been seen and
are exploratory.

% Sources: REPORT_V3.md (Design, Checker v3); REPORT_V3_1.md; Supplementary S19.
A local language model writes the report from the scan's record, and a deterministic checker tests every sentence
against that record. The model (Qwen3.8-27B) runs offline at temperature 0 and receives only the record; the checker
rejects any number, flag or confidence statement that is not in the record, any omission of required content, and any
wording that offers a diagnosis, advice or a probability (Supplementary Section~S19).

\subsection{Cohorts and the task}\label{sec:2.2}

% Sources: TABLE1_COHORT.md; UCSF_DATA.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
PRISM was developed on MU and tested on LUMIERE and UCSF with models fitted once on MU and applied unchanged, which we
call locked (Table~\ref{tab:1}). MU records the dates of surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy, and progression as a
documented date \citep{mahmoud2025}. LUMIERE records no treatment dates; each scan has an expert rating of
response, and the rater notes whether the scan falls within three months of radiotherapy \citep{suter2022}. UCSF
holds one pair of consecutive post-treatment scans per patient, with the date of progression and the start of
radiotherapy \citep{fields2024}. Because the first scan of each pair has no earlier scan, we evaluated the second scan
only. UCSF was obtained under a data use agreement. The primary population in every cohort is grade 4 glioma.

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, Cohort, External test); UCSF_PLAN.md; INTENDED_USE.md.
The task is detection at the scan: is this the scan at which progression is documented? On MU a positive is the first
scan on or after the documented progression date, and a negative is a scan followed by at least 120 days without
progression. At LUMIERE a positive is the first scan rated as progressive disease, and a negative is an earlier scan
rated stable or responding. At UCSF a positive is a second scan with progression dated within 30 days of it and
nearer to it than to the first scan; a negative is a second scan with progression at least 120 days later, or with at
least 120 days of follow-up without progression. On MU the intended use is scans from the start of radiotherapy
onwards, and every MU result below is for this population; LUMIERE and UCSF are evaluated on all scans in their
detection sets. We divide the intended use into phases of care: during radiotherapy, early after radiotherapy (up to
84 days, 12 weeks, after its end) and later. Predicting progression within 120 days of a scan, the task with which
this work began, is reported as a secondary task.

\subsection{Evaluation}\label{sec:2.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan); SPLIT_VARIANCE.md (Results table 1); Supplementary S8 and Figure S2.
% OUTLINE open question (split variance): settled here as one paragraph + supplementary pointer (council round 1
% recommendation, pending author). UCSF own-split result moved to RESERVE.md.
On MU, every model was evaluated by repeated patient-grouped cross-validation. We used five repeats of five folds,
with all scans of a patient in the same fold, and report the mean over repeats of the pooled held-out AUC with a 95\%
confidence interval (CI) from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap draws. In no analysis does a patient appear in more than
one of fitting, calibration and testing. We chose this design because in cohorts of this size one split can move an
AUC by more than a change of model. We saw this in our first evaluation, in which a radiomics model reached an AUC of
0.80 on one test split and averaged 0.72 when re-selected and re-tested over 500 splits of the same design
(Supplementary Section~S8 and Figure~S2).

% Sources: UCSF_PLAN.md (0e7c9fe); SYSTEM_PLAN.md (e7cba80); REPORT_V3_1_PLAN.md (4f7e7b9); UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A, B.
For the external tests, each model was fitted once on MU and applied once, under plans committed before any external
label was read. The locked models were fitted on all grade 4 detection scans of MU, progression and non-progression
scans from every phase of care; the UCSF plan named all MU detection scans, and fitting on all gliomas gave the same
results. External AUCs have 95\% CIs from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap draws. As a reference, we also fitted each
UCSF model on UCSF itself by repeated cross-validation. Table~\ref{tab:2} lists the questions fixed in advance and
their answers.

% Sources: NUMBERS.md (status column); OUTLINE.md decision 8.
Analyses that were not planned are labelled exploratory wherever they appear. They are the definition of the image
pathway on MU, the three-step comparison of image models, the clinical-only, image-only and timeline-only models at
LUMIERE, the single-scan comparison with models trained at UCSF, the audits of what each score rests on, the
agreement rule and the reliability card, a stricter flag rule, and the second evaluation of the reports, which
followed a failure seen in the first. Comparisons within a phase of care or within an enhancement category are
diagnostics; they test what a score rests on and set no decision rule.

\section{Results}\label{sec:3}

\subsection{Both pathways detect the progression scan on MU}\label{sec:3.1}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Primary table; EXPLORATORY pure imaging, images + clinical); INTENDED_USE.md; NUMBERS.md 3.
In the intended use on MU (146 scans; Table~\ref{tab:1}), the timeline pathway detected the scan at which
progression was documented with an AUC of 0.82 (95\% CI 0.75--0.90), and the image pathway with 0.71 (0.63--0.78). Clinical data alone, without the timeline, scored 0.53, close to chance. The combined score also
reached 0.82; on MU the images did not raise the timeline's AUC, and the planned imaging model fell short of the
pre-specified gain of 0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:2}). Over all scans of all gliomas the planned model did gain 0.04
(0.01--0.08), through image inputs that encode the scan schedule (Section~\ref{sec:3.3}). What the images contribute
on their own is examined within phases of care in Section~\ref{sec:3.3}. Predicting progression within 120 days was
harder; PRISM's model reached 0.60 (0.52--0.69) in the intended use (Supplementary Section~S23). A timeline pathway
needs treatment dates, which the two external cohorts largely lack, so we next tested how each pathway transfers to
them.

\subsection{The models transfer to two external centres without refitting}\label{sec:3.2}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (External test; EXPLORATORY clinical alone on LUMIERE); UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A; NUMBERS.md 4.
Fitted once on MU and applied unchanged, the combined model reached 0.77 (0.68--0.86) on LUMIERE (Figure~\ref{fig:2}),
about as high as in cross-validation on MU, and met its planned transfer rule (Table~\ref{tab:2}). At LUMIERE each
pathway added to the other; images alone scored 0.65 and the reduced timeline alone 0.69 (both exploratory), below the
combined score. Clinical data alone stayed close to chance in all three cohorts (0.55 at LUMIERE, exploratory, and
0.50 at UCSF).

% Sources: UCSF_RESULTS.md (Part A, Part B, Q1--Q3, contrasts); NUMBERS.md mismatch 1. Council ruling 1.
At UCSF, both planned image models transferred. Measurements of the single scan reached 0.80 (0.69--0.89), and the
image pathway, which adds change from the previous scan, reached 0.73 (0.60--0.84), meeting its planned transfer rule
(Table~\ref{tab:2}). Training at UCSF raised the image pathway to 0.86 (difference 0.14, 0.03--0.26), while the
single-scan model scored about the same when trained there (0.83, exploratory). The change inputs therefore transferred
less well than the measurements of the scan itself, which is consistent with the change between the two UCSF scans
spanning a much narrower range than at MU. The reduced timeline transferred least, at 0.56, because UCSF records only
the start of radiotherapy. These results show that PRISM's measurements transfer to a new centre, and that a model
trained at the centre can do better. Because similar AUCs at different centres can rest on different evidence, we
audited what each score used.

\subsection{What each score rests on depends on the cohort}\label{sec:3.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Cohort by phase; within phase; EXPLORATORY images + clinical); STORY.md gap 3.
The audit (exploratory) shows that at MU the scores rest mainly on where the patient is in treatment, and at UCSF on
how much the tumour enhances (Figure~\ref{fig:3}). At MU the scan schedule alone separates most of the two classes;
most progression scans came more than 12 weeks after radiotherapy and most non-progression scans came within those 12
weeks (Table~\ref{tab:1}). Across phases of care, adding the timeline to clinical data and images gained 0.11
(0.04--0.20); within a phase the gain was $-$0.02 ($-$0.13 to $+$0.11) (Figure~\ref{fig:3}A). Within a phase, images
alone reached 0.68 (0.58--0.77) and the timeline 0.64 (0.51--0.78), so within a phase the images gave about as much
evidence as the timeline.

% Sources: UCSF_AUDIT.md sections 3--6, EXPLORATORY intervals table and Overall verdict; NUMBERS.md 5. Council ruling 2.
At UCSF the progression calls rest largely on enhancement. Enhancing tumour volume alone, the simplest baseline,
gave an AUC of 0.88 (0.79--0.95), above the locked image models; UCSF rated enhancement as increased at 27 of the 29
progression scans, and progression was dated a median of 7 days after the evaluated scan (Figure~\ref{fig:3}B). Time
since diagnosis, which separates the classes at MU, gave 0.54 at UCSF. Among the 37 scans rated as showing increased
enhancement, the single-scan image model reached 0.65 (0.44--0.84), so beyond that rating the measurements added
little. At UCSF the AUCs therefore largely measure agreement with a progression call made from enhancement at the
same scan, which is what PRISM's image pathway measures. The combined score's higher AUC at UCSF
(Figure~\ref{fig:2}) rests partly on the interval between the two scans, which was shorter before progression.

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (EXPLORATORY pure imaging; I as run); NUMBERS.md 5 ladder and mismatch 8.
Adding inputs to the image model raised its AUC on MU, and comparing within phases of care shows what each addition
contributed (Figure~\ref{fig:3}C). Measurements of the current scan alone scored lowest, and adding change from earlier
scans gave the image pathway of Section~\ref{sec:3.1}. Adding the interval since the previous scan, a flag for
whether there was one and the smallest volume counted from the end of radiotherapy raised the AUC to 0.81, close to
the timeline; within a phase these three inputs added nothing ($-$0.01, $-$0.06 to $+$0.04), because they encode the
scan schedule. They form the imaging block of the planned model, so PRISM's image pathway leaves them out.

% Sources: DETECTION_DEEP.md; UCSF_RESULTS.md Part C (Q4); NUMBERS.md mismatch 6. Council ruling 6; resegmentation in RESERVE.md.
Deep networks fine-tuned to score UCSF scans directly learned above a permuted-label control (Table~\ref{tab:2}),
but their scores tracked enhancing volume (correlation about 0.8) and their AUC stayed below that of enhancing volume
alone; on MU they did not meet the planned gain over the timeline. Because the evidence behind a score differs by
cohort and by situation, PRISM also states how far each score can be trusted.

\subsection{The conformal sets meet their coverage threshold at all three centres}\label{sec:3.4}

% Sources: SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S1 and Specification; NUMBERS.md 6 and mismatch 13. Council ruling 4.
At the 80\% level, the conformal sets of the combined score covered between 0.78 and 0.87 of scans in the three
cohorts, meeting the threshold of 0.78 fixed in advance, a tolerance of 0.02 below the nominal level
(Figure~\ref{fig:4}A). At UCSF the point estimate sat at the threshold and its interval reached below it
(0.68--0.87). Most sets were informative, holding a single outcome for 62\% to 75\% of scans. Coverage fell short for
the image pathway alone at LUMIERE and the timeline alone at UCSF, and it was uneven across situations within each
cohort (Supplementary Section~\todo{S24}).

% Sources: SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S2, S3 and EXPLORATORY reduced design (3d20c41); NUMBERS.md mismatches 4 and 5.
On MU, the warning that the two pathways disagree picked out the less accurate scans. The pathways disagreed on 40\%
of MU scans; the combined score's AUC was 0.90 where they agreed and 0.59 where they disagreed (difference 0.31,
0.18--0.43), and setting the disagreeing scans aside gained more than removing the same number matched for phase of
care (Table~\ref{tab:2}; Figure~\ref{fig:4}B). At LUMIERE and UCSF disagreement did not pick out the less accurate
scans. A stricter rule chosen after these results, flagging a scan only when the pathways disagree and its set holds
both outcomes, is described in Supplementary Section~\todo{S24} (exploratory, not tested on new data).

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (Reading, lookup table); SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S4; NUMBERS.md mismatch 14.
The reliability card states, for each scan, how much measured evidence lies behind its situation, and for most scans
in our cohorts this is little (exploratory). Across the three cohorts only four situations held at least 10
progression and 10 non-progression scans, one at MU and three at LUMIERE; every situation at UCSF had few cases
(Figure~\ref{fig:4}C). At MU no situation in the first 12 weeks after radiotherapy held more than three progression
scans. At LUMIERE scans in tested situations had lower error than scans with few cases; at MU the order was reversed,
because the few-case scans there were mostly non-progression scans early after radiotherapy, which the models score
easily (Table~\ref{tab:2}). The clinician reads these statements in the report, which we evaluated next.

\subsection{The reports are faithful to the record}\label{sec:3.5}

% Sources: REPORT_V3_1.md (S5--S7, ladder); REPORT_V3.md (S5 failure); NUMBERS.md 7 and mismatches 9, 10. Council ruling 5.
Every evaluation report written by the local model passed the deterministic check against its record, so every
number, flag and confidence statement in it could be traced to the record (114, 162 and 130 reports at MU, LUMIERE
and UCSF; Figure~\ref{fig:5}). These counts come from a second evaluation, after one revision of the prompt, on
patients not used in development. The first evaluation had failed at LUMIERE (0 of 178) because one sentence quoted a
number that was in no cited field; we corrected the prompt and left the checker unchanged. The reports stated the
evidence tier for all 220 scans in situations with few or no cases, and followed a changed field in 28 of 30
perturbed records; both misses were changes in enhancing volume from a previous value of zero. The clinician
therefore reads the segmentation, both pathway scores and the confidence as they stand in the record. Of the seven
open-weight models we compared, the smallest that passed in every cohort had 27 billion parameters and ran on one GPU
(Supplementary Section~\todo{S25, second report evaluation, to be added}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:4}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 1); NUMBERS.md 3--6.
PRISM gives the clinician separate image and treatment-timeline evidence at each scan, transfers to two external
centres without refitting, and states how far its scores can be trusted there. At the development centre the
timeline provided most of the evidence, largely because of when patients were scanned, and within a phase of care
the images provided about as much. At UCSF, where few treatment dates are recorded, the image measurements provided
the score, and it matched the enhancement-based call made at the same scan. Training at UCSF raised the image pathway
further, so the locked results show transfer rather than the best a centre can reach. Because the pathways were kept
apart, these differences could be measured at each centre, and the confidence and the card bring them into each
report.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 2); SYSTEM_PLAN.md. Council ruling 7 (combined score definition).
Because its modules are separate, PRISM runs where data are incomplete. The image pathway needs only the scans and
their masks, which is what most public surveillance datasets hold; at LUMIERE and UCSF it ran without treatment
dates. Where dates exist, the timeline is added as a second pathway, and the combined score, one ridge model on both
sets of inputs, is shown beside both without changing the image model. A new cohort adds rows to the reliability card,
so the tiers change as evidence accumulates and no confidence model has to be refitted.

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (example card); NUMBERS.md 10 (scenario).
Consider, as an illustration, a scan eight weeks after radiotherapy in which the enhancing tumour has grown. At MU, 1
of the 9 scans in this situation on the card was progression (exploratory). The timeline pathway scores such a scan
low, because most scans at this point in treatment are not progression, and the image pathway scores it high, because
enhancement has grown. PRISM's report would show both scores, state that they disagree, give a conformal set that
holds both outcomes and record that the card has few cases for this situation. The clinician then sees that the
system has no measured basis for a call at this scan, in the weeks when pseudoprogression is most common, and decides
with information PRISM does not use, such as symptoms, steroid dose and the radiotherapy plan.

% Sources: SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md (1.3, 1.13, 2.1, 3.1, Finding); COMPARABLE_RESULTS.md; RESERVE.md (Christodoulou).
Each of PRISM's components has been described before, and we are not aware of a system that combines them.
Automatic segmentation of post-treatment glioma with measurement of change between scans is established
\citep{rudie2022}, conformal prediction has been applied to glioma segmentation \citep{elyassirad2025}, clinical and
imaging data have been fused to separate pseudoprogression from progression \citep{gomaa2025}, and model cards report
performance by subgroup \citep{mitchell2019}. The closest system we found combines treatment variables extracted by a
language model with tumour volumes to assign a structured follow-up category at one centre, without an uncertainty
attached to the output \citep{jabal2026}. On MU, a radiomics model reached an AUC of 0.80 with a label assigned at the
patient level and one held-out set \citep{christodoulou2026}, so its result and ours answer different questions. On
LUMIERE, a classifier of four response categories reached a mean AUC of 0.81 \todo{cite Tikhonov 2025,
arXiv:2509.06511; full reference not yet verified}, and \citet{chen2026} predicted recurrence risk from preoperative
and postoperative MRI across centres; both address other labels.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 5); NUMBERS.md 10; UCSF_AUDIT.md (caveat sentence); REPORT_V3_1.md Limits.
This study has limits. PRISM was developed at one centre, with 126 grade 4 patients, and the two external tests are
its only evidence of transfer. Progression is the progression documented by each centre, without expert re-reading of
the scans; at UCSF it agrees closely with an enhancement call at the same scan, and a quarter of the non-progression
scans there show no enhancement. Subgroups are small; UCSF contributes 29 progression scans, and its intervals are
0.15 to 0.25 wide. The coverage threshold was met on point estimates, and at UCSF the interval reached below it; the
disagreement warning picked out less accurate scans only at MU. The three-step image comparison, the agreement rule,
the card and the stricter flag rule are exploratory and need testing on new data. The report checker is lexical, so
it can confirm that every number and statement is in the record but cannot judge how a clinician reads the report,
and no clinician has yet read the reports. PRISM forecast progression within 120 days less well than it detected it
at the scan, so we present it as a detection system and report the forecast as a secondary task.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 6); RELATED_WORK.md section 3 (Burdenko, UCSD-PTGBM).
The next step is to pool the rare situations across cohorts and train on them. Early progression after radiotherapy
and growing enhancement without progression are rare at any one centre. The Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM datasets add
radiotherapy detail and advanced sequences, and each new cohort adds cases to the card's thin situations, which is
where models fine-tuned on these long-tail cases could be tested. A reader study in which clinicians use PRISM's
reports at the scan would show whether separate evidence and a stated confidence change their decisions.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim).
PRISM shows that a surveillance system can keep its kinds of evidence apart, transfer them to other centres without
refitting and state how far each has been tested there. In our three cohorts the images and the treatment timeline
each contributed, in different proportions at each centre, and PRISM shows the clinician both scores and the evidence
behind them at every scan.
