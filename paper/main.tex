\begin{abstract}
Surveillance MRI after treatment for glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas must be interpreted with prior imaging
and the treatment course. A progression score alone does not show the clinician the evidence that produced it or what
it leaves out. We built PRISM, a modular system that combines image-derived measurements, treatment context, separate
image and timeline pathways, uncertainty and provenance in a locally generated, checked report. On MU-Glioma-Post,
the timeline and image pathways reached AUCs of 0.82 and 0.71. Applied unchanged, the combined score reached 0.77 on
LUMIERE; at UCSF-ALPTDG, single-scan image measurements reached 0.80 and the image pathway 0.73. The evidence behind
the scores differed by cohort: treatment timing at MU-Glioma-Post and enhancing volume at UCSF-ALPTDG. In a second,
cross-cohort report evaluation, 406 of 406 reports passed a lexical check against their structured records. PRISM is
designed to make evidence inspectable, not to replace clinical judgement; its effect on clinical decisions needs a
reader study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Introduction 1); refs taal2008, brandes2008.
Glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas are followed with repeated MRI after surgery and chemoradiotherapy, and
each scan is read against earlier scans and the course of treatment. The call is hardest soon after radiotherapy,
when treatment can increase contrast enhancement without tumour growth. This pseudoprogression is most common in the
first weeks after chemoradiotherapy, and more so when the promoter of the DNA-repair gene MGMT is methylated
\citep{taal2008,brandes2008}.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine C1--C5); refs an2021, varoquaux2018, shi2025.
A model's probability alone does not show the treatment context, the
measurements behind it, its uncertainty or what it left out. Surveillance data make these gaps hard to close, because
public datasets often hold images but few treatment dates, centres document progression differently, and the hardest
situations are rare. Only 3 of 20 studies in a recent review of automated response assessment tested
their models externally \citep{shi2025}.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (question, claim); NUMBERS.md sections 3--7.
We built PRISM to write, inside the hospital, a report for each post-treatment scan; the report shows the image
evidence and the treatment-timeline evidence separately and states how far each has been tested. We developed it on
MU-Glioma-Post (MU) and applied its models unchanged to LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG (UCSF). The combined and image models ranked scans at both external centres, but what drove a score differed by cohort, mostly the position in treatment at MU and
enhancement at UCSF.

The timeline pathway scores a scan from clinical data and the patient's position in treatment, both taken as of the
scan day. Where a cohort lacks treatment dates, a reduced timeline keeps the inputs it has
(Table~\ref{tab:models}), with the flag for the first weeks after radiotherapy taken from the rater's note at LUMIERE
and approximated at UCSF. The image pathway scores the scan from 27 measurements of the segmented
tumour and its change (Table~\ref{tab:models}; \todo{Supplementary section: image pathway inputs and their
harmonisation at LUMIERE and UCSF}) and uses no dates or intervals. The measurements come from the masks released with each cohort
\citep{mahmoud2025,suter2022,fields2024}.

% Sources: SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 2; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md Specification; prism/analyses/system.py (derive: medians over
% each cohort's evaluation scans); NUMBERS.md 12A (MU-fixed medians, EXPLORATORY); RELIABILITY_CARD.md;
% refs angelopoulos2023, mitchell2019.
The confidence has three parts. A patient-level split conformal set at the 80\% level \citep{angelopoulos2023}
holds one outcome when the score is clear and both when it is not. It comes from the combined model refitted on 70\%
of the MU patients and calibrated on the other 30\% (within each training fold on MU) and is applied unchanged
elsewhere, which assumes that external scans are exchangeable with MU's (\todo{Supplementary section: system
results}). The two pathways agree when both scores lie on the same side of their medians over the cohort's evaluation
scans; cohort medians need a batch of scans, so we also tested medians fixed on MU, which work for a single scan.
The reliability card\expl{} \citep{mitchell2019} describes the scan's situation, its phase of care crossed with the
change in enhancing volume, from that cohort's held-out results. Because those results include the scan itself, the
card describes it slightly optimistically. The card gives AUCs for a situation with at least 10 scans of each outcome
(tested), counts only for one with fewer (few cases), and marks one with none as not tested.

% Sources: REPORT_V3.md (Design, Checker v3); REPORT_V3_1.md; Supplementary S19.
A local language model (Qwen3.8-27B, temperature 0) writes the report from the record alone, so patient data do not
leave the hospital. A
deterministic checker rejects any number, flag or confidence statement that is not in the record, any missing
required content, and any wording that offers a diagnosis, advice or a probability (Supplementary Section~S19).

\subsection{Cohorts and the task}\label{sec:2.2}

% Sources: TABLE1_COHORT.md; UCSF_DATA.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, Cohort, External test); INTENDED_USE.md.
Grade 4 glioma is the primary population in all three cohorts (Table~\ref{tab:1}); we fitted each model once on MU
and applied it unchanged, or locked, to the others. The task is to decide whether each scan is the one at which the cohort documented progression. On MU a positive is the first scan on or after the
documented progression date, and a negative is a scan followed by at least 120 days without progression. At LUMIERE
a positive is the first scan rated as progressive disease, and a negative is an earlier scan rated stable or
responding. UCSF holds one pair of consecutive scans per patient, and we evaluated the second: a positive is a second scan whose
progression date lies within 30 days of it and closer to it than to the first scan, and a negative is followed by at least 120
days without progression. On MU the intended use is scans from the start of radiotherapy, divided into three phases
of care: during radiotherapy, early after it (up to 12 weeks after its end) and later; MU results are for the intended
use unless stated. Predicting progression within 120 days of a scan is a secondary task.

\subsection{Evaluation}\label{sec:2.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan); SPLIT_VARIANCE.md; Supplementary S8 and Figure S2; UCSF_PLAN.md (0e7c9fe);
% SYSTEM_PLAN.md (e7cba80); REPORT_V3_1_PLAN.md (4f7e7b9); UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A, B, C; NUMBERS.md status column.
The locked models were fitted on all 181 grade 4 detection scans of MU, from every phase of care, and the external
tests followed plans committed before any score was compared with an external label. One deviation, in the MU scans used for fitting, changed the LUMIERE AUC by less than 0.01 (\todo{Supplementary
section: deviations from the plans}). On MU we evaluated every model by five
repeats of five-fold cross-validation grouped by patient and report the mean pooled held-out AUC with a 95\%
confidence interval (CI) from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap draws; external AUCs carry a 95\% CI from the same patient-level bootstrap. The MU intervals resample held-out predictions
and do not include the variance from refitting. No patient
contributed to more than one of fitting, calibration and testing. We used repeated splits because single splits are
unstable in small cohorts \citep{an2021,varoquaux2018}. An earlier radiomics model on MU, for example, reached 0.80 on
one test split but averaged 0.72 when re-selected and re-tested over 500 splits of the same design (Supplementary
Section~S8 and Figure~S2). As a reference, we also fitted each UCSF model on UCSF by cross-validation. To test whether deep networks see more
than the measurements, we fine-tuned segmentation networks to score scans directly and compared them with the same
networks trained on permuted labels

\section{Results}\label{sec:3}

\subsection{The timeline led at MU, and the combined and image models transferred without refitting}\label{sec:3.1}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Primary table; EXPLORATORY pure imaging); INTENDED_USE.md; NUMBERS.md 3.
In the intended use on MU (146 scans), the timeline pathway detected the progression scan with an AUC of 0.82 (95\%
CI 0.75--0.90) and the image pathway with 0.71 (0.63--0.78)\expl; clinical data alone scored 0.53 (0.45--0.61;
Figure~\ref{fig:2}). The combined score also reached 0.82 (0.74--0.89)\expl, with no detectable gain over the timeline
($-$0.001, $-$0.05 to $+$0.05). A planned model that added image measurements and three schedule inputs to the timeline also fell short of its
target gain of 0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:2}). Most of the timeline's gain lay between phases of care\expl{}
(Section~\ref{sec:3.3}).  Forecasting progression within 120 days was harder, at 0.60
(0.52--0.69) in the intended use (Supplementary Section~S23).

\subsection{The scores followed the treatment schedule at MU and enhancement at UCSF}\label{sec:3.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Cohort by phase; within phase; EXPLORATORY images + clinical); STORY.md gap 3.
At MU the scores depended mainly on where the patient was in treatment, and at UCSF on the volume of enhancing
tissue\expl{} (Figure~\ref{fig:3}). At MU most progression scans came more than 12 weeks after radiotherapy and most
non-progression scans within those 12 weeks (Table~\ref{tab:1}), so the schedule alone could separate most of them. A
within-phase AUC pools only the pairs of a progression and a non-progression scan from the same phase of care; most
such pairs come from the later phase (56 progression and 32 non-progression scans). Adding the timeline to clinical data and images\expl{} changed the AUC by $+$0.11 (0.04--0.20) across phases of care
and by $-$0.02 ($-$0.13 to $+$0.11) within them (Figure~\ref{fig:3}A). Within a phase, the image pathway scored 0.68 (0.58--0.77) and the timeline 0.64
(0.51--0.78), with no difference detectable.

On MU the image model gained most from inputs that carry timing\expl. Adding change from earlier scans to the
current scan's measurements, which gives the image pathway, raised the AUC by 0.05, with an interval that included
zero ($-$0.003 to $+$0.106). Adding the planned model's three schedule inputs (Table~\ref{tab:models}) raised it by a
further 0.10 (0.05--0.15) across phases of care but not within them ($-$0.01, $-$0.06 to $+$0.04)
(Figure~\ref{fig:3}C).

% Sources: UCSF_AUDIT.md sections 3--6 and Overall verdict; UCSF_RESULTS.md Part C (Q4); NUMBERS.md 5.
At UCSF the progression labels coincided with a rated increase in enhancement\expl, so a model could score well there
by tracking it: UCSF rated enhancement as increased at 27 of the 29 progression scans, and progression was dated a
median of 7 days after the scan (Figure~\ref{fig:3}B). Enhancing volume alone reached 0.88 (0.79--0.95) there, above the point estimates of the
locked image models, and far above its AUC at MU and LUMIERE (Figure~\ref{fig:2}). Among the 37 scans rated as
increased, the single-scan model reached 0.65 (0.44--0.84), so this sample cannot show whether the measurements add
to that rating. Deep networks fine-tuned on UCSF scans beat the permuted-label control (Table~\ref{tab:2}), and their
scores tracked enhancing volume\expl{} (\todo{Supplementary section: deep networks at MU and UCSF}).

\subsection{Coverage meets its target on point estimates; disagreement marks scans the combined score ranks less well at MU}\label{sec:3.4}

% Sources: SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S1; NUMBERS.md 6 and 12B.
The 80\% conformal sets of the combined score met the planned coverage threshold of 78\% on point estimates in all
three cohorts, covering 78\% to 87\% of scans, and held a single outcome for 62\% to 75\% of scans
(Figure~\ref{fig:4}A); the intervals at MU (76\% to 88\%) and UCSF (68\% to 87\%) reached below the threshold, and the image
set at LUMIERE and the timeline set at UCSF fell below it. Coverage differed by outcome
at the external centres\expl. LUMIERE covered 77\% (67\% to 87\%) of progression scans and 94\% of non-progression
scans, and UCSF 97\% of progression and 65\% (51\% to 79\%) of non-progression scans. Calibrating the sets separately
for each outcome on MU did not fix this, and non-progression coverage fell to 73\% (63\% to 82\%) even on MU ( \todo{Supplementary section: system results}).

\subsection{After one revision, every report passed an automatic check against its record}\label{sec:3.5}

% Sources: REPORT_V3_1.md (S5--S7); REPORT_V3.md (S5 failure); NUMBERS.md 7.
Every report written by Qwen3.8-27B passed the lexical check against its record, 114 at MU, 162 at LUMIERE and 130
at UCSF; of six other local models given the same prompt, which had been revised for Qwen3.8-27B, only Gemma-4 31B
also did (Figure~\ref{fig:5}B). All 220 reports in few-case or untested situations said so
(71 at MU, 19 at LUMIERE and 130 at UCSF). When one confidence or measurement field was changed in 30 records from the
three cohorts, 28 reports followed the change, against a planned minimum of 27 (Table~\ref{tab:2}). These results
followed one revision of the prompt\expl. Before it, every LUMIERE report failed because a fixed sentence quoted a
number held in no field, so we corrected the prompt, left the checker unchanged and evaluated again on patients not
used for the revision (\todo{Supplementary section: report revision}). The check confirms that a report matches its
record; it cannot judge emphasis, what a clinician would infer, or whether the segmentation is right.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:4}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 1); NUMBERS.md 3--6.
PRISM's models ranked scans at two other centres without refitting, but the evidence behind a score depended on how
each cohort was scanned and labelled: mostly the treatment schedule at MU and, at UCSF, the enhancement on which
progression was called\expl. An AUC cannot show which evidence a model used; the audits of Section~\ref{sec:3.3} suggested it, and the separate
pathways put it in front of the clinician. The risk scale did not transfer to UCSF, so a new centre would recalibrate the score and set any threshold
on local scans.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 2); SYSTEM_PLAN.md.
Because its modules are separate, PRISM runs on what a cohort holds, given scans and masks. The image pathway needs
only those, which most public datasets have, and a single-scan version serves where there is no earlier scan. The timeline
is added where dates exist. The reliability card is a lookup table, so a new cohort adds rows to it and a situation's
tier can change as evidence accumulates.

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (example card); NUMBERS.md 10 (scenario).
Consider a scan eight weeks after radiotherapy in which the enhancing tumour has grown (Figure~\ref{fig:5}A).  The timeline
pathway scores such a scan low, because most scans at this point in treatment are not progression, and the image
pathway scores it high, because enhancement has grown. The report shows both, states that they disagree and that the conformal set holds both outcomes, and records from the card that the situation has few cases (at MU, 1 of 9 such scans was progression\expl). PRISM
therefore has too few tested cases to support a call here, in the weeks when pseudoprogression is most common, and the
clinician decides with information it does not use, such as symptoms, steroid dose and the radiotherapy plan.

% Sources: SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md; COMPARABLE_RESULTS.md; RESERVE.md (Christodoulou).
Each component has been described before: longitudinal segmentation with measurement of change
\citep{rudie2022}, conformal prediction for glioma segmentation \citep{elyassirad2025}, fusion of clinical and imaging
data to separate pseudoprogression from progression \citep{gomaa2025} and model cards \citep{mitchell2019}. The closest system assigns a structured follow-up category from treatment variables
extracted by a language model and tumour volumes, at one centre and without an uncertainty \citep{jabal2026}. On these
cohorts, earlier work predicted a patient-level label on MU \citep{christodoulou2026} and response categories on
LUMIERE (\todo{cite Tikhonov 2025, arXiv:2509.06511; full reference not yet verified}); separately, \citet{chen2026}
predicted recurrence risk from preoperative and postoperative MRI across centres. None detects progression at the
scan, and we are not aware of a system that combines these components with each kind of evidence kept apart and the
system's own test results attached.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 5); NUMBERS.md 10; UCSF_AUDIT.md; REPORT_V3_1.md Limits.
PRISM was developed at one centre, and the two external tests are its only evidence that the ranking transfers. The
label is progression as each centre documented it; no scan was re-read, and at UCSF most progression scans were also
rated as showing increased enhancement. Of the confidence measures, only average coverage met its planned threshold at all three centres, and only on point
estimates; with cohort medians, disagreement\expl{} marked less well ranked scans at MU alone, and that rule needs a batch of
a cohort's scans; the version a single scan can use (MU medians) is exploratory. No UCSF scan fell in a tested
situation. Subgroups are small, the analyses marked
\textdagger{} need testing on new data, and no clinician has yet read the reports. PRISM forecast progression within 120 days less well than it
detected progression at the scan (AUC 0.60, 0.52--0.69, in the intended use).

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 6); RELATED_WORK.md section 3.
The next steps are to pool the rare situations (early progression after radiotherapy, and growing enhancement without
progression) across cohorts, including the Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM datasets, and test models fine-tuned on them there;
and a reader study to show whether separate evidence and a stated confidence change clinicians' decisions.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim).
PRISM keeps image and treatment-timeline evidence apart and shows the clinician both scores at every scan with how
far each has been tested there. Its models ranked scans at two other centres without refitting, while the scores followed the treatment schedule at
MU and enhancement at UCSF; showing the two kinds of evidence apart puts this in front of the clinician.
