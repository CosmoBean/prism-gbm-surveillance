\begin{abstract}
After treatment for aggressive glioma, each follow up MRI is read against the treatment course, yet most
published models score the image alone. PRISM is a local system intended for clinical decision support that scores
each scan with separate imaging, clinical-history and combined models and has a local language model write a report
checked against the record. On identical folds of the public MU-Glioma-Post cohort, the clinical-history and
imaging models reached AUCs of 0.82 and 0.71, against 0.58 for the published radiomics pipeline run with its authors'
code. Fitted
on MU and applied unchanged, the combined model reached 0.77 at LUMIERE and 0.86 at UCSF-ALPTDG, where enhancing
volume alone reached 0.88. A baseline of timing and enhancing volume reached 0.80 on MU, and no PRISM score
clearly beat it. After a prompt revision, all 406 reports passed the planned checks (exploratory). Whether PRISM
changes decisions awaits a reader study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

% Sources: refs wen2023, taal2008.
After surgery and chemoradiotherapy for glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas, each follow up MRI is read against
earlier scans and the course of treatment. Enhancement can grow because the tumour grows or because of treatment, and
the second is frequent in the first 12 weeks after radiotherapy \citep{wen2023,taal2008}. The time of a scan is
therefore part of the evidence a clinician uses to read it.

% Sources: refs booth2022, shi2025, gomaa2025, badgeley2019, beaulieujones2021, christodoulou2026; COMPARABLE_RESULTS.md.
Most models proposed for this setting score the image alone and are tested on a single split of one cohort. In a
meta-analysis of machine learning studies of treatment response, a third had an external test set \citep{booth2022},
and a systematic review of automated response assessment found clinical information in 3 of 20 tools and external test
data in 3 of 20, and judged none ready for the clinic \citep{shi2025}. In one study that compared them on an external test set, clinical inputs matched images, and time since the end of
treatment was the strongest input \citep{gomaa2025}. A score can also
rank scans well by recognising when they were taken, because follow up schedules tie the time of a scan to its label
\citep{badgeley2019}, so what a model adds beyond the evidence a reader already holds is the quantity to measure
\citep{beaulieujones2021}. On the public MU-Glioma-Post cohort, the one published progression model reported an AUC
of 0.80 from radiomics on a single held out set of 30 patients \citep{christodoulou2026}.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (system-first 2026-11-15); NUMBERS.md 3--7; METHODS_RESULTS.md E4.
PRISM is a system intended for clinical decision support that makes progression scores inspectable. For each follow up scan it
builds one record that holds tumour volumes measured from segmentation masks, the treatment course, three separately
fitted scores and the source of every value, and a local language model writes a report from that record alone. We
developed PRISM on MU-Glioma-Post, fitted its models once, and applied them unchanged in the LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG
cohorts. We report four results.
\begin{enumerate}
\item On identical folds and labels, a model of the treatment course, and also an imaging model of 27 tumour
measurements, ranked scans better than the published radiomics pipeline run with its authors' code
(Section~\ref{sec:4.1}). Both margins held with masks from a second segmenter, although the imaging model's own AUC
depends on the masks.
\item The locked models kept ranking scans at two external centres (Section~\ref{sec:4.2}).
\item Most of that ranking rests on the time after radiotherapy and the enhancing tumour, which a reader also has,
although the baseline measures the volume a reader estimates by eye. We show this with a reader baseline, a model built from that evidence alone, and with an exact split of the AUC into pairs of
scans within and across time windows \citep{kallus2019}, and PRISM keeps the two kinds of evidence as separate scores
at every scan (Section~\ref{sec:4.3}).
\item The confidence statements describe the evidence behind a score but do not single out scans. Every report
passed the planned checks, which catch numbers placed under the wrong region or score but miss direction errors
phrased outside a fixed word list (Sections~\ref{sec:4.4} and~\ref{sec:4.5}).
\end{enumerate}
We have not tested whether PRISM changes a clinician's reading; a reader study is designed.

\section{PRISM}\label{sec:2}

% Sources: SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 1.
PRISM turns one follow up scan and the patient's clinical record into a structured record and a report in five stages
(Figure~\ref{fig:1}). It measures the tumour, scores the scan with three models, attaches the confidence statements,
writes every value into one record with its source, and has a local language model write the report, which is then
checked against the record. Table~\ref{tab:models} lists every score in the paper.

\subsection{Inputs and measurements}\label{sec:2.1}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, B1); UCSF_IMAGING.md; NUMBERS.md 2.1; refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
PRISM takes three inputs for a scan. The first is the segmentation of the current and earlier scans, which gives the
volumes of enhancing tumour, FLAIR hyperintensity, necrosis and the resection cavity. We used the images and masks as
released with each cohort \citep{mahmoud2025,suter2022,fields2024}, so every volume can be traced to a public file.
From the masks and images PRISM computes 27 measurements. Nineteen describe the current scan (the log volumes of six
compartments and 13 measures of shape and of T1 contrast and FLAIR intensity relative to normal appearing brain,
Supplementary Table~S47), and eight describe change since the previous scan and since the patient's smallest volume.
The second input is the treatment course as of the scan day (days since diagnosis and surgery, and the start, end and
dose of radiotherapy and chemotherapy). From it PRISM places the scan in a time window after radiotherapy (before its
end, within 12 weeks after its end, later, or unknown when the dates are missing). The third input is the patient's
age, sex, and IDH mutation and MGMT promoter methylation status. Every input is taken as of the scan day, except that
IDH and MGMT status were used at 8 grade 4 scans taken up to 58 days before the diagnosis date (Supplementary
Section~S4).

\subsection{Three models}\label{sec:2.2}

% Sources: prism/models/formulations.py; prism/experiments/c3_radiomics.py (BASE_PRE); NUMBERS.md 2.1.
PRISM scores each scan with three models and shows all three. The clinical-history model uses the treatment course
and the patient characteristics, the imaging model uses the 27 measurements and no dates, and the combined model uses
both. Keeping the first two apart is what lets the record show which kind of evidence a score follows. Each is a
logistic regression with an L2 penalty, chosen because it is simple and its inputs are few, and deep networks tuned on
MU did not improve on it (Table~\ref{tab:2}). The inputs are standardised, and the penalty is chosen from six values of
the inverse strength $C$ (0.01 to 3) by threefold cross validation grouped by patient, minimising the log loss. Where a
cohort lacks treatment dates, PRISM uses a reduced clinical-history model with the inputs that cohort has. LUMIERE's
masks have three compartments, so for LUMIERE we fitted an imaging model on MU with only those three volumes and their
change. Because the imaging model needs only scans and masks, PRISM gives an imaging score in a cohort with no
treatment dates.

\subsection{Record and report}\label{sec:2.3}

% Sources: prism/report/facts_v3_1.py; prism/report/prompts_v3_1.py; REPORT_V3_1.md; Supplementary S19, S24.
Every value PRISM computes for a scan enters one structured record under a fixed key, with its source. A local language
model (Qwen3.8-27B, revision 1d4bf0f2, greedy decoding) writes the report from that record alone, so the record never leaves the computer
that runs PRISM. Decoding is constrained to a JSON schema in which every sentence carries its section and the keys of
the record fields it uses, and the model sees no images and does no arithmetic. The record also carries two
descriptive statements. One says whether the clinical-history and imaging scores fall on the same side of their cohort
medians. The other is a reliability card, after model cards \citep{mitchell2019}, that gives for the scan's situation
(time window and state of the enhancing tumour) how many scans with and without progression the models were tested
on and, with at least 10 of each (the tested tier), their AUCs, and otherwise only the counts (few cases). The card is
built without any scan of the patient it describes. Because the released volumes are noisy (Section~\ref{sec:3.3}),
the report calls them indicative. The report follows the content of the BT-RADS follow up template
\citep{weinberg2018} without its category or advice, which PRISM does not give (Supplementary Section~S24).

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:3}

\subsection{Cohorts and the task}\label{sec:3.1}

% Sources: TABLE1_COHORT.md; UCSF_DATA.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; DETECTION_TASK.md; INTENDED_USE.md; NUMBERS.md 2.2.
Grade 4 glioma is the primary population in all three cohorts (Table~\ref{tab:1}). The task is to tell the scan at
which a cohort documented progression from scans followed by at least 120 days without it. This drops scans with
shorter follow up, among them scans shortly before progression, so on MU we also evaluated every score with all
scans before progression as negatives, as the detection plan specified. Each cohort defines
progression in its own way (Table~\ref{tab:eval}), so AUCs are compared between models within a cohort. The design
ties time to the label. On MU most progression scans were taken later than 12 weeks after radiotherapy and most scans
without progression within 12 weeks (Table~\ref{tab:1}), so the time window alone separates many pairs. On MU the
intended use covers scans from the start of radiotherapy onwards, and MU results refer to it: 146 scans of 80
patients, out of 181 detection scans of 92 patients among the cohort's 126 grade 4 patients.

\subsection{How each cohort was evaluated}\label{sec:3.2}

Each cohort played one role (Table~\ref{tab:eval}). On MU we evaluated every model by five repeats of fivefold cross
validation grouped by patient and report the mean pooled held out AUC, because single splits are unstable in small
cohorts \citep{varoquaux2018}. We then fitted each model once on all 181 grade 4 detection scans of MU, including the
35 taken before radiotherapy, locked it, and applied it unchanged to LUMIERE and UCSF under plans committed before any

further plans written before they were coded. UCSF-ALPTDG was no longer on our hardware when they ran, so they were

\subsection{What the scores rest on}\label{sec:3.3}

% Sources: CHRISTODOULOU_FAITHFUL_PLAN.md (b1ce85b, deviations e636073); CHRISTODOULOU_REPLICATION.md (faithful run);
% ref christodoulou2026.
\paragraph{The published approach.} We ran the published MU radiomics pipeline \citep{christodoulou2026} with the
authors' public code and settings, on the same patients, masks, label and folds as PRISM. It extracts 4,892 radiomic
features from the original, Laplacian of Gaussian and wavelet images of the four sequences of the current scan, keeps
256 of them within each training set by the authors' ranking, and fits a class weighted gradient boosted model tuned
over 400 Bayesian search trials with patient grouped inner folds, followed by Platt calibration. The authors' ranking
adds permutation importance only when an inner fold holds at least 30 scans, so with our 13 to 16 scans it ranked features
by coefficient size alone; we left the code unchanged. The published study gave every scan of a patient who ever
progressed the label of progression. We kept PRISM's label, one scan per documented progression, so the comparison is
of approaches on one task rather than a replication of the published estimate. The plan, committed before the run,
stated that the comparison with PRISM's imaging model would be withdrawn if the pipeline did not rank below it.

% Sources: MASK_SENSITIVITY_PLAN.md (b1ce85b, deviations c235649); METHODS_RESULTS.md E5; ref hatamizadeh2022.
\paragraph{Mask sensitivity.} The masks released with MU are noisy: between scans at most 30 days apart the enhancing
volume changes by a median factor of 2.6. To test whether the MU results depend on them, we replaced every MU mask
with the label map of one public segmenter, a Swin UNETR trained on BraTS 2021 \citep{hatamizadeh2022}, kept the
resection cavity from the released mask, recomputed the 27 measurements, and refitted the imaging and combined models
and the reader baseline in the same folds. The plan called the results robust if every paired difference had a CI
including zero and the reading against the reader baseline did not change.

% Sources: CLINICIAN_BASELINE_PLAN.md (e8014a8); METHODS_RESULTS.md E4; ref beaulieujones2021.
\paragraph{The reader baseline.} To measure what a score adds to evidence available to the reader
\citep{beaulieujones2021}, we fitted a ridge logistic regression on the time window after radiotherapy, log enhancing
volume and its change since the previous scan, with the same pipeline, folds and locking as PRISM's models. At LUMIERE the window is
the rater's flag. The plan fixed the reading in advance. A gain whose 95\% CI lies above zero counts as a gain, and
one whose CI includes zero is reported as no clear gain.

% Sources: METHODS_PLAN.md M1; prism/analyses/methods.py; refs kallus2019, janes2008, janes2009.
\paragraph{Splitting the AUC by time window.} Let $P$ and $N$ be the scans with and without progression, $s$ a score,
and $c$ the time window of each scan. The AUC is the share of pairs ranked correctly,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC} = \frac{1}{|P|\,|N|} \sum_{i \in P} \sum_{j \in N} K(s_i, s_j), \qquad
K(a, b) = \mathbb{1}[a > b] + \tfrac12\, \mathbb{1}[a = b].
\end{equation}
The pairs split into $W = \{(i, j) : c_i = c_j\}$, two scans from the same window, and its complement $B$. With
$A_W$ and $A_B$ the same average over $W$ and over $B$, and $w = |W| / (|P|\,|N|)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC} = w\, A_W + (1 - w)\, A_B
\end{equation}
holds exactly \citep{kallus2019}. $A_W$ is the ranking with the window held fixed, a pair weighted analogue of the
covariate adjusted AUC \citep{janes2008,janes2009}. A score with $A_B$ above $A_W$ ranks pairs across windows better
than pairs within one, which can come from the window or from real differences between the tumours of different
windows. To show how much of $A_B$ the window alone could supply, we also score every scan by the progression rate of
its window in the training folds (the window only score). At MU the window is from the treatment dates (during
radiotherapy, within 12 weeks after its end, and later). At LUMIERE it is the rater's flag for scans within three
months of radiotherapy, and the window only score takes MU's rate for its first 12 weeks and for the other windows.
The identity was checked on every bootstrap sample.

\subsection{Confidence statements}\label{sec:3.4}

% Sources: prism/audit/conformal.py; METHODS_PLAN.md M2; refs angelopoulos2023, vovk2022book, romano2020,
% barber2021limits, johansson2015, ding2023, mehrtens2025.
A conformal set names the outcomes a score cannot rule out \citep{angelopoulos2023}. Write $\hat p(1) = \hat p$ for a
scan's score and $\hat p(0) = 1 - \hat p$. For each calibration scan $i$ with outcome $y_i$ the nonconformity score is
$s_i = 1 - \hat p_i(y_i)$. Because a patient contributes several scans, each calibration scan is weighted by one over
the number of that patient's calibration scans. With $\alpha = 0.2$ and $n$ calibration patients, the threshold $\hat
q$ is the smallest $s_i$ whose cumulative weight reaches $(1 - \alpha)(n + 1)$, and a new scan receives
\begin{equation}
C(\hat p) = \{\, y \in \{0, 1\} : 1 - \hat p(y) \le \hat q \,\}.
\end{equation}
As planned, the combined model was refitted on 70\% of the training patients and calibrated on the other 30\%, inside
each training fold on MU and once on all MU training patients for the external cohorts. If a new patient is
exchangeable with the calibration patients, about 80\% of their sets contain the true outcome, on average over scans
but not necessarily within a subgroup \citep{romano2020,barber2021limits}. The weighting by patient makes even the
average coverage approximate.

% Sources: METHODS_PLAN.md M2; Supplementary Section S26.
Group-conditional (Mondrian) sets \citep{vovk2022book} compute $\hat q$ separately within each group. Our groups are
the situations of the reliability card, the time window crossed with the state of the enhancing tumour (absent below
0.1\,cc, without comparison when there is no previous scan, increased when it grew by at least 1\,cc and 40\%, or not
increased). Most groups are small, so each scan is calibrated at the finest of three levels that holds at least 10
calibration patients, its group, then its enhancement state over all windows, then all calibration scans, a fixed
fallback of the kind used for small Mondrian groups \citep{johansson2015,ding2023}. The level used is written into
the record. Coverage carries over to an external cohort only if its scans are exchangeable with MU's
\citep{mehrtens2025}. Because group calibration can raise coverage in a group simply by making sets larger, we
compared it after the planned run with marginal sets at the $\alpha$ that gives the same share of sets holding both
outcomes (exploratory).

\subsection{Checking the reports}\label{sec:3.5}

% Sources: prism/report/checker.py; prism/report/verifier.py (v1.1, blob d4b93db5); METHODS_PLAN.md M3; refs zeba2026,
% wiseman2017, kryscinski2020, gabriel2021, rao2025rexerr.
The report is a list of claims, each a sentence with the record fields it cites. The planned checker of PRISM, frozen
before any evaluation report was generated, rejects a report that cannot be parsed, cites a field that does not exist,
states a number found in no cited field, omits a raised flag, misstates the conformal set or the agreement, omits the
card's tier where evidence is thin, leaves out a score, quotes a performance figure absent from the card, or offers a
diagnosis, a response category, advice or certainty words. A rejected report is not repaired. Such rule based checks
compare generated text with a source record \citep{zeba2026}, as extractive evaluations of data to text systems do with
a learned extractor \citep{wiseman2017}. Because the checker accepts a number that equals a number in any cited field,
it accepts a sentence that gives the FLAIR volume as the enhancing volume while citing both fields, or that writes
``larger'' where the record says ``smaller''.

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md M3; METHODS_PLAN.md M3.
A verifier adds two rules that bind a claim to what it refers to. A sentence is split into clauses at commas,
semicolons, colons and the word ``and'', and each number and direction word in a clause is assigned to the nearest
tumour region or score named in it. The first rule requires every such number to equal, up to its written rounding, a
cited value of that region or score. The second requires every direction word on a fixed list (larger, increased,
grew, rose, higher, and their opposites) to agree with the cited direction of its region. The rules as first written
read the region only from the names of cited fields and rejected every evaluation report, because most fields are
sentences such as ``enhancing tumour 3.5\,cc larger than at the previous scan''. We then let the rules read the region
from the text of a cited field as well, tested this change only on synthetic records and the 48 MU and LUMIERE reports
used to develop the prompt, froze it, and ran it again on the evaluation reports. To measure the verifier we planted one error
at a time in correct reports, with 24 kinds of error, as error injection is used to evaluate
factuality checks \citep{gabriel2021,rao2025rexerr}. Three binding errors were held out of rule writing. Because we
wrote both the rules and these errors, a separate language model agent that had not seen the rules then wrote further
errors in its own words, with correct paraphrases as controls (exploratory).

\section{Results}\label{sec:4}

\subsection{Models of the treatment course and of tumour measurements beat image-only radiomics}\label{sec:4.1}

% Sources: CHRISTODOULOU_REPLICATION.md (faithful run; first re-implementation: Reading, 100 splits); DETECTION_TASK.md;
% NUMBERS.md 3, 8; METHODS_RESULTS.md E5; CHRISTODOULOU_REPLICATION.md (faithful run on the BraTS21 masks).
On the same MU scans, masks, label and folds, the published radiomics pipeline run with its authors' code reached an
AUC of 0.58 (0.50--0.66), and PRISM's clinical-history model 0.82 (0.75--0.90), a difference of 0.24 (0.15--0.34).
The pipeline also ranked scans below PRISM's imaging model, which reached 0.71 (0.63--0.78) from 27 measurements, by
0.13 (0.02--0.23). Its tuning AUC within the training folds averaged 0.70 and did not carry to the held out folds. An
earlier re-implementation from the published description, with fewer features and 40 search trials, also reached
0.58, a plain logistic regression on its 256 features 0.64, and enhancing volume alone 0.62 (exploratory), so on
MU the selected radiomic features ranked scans less well than enhancing volume alone. Single held out
sets vary widely at this size. Over 100 random held out sets of 18 of our 92 grade 4 patients that re-implementation
ranged from 0.32 to 0.88 on the intended use scans (mean 0.63), so the published estimate, from one held out set and a different label, cannot be compared with ours.

The imaging model's AUC depends on the masks, but its margin over the radiomics pipeline does not. With every MU
mask taken from the BraTS 2021 segmenter, whose enhancing volumes changed less between close scans (median factor 2.2), the imaging model reached 0.63 (0.55--0.70), 0.08 (0.01--0.16) below its AUC on the released masks,
and the loss lay mostly within time windows. On the same masks the radiomics pipeline, run again with its authors'
code, fell to 0.53 and still ranked below the imaging model, by 0.10 (0.01--0.19), and below the clinical-history
model, by 0.30 (0.19--0.40). Within one time window, on either mask set, the margin over the imaging model had a CI
including zero. On these masks the combined model reached 0.80 and the reader baseline 0.78, each 0.02 below its
value on the released masks, with CIs including zero.

With all MU scans before progression as negatives, adding 89 scans, the imaging model fell to 0.66, by 0.054
(0.018--0.089), beyond the planned limit of 0.05, while the clinical-history model (0.81) and the combined
model (0.78) changed less. Part of the imaging model's AUC therefore comes from leaving out the scans shortly before
progression.

Clinical data alone, without dates, reached 0.53
(0.45--0.61), and neither a second segmentation of MU nor deep networks tuned on MU improved detection
(Table~\ref{tab:2}). The clinical-history model's lead over the images came from the treatment course, which
Section~\ref{sec:4.3} takes apart, while the imaging model's lead over the radiomics pipeline is a comparison of
image evidence alone.

\subsection{The locked models ranked scans at two external centres}\label{sec:4.2}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md; UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A--C; NUMBERS.md 3, 4, 12C, 15.
Fitted once on MU and applied unchanged, the combined and imaging models kept ranking scans at both external centres
(Figure~\ref{fig:2}). The reduced combined model, whose inputs every cohort records (at UCSF with an approximated radiotherapy window), reached 0.77 (0.68--0.86) at LUMIERE, and the imaging
model 0.65 (0.56--0.73, exploratory). At UCSF the imaging model reached 0.73 (0.60--0.84), images from one scan 0.80
(0.69--0.89) and the locked combined model 0.86 (0.76--0.93),
while the reduced clinical-history model, whose radiotherapy flag is approximated there, scored 0.56 (0.42--0.69). On
MU the combined model reached 0.82, no more than the clinical-history model, and its reduced form 0.80. The scores
rank scans at a new centre but are not risks there. On MU the calibration slopes were about 0.75, with CIs including
1, but at LUMIERE the imaging score under-predicted progression (calibration intercept 0.41, 0.15--0.70), and at
UCSF the combined model's slope was 3.3, so its predictions were too compressed. A centre would recalibrate the
scores on its own scans before reading them as probabilities.

What a score followed differed between the centres, and the labels partly explain why. LUMIERE's label is an expert's
rating of the scan itself, and at UCSF the label coincided with enhancement, since UCSF rated enhancement as increased
at 27 of the 29 progression scans and dated progression a median of 7 days after the scan (exploratory). Where the label is read
from the image, an image score is partly tested against its own input. At UCSF enhancing volume alone, with no fitted
model, reached 0.88 (0.79--0.95), above the locked imaging models, and our earlier re-implementation of the radiomics pipeline, locked, reached 0.80
there, as high as PRISM's images from one scan (both exploratory). At UCSF the models transferred, but because enhancing volume alone scored highest there, these
results cannot show that the images add to it.

\subsection{Most of the ranking rests on the timing and the enhancing tumour}\label{sec:4.3}

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md E4.
A baseline built from the time window and the enhancing tumour alone ranked scans almost as well as PRISM. It reached
0.80 (0.71--0.88) on MU and, locked, 0.73 (0.64--0.81) at LUMIERE. The combined model ranked scans better than it by
0.02 ($-$0.03 to $+$0.08) on MU and 0.04 ($-$0.01 to $+$0.10) at LUMIERE, so neither centre showed a clear gain. Within one time window the largest gain over the baseline was the imaging model's, 0.10 ($-$0.04 to $+$0.26) on MU, also with no
clear gain. With the BraTS 2021 masks the reading was the same (combined model $+$0.03, $-$0.04 to $+$0.09), and the
imaging model fell below the baseline, by 0.15 (0.05--0.25). With all scans before progression as negatives the
reading was also the same (combined model $+$0.01, $-$0.03 to $+$0.06).

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md M1 and E1.
The split of the AUC shows where each score's ranking lies (Figure~\ref{fig:3}). At MU 38\% of pairs fell within one
window. The clinical-history model ranked 0.93 (0.88--0.97) of the pairs across windows correctly and 0.64
(0.51--0.78) of the pairs within one. Ranking scans by their window's progression rate alone also ranked 0.93 of the
pairs across windows correctly, so the window could supply all of the clinical-history model's advantage there. The
imaging model ranked 0.68 (0.58--0.77) within a window and 0.73 across, a difference whose interval includes zero, and
the combined model 0.65 within and 0.92 across. Nine in ten pairs within a window come from the later window, so the
within window AUC is close to the AUC of scans taken later than 12 weeks after radiotherapy (exploratory,
Supplementary Section~S26). Adding clinical history to images raised the AUC by 0.11 (0.03--0.19) at MU and 0.12
(0.04--0.21) at LUMIERE, and at both centres the whole gain lay in pairs across windows. At LUMIERE only one of the scans the rater flagged as within three months of radiotherapy had progression
(Table~\ref{tab:1}), and the reduced models take the flag
as an input, so the reduced clinical-history model's across window AUC of 0.96 against 0.59 within one shows mainly how much the flag carries. There the
imaging model ranked scans better within a window (0.71) than across (0.51).

\subsection{The confidence statements describe the evidence but do not single out scans}\label{sec:4.4}

% Sources: NUMBERS.md 6 and 12B; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S1; NUMBERS.md line 406 (singletons).
The planned marginal sets of the combined score met the coverage rule on point estimates at all three centres,
covering 82\%, 87\% and 78\% of scans at MU, LUMIERE and UCSF, and named a single outcome for 75\%, 62\% and 64\% of
scans. They did not cover every subgroup. At UCSF they covered 97\% of progression scans and 65\% (51\% to 79\%, exploratory CI) of
scans without progression, and at MU the group of scans later than 12 weeks after radiotherapy with increased
enhancement was covered at 68\% (55\% to 81\%).

Calibrating within groups changed which groups were covered more than how well the worst ones were
(Figure~\ref{fig:4}). In the later group with increased enhancement coverage rose to 72\%, but a
marginal set enlarged to the same share of sets holding both outcomes covered it at 74\% (exploratory), so the gain
came from larger sets. Group calibration held both outcomes more often (31\% of MU sets against 25\%) and left 4\% of
MU sets empty, a defect at this cohort size.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:5}

% Sources: Sections 4.1--4.3; refs shi2025, booth2022.
PRISM did not clearly rank scans better than a baseline of timing and enhancement, so its use to a clinician is not
a better ranking than that evidence gives. What it offers, and what a reader study must test, is that it scores every scan from both kinds of evidence,
keeps them apart, and comes from models that kept ranking scans, locked, at two other centres. The models a clinician
is likely to be offered are image-only and tested on one split \citep{booth2022,shi2025}, and on identical data the
published pipeline for this cohort ranked scans below both of PRISM's separate models, on two sets of masks, though
not clearly within one time window. PRISM's two scores show that here the ranking rests mostly on the timing and the
enhancing tumour. The reader baseline is a model of that evidence, not a
clinician, and whether a clinician combines the same evidence as consistently, from volumes estimated by eye and a
treatment history gathered from the chart, is untested. PRISM does it the same way at every scan, from volumes
measured on the masks, noisy as they are, and says so in a report written on the computer that runs PRISM, whose card states how many comparable scans the
models were tested on. A fixed template passed the same checks, so the language model adds readable prose, not
correctness, and whether that prose helps is again a question for the reader study.

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md M1, E4; refs kallus2019, janes2008, beaulieujones2021, damour2022.
Any group reporting a glioma progression model could report two numbers next to its AUC: its gain over a baseline
of timing and enhancement, and the split of its AUC into pairs within and across time windows \citep{kallus2019},
a pair weighted analogue of the covariate adjusted AUC \citep{janes2008}. Neither needs new images, only the time of
each scan relative to treatment, which most public glioma cohorts record only in part.

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md M2, E2; NUMBERS.md 6.
Calibrating within groups covered the weakest group no better than enlarging the sets. Among the groups of
Figure~\ref{fig:4}, the marginal sets covered later scans with increased enhancement least, and calibrating within groups did not cover that group better than enlarging the sets, so
in cohorts this small its main effect was to move coverage between groups. Larger cohorts, or calibration pooled
across centres, would be needed for coverage within the smallest groups, and a centre adopting PRISM
would calibrate on its own scans.

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md M3, E3; refs pham2026, mansour2026.
The verifier rejected every planted value placed under the wrong region or score, including those written by an
agent that had not seen the rules, and no correct paraphrase. Binding direction words caught only the words on its list, which is the weakness of any
fixed vocabulary, and a wider list would need a fresh set of independent errors to test it. Other rule based gates for
clinical text forbid numbers altogether \citep{pham2026} or trace numbers to a measurement file without measuring the
check \citep{mansour2026}. The checks show that a report states what its record holds, not that the record is right,
and the record inherits the noise of the released masks.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md; NUMBERS.md 10; UCSF_AUDIT.md.
PRISM was developed on one public cohort, and the two external tests are its only evidence that the ranking transfers.
The label is progression as each centre documented it, scans shortly before progression are left out of the
primary analysis, no scan was read again, and at LUMIERE and UCSF the label is
partly read from the images the scores use. The reader baseline, the AUC split and group calibration ran on MU and
LUMIERE only, because UCSF-ALPTDG was no longer on our hardware, and at LUMIERE the time window is a single rater's
flag. In the first 12 weeks after radiotherapy, when
progression and treatment effects are hardest to tell apart, MU holds too few progression scans to test any score, and
the report says so. No clinician has read the reports. The next steps are to run the same analyses on UCSF-ALPTDG, to
pool the rare situations across cohorts, including the Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM datasets, and to run the reader study
we have designed, which is the test of whether PRISM supports clinical decisions.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

% Sources: Sections 4.1--4.5.
On public glioma follow up data, simple models of the treatment course and of tumour measurements ranked scans better
than the published image-only pipeline run with its authors' code, and kept ranking them, locked, at two other centres. Most of that
ranking rests on the timing and the enhancing tumour, which a reader also has. PRISM keeps that evidence apart from the image
evidence at every scan and reports both in a local report checked against the scan's record.
