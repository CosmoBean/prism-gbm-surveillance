\begin{abstract}
Surveillance MRI after treatment for glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas must be interpreted with prior imaging
and the treatment course. A progression score alone does not show the clinician the evidence that produced it or what
it leaves out. We built PRISM, a modular system that combines image-derived measurements, treatment context, separate
image and timeline pathways, uncertainty and provenance in a locally generated, checked report. On MU-Glioma-Post,
the timeline and image pathways reached AUCs of 0.82 and 0.71. Applied unchanged, the combined score reached 0.77 on
LUMIERE; at UCSF-ALPTDG, single-scan image measurements reached 0.80 and the image pathway 0.73. The evidence behind
the scores differed by cohort: treatment timing at MU-Glioma-Post and enhancing volume at UCSF-ALPTDG. In a second,
cross-cohort report evaluation, 406 of 406 reports passed a lexical check against their structured records. PRISM is
designed to make evidence inspectable, not to replace clinical judgement; its effect on clinical decisions needs a
reader study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Introduction 1); refs taal2008, brandes2008.
Glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas require repeated MRI surveillance after surgery and chemoradiotherapy. At
each scan, the clinical team interprets the current images against earlier images and the course of treatment. The
decision is hardest soon after radiotherapy, when treatment can increase contrast enhancement without tumour growth.
This pseudoprogression is most common in the first weeks after chemoradiotherapy and more frequent when the promoter
of MGMT, a DNA-repair gene, is methylated \citep{taal2008,brandes2008}. The information needed for that judgement is
longitudinal: images and their change, treatment timing and the parts of the record that are known on the scan day.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine C1--C5); SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md rows 1.7, 6.3; refs an2021, varoquaux2018, yu2022.
Radiomics and other prediction models can quantify image patterns, but a probability alone does not show the
treatment context, the measurements behind an image input, the uncertainty around a score, or what the model did not
use. These gaps are hard to fill from surveillance data. Public datasets often contain images but few treatment dates,
centres document progression differently, the hardest situations are uncommon, and segmented volumes need inspection
before they are used as inputs. Small cohorts also make a single test split an
unstable account of performance \citep{an2021,varoquaux2018}. Only 3 of 20 studies in a recent systematic review of
automated response assessment included external evaluation \citep{shi2025}.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (question).
Our initial radiomics work on MU-Glioma-Post led us to ask whether a system could turn the evidence available at a
post-treatment scan into a report that a clinician can inspect: one that keeps image and treatment-timeline evidence
separate, transfers to other centres without refitting, states where the evidence is thin and is written inside the
hospital.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim); NUMBERS.md sections 3--7.
PRISM, the system we built, turns each scan into a structured evidence record: clinical and treatment-timeline inputs,
measurements from the segmented tumour, separate image and timeline scores, a combined score, uncertainty fields and
their provenance. A local language model writes the narrative from that record, and a deterministic checker tests the
narrative against it. We developed PRISM on MU-Glioma-Post (MU) and applied its models unchanged to LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG (UCSF). The
component models ranked scans at the external centres, but the inputs that drove a score differed by cohort, so the report shows the two pathway scores separately, each with the inputs it is based on.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:2}

\subsection{PRISM}\label{sec:2.1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine table); SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 1.
PRISM handles each scan in five modules (Figure~\ref{fig:1}) and writes their outputs to one structured evidence
record. The timeline pathway and the image pathway keep the two kinds of evidence apart, so that the image pathway
can run where no treatment dates exist and the audit can show which evidence a score used. Segmentation masks supply
the measurements and images displayed in the report. The record also carries a set of plausible outcomes, agreement
between pathways, how many scans like this one have been tested and the provenance of every displayed value. The
report is written locally from that record, so patient data do not leave the hospital.

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, B1, B0; External test, Tbase); UCSF_RESULTS.md deviation 3; NUMBERS.md 2.1.
The timeline pathway scores a scan from clinical data and the patient's position in treatment. On MU its inputs are age, sex, IDH mutation and MGMT promoter methylation, the days since diagnosis and since
surgery, the start, end and dose of radiotherapy, chemotherapy and the visit index, each taken as of the scan day. The
first four alone form the clinical-only reference model. Where a cohort records no treatment dates, PRISM uses a reduced timeline that
keeps the clinical inputs, the days since surgery and a flag for the first weeks after radiotherapy. The flag comes
from the rater's note at LUMIERE and is approximated from the start of radiotherapy at UCSF. Both pathways are ridge
logistic regressions; scaling and any selection were fitted on the training data only. An unknown IDH or MGMT status
is coded as the reference category, and a missing measurement is filled with the training median, with a
missing-value indicator, inside the fitted pipeline.

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (EXPLORATORY pure imaging); UCSF_IMAGING.md; SPLIT_VARIANCE.md; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md Deviations 1.
The image pathway scores a scan from simple measurements of the segmented tumour. On MU its 27 inputs are the
volumes, shape and relative intensity of the segmented compartments at the scan, their change from the previous
scan, and the enhancing and total lesion volumes relative to the smallest in the patient's history
(\todo{Supplementary section: image pathway inputs and their harmonisation at LUMIERE and UCSF}). It holds no dates,
intervals or treatment flags, because such inputs carry when a scan was taken, which the timeline already holds, and
the image pathway has to run where no dates exist (Section~\ref{sec:3.3}). A single-scan version keeps only the 19
measurements of the current scan.

The combined score is one ridge model fitted on the inputs of both pathways and is shown beside them. At LUMIERE and
UCSF its inputs are the reduced timeline, the harmonised volumes and their change, the interval since the previous
scan and whether there was one.

% Sources: SEG_FIGURE.md; UCSF_IMAGING.md; NUMBERS.md mismatch 6; refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
The measurements come from the segmentation masks supplied with each cohort, which are the automatic masks released with MU
\citep{mahmoud2025}, the DeepBraTumIA masks of LUMIERE \citep{suter2022} and the masks provided with UCSF
\citep{fields2024}. PRISM renders the report's figure from the same masks by code.

% Sources: SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 2; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md Specification and "cost of the split"; RELIABILITY_CARD.md;
% refs angelopoulos2023, mitchell2019. Agreement rule with medians fixed on MU: NUMBERS.md 12A (round 4).
Each scan's confidence has three parts, a conformal set, a pathway-agreement statement and a reliability card
\citep{mitchell2019}. The conformal set is a patient-level split conformal prediction set at the 80\% level
\citep{angelopoulos2023}, built to contain the true outcome for at least 80\% of scans; it holds one outcome when
the score is clear and both when it is not. It comes from the combined model refitted on 70\% of the MU patients and calibrated on the remaining 30\%
(\todo{Supplementary section: system results}). On MU this split is made inside each training fold. For the external
cohorts we applied one MU-calibrated set unchanged, which assumes that their scans are exchangeable with MU's. The two pathways agree when both scores lie
above, or both below, their own median on MU, so the rule needs no labels and can be applied to a single scan. The medians come
from the MU training scans of the locked models, or, on MU, from the held-out scores of the other folds.
The reliability card describes the scan's situation, its phase of care crossed with the change in enhancing tumour
since the previous scan, by looking it up in a table of that cohort's held-out results; the scan's own result is
part of its row. A situation with at least 10 progression and 10 non-progression scans is tested, and the card gives
its AUCs; one with fewer has few cases, and the card gives counts only; one with none is not tested.

% Sources: REPORT_V3.md (Design, Checker v3); REPORT_V3_1.md; Supplementary S19.
A local language model writes the report from the scan's record, and a deterministic checker tests the report
against that record. The model (Qwen3.8-27B) runs offline on one GPU at temperature 0 and receives only the record; the checker
rejects any number, flag or confidence statement that is not in the record, any omission of required content, and any
wording that offers a diagnosis, advice or a probability (Supplementary Section~S19).

\subsection{Cohorts and the task}\label{sec:2.2}

% Sources: TABLE1_COHORT.md; UCSF_DATA.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
In all three cohorts the primary population is grade 4 glioma. We fitted each model once on MU and applied it
unchanged to LUMIERE and UCSF, and we call these models locked. Table~\ref{tab:1} lists what each cohort
records; UCSF holds one pair of consecutive post-treatment scans per patient, and because the first scan has no earlier
scan we evaluated the second only. Progression is documented in each cohort's own record, as a dated clinical decision
at MU, as an expert rating of response at each scan at LUMIERE and as a progression date supplied with the dataset at
UCSF.

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, Cohort, External test); UCSF_PLAN.md; INTENDED_USE.md.
The primary task is to decide, at each scan, whether this is the scan at which progression is documented. On MU a positive is the first
scan on or after the documented progression date, and a negative is a scan followed by at least 120 days without
progression. At LUMIERE a positive is the first scan rated as progressive disease, and a negative is an earlier scan
rated stable or responding. At UCSF a positive is a second scan whose progression date lies within 30 days of it and closer to it than to the
first scan; a negative is a second scan followed by at least 120 days without progression. On MU the intended use is scans from the start of radiotherapy
onwards, and unless stated MU results are for this population; LUMIERE and UCSF are evaluated on all scans in their
detection sets. We divide the intended use into three phases of care: during radiotherapy, early after radiotherapy (up to 12
weeks after its end) and later. A secondary task, predicting progression within 120 days of a scan, is reported in
the supplement.

\subsection{Evaluation}\label{sec:2.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan); SPLIT_VARIANCE.md (Results table 1); Supplementary S8 and Figure S2.
% OUTLINE open question (split variance): settled here as one paragraph + supplementary pointer (council round 1
% recommendation, pending author). UCSF own-split result moved to RESERVE.md.
On MU, every model was evaluated by repeated patient-grouped cross-validation. We used five repeats of five folds,
with all scans of a patient in the same fold, and report the mean over repeats of the pooled held-out AUC with a 95\%
confidence interval (CI) from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap draws. For the models and the conformal sets, no
patient appears in more than one of fitting, calibration and testing. We chose repeated splits because an earlier radiomics model on MU reached an AUC of
0.80 on one test split and averaged 0.72 when re-selected and re-tested over 500 splits of the same design
(Supplementary Section~S8 and Figure~S2).

% Sources: UCSF_PLAN.md (0e7c9fe); SYSTEM_PLAN.md (e7cba80); REPORT_V3_1_PLAN.md (4f7e7b9); UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A, B.
For the external tests, the locked models, fitted on all grade 4 detection scans of MU from every phase of care,
were applied under plans committed before any model score was compared with an external label; the one deviation from a plan is in the note
to Table~\ref{tab:2} (\todo{Supplementary section: deviations from the plans}).
External AUCs have 95\% CIs from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap draws. As a reference, we also fitted each
UCSF model on UCSF itself by repeated cross-validation. To test whether deep networks see more in the images than the
measurements do, we fine-tuned segmentation networks to score each scan directly, against a control trained on
permuted labels (\todo{Supplementary section: deep networks at MU and UCSF}). Table~\ref{tab:2} lists the planned questions and their answers.

% Sources: NUMBERS.md (status column) and section 12; OUTLINE.md decision 8. Round 5: one marker (dagger) replaces the
% "(exploratory)" tags; the full list moves to the supplement; report-model comparison is planned (REPORT_V3_1_PLAN.md 4).
Results of analyses not fixed in advance carry a dagger (\textdagger) where they first appear in a paragraph,
legend or table, and the analyses are listed in \todo{Supplementary section: analyses not fixed in advance}. Among
them are the definition of the image pathway on MU, whose use at UCSF and in the system tests was planned, and the
agreement rule and the reliability card, which were defined after the cohort results had been seen. We use comparisons within a phase of care or within an enhancement category to diagnose what a score depends on; a
within-phase AUC pools only the pairs of a progression and a non-progression scan from the same phase.

\section{Results}\label{sec:3}



\subsection{The pathways provide distinct evidence at the development centre}\label{sec:3.1}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Primary table; EXPLORATORY pure imaging, images + clinical); INTENDED_USE.md; NUMBERS.md 3.
In the intended use on MU (146 scans; Table~\ref{tab:1}), the image pathway\expl{} detected the progression scan
with an AUC of 0.71 (95\% CI 0.63--0.78), and the timeline pathway with 0.82 (0.75--0.90), mostly by separating
phases of care, within which it did no better than the images (Section~\ref{sec:3.3}). Clinical data alone, without
the timeline, scored 0.53, close to chance. The combined score that PRISM shows, fitted on the inputs of both
pathways\expl, also reached 0.82, so on MU the images did not raise the timeline's AUC. The planned imaging model, which adds measurements and three schedule inputs to the timeline
(Section~\ref{sec:3.3}), fell short of its target gain of 0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:2}). Predicting progression within 120 days of a scan was harder;
the model reached 0.60 (0.52--0.69) in the intended use (Supplementary Section~S23). A timeline pathway needs treatment dates, which the two
external cohorts largely lack, so we next tested how each pathway transfers to them.

\subsection{The components rank scans at two external centres without refitting}\label{sec:3.2}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (External test; EXPLORATORY clinical alone on LUMIERE); UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A; NUMBERS.md 4;
% enhancing volume alone: NUMBERS.md 12C (MU 0.622 on MU-IU, not 0.635).
The locked combined model reached 0.77 (0.68--0.86) on LUMIERE (Figure~\ref{fig:2}) and met its planned transfer
rule (Table~\ref{tab:2}). At LUMIERE the combined score was above either pathway alone, images 0.65 and the reduced
timeline 0.69\expl{} (the gain over the timeline alone had an interval that included zero). Clinical data alone
stayed close to chance in all three cohorts (Figure~\ref{fig:2}). Enhancing tumour volume alone, with no fitted
model\expl, scored 0.62 on MU, below the image pathway, and 0.66 on LUMIERE, level with it; at LUMIERE only the
combined score did better than this simplest baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:2}).

% Sources: UCSF_RESULTS.md (Part A, Part B, Q1--Q3, contrasts); NUMBERS.md mismatch 1. Council ruling 1.
At UCSF, both planned image models transferred. Measurements of the single scan reached 0.80 (0.69--0.89), and the
image pathway, which adds change from the previous scan, reached 0.73 (0.60--0.84), meeting its planned transfer rule
(Table~\ref{tab:2}). The change inputs therefore transferred less well than the measurements of the scan itself.
Training at UCSF raised the image pathway to 0.86 (difference 0.14, 0.03--0.26), while the single-scan model scored
about the same when trained there\expl. The reduced timeline had the lower AUC of the two pathways, 0.56; UCSF can only approximate its flag for the early
weeks after radiotherapy (Section~\ref{sec:2.1}). The locked combined score reached 0.86 (0.76--0.93), but it includes
these approximated timeline inputs, and part of its AUC comes from the interval between the two scans, which was shorter before progression\expl{} (Figure~\ref{fig:2}).

% Sources: NUMBERS.md 12D (operating point, EXPLORATORY) and 12F (calibration: UCSF PLANNED secondary, LUMIERE
% EXPLORATORY); SYSTEM_RESULTS.md review-round-3 sections D and F.
Read as a risk, the combined score was calibrated in the large at
LUMIERE\expl{} but overpredicted progression at UCSF (observed over expected 0.72, 0.55--0.91), and a
threshold set at the MU median\expl{} called 90\% of UCSF scans positive. A new centre therefore needs to recalibrate the score
before reading it as a probability or applying a threshold (\todo{Supplementary section: calibration and
thresholds}). 

\subsection{The evidence behind a score differs by cohort}\label{sec:3.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Cohort by phase; within phase; EXPLORATORY images + clinical); STORY.md gap 3.
At MU the scores depended mainly on where the patient was in treatment, and at UCSF on how much the tumour
enhanced\expl{} (Figure~\ref{fig:3}). At MU the scan schedule alone separates most
progression scans from most non-progression scans, because most progression scans came more than 12 weeks after
radiotherapy and most non-progression scans came within those 12 weeks (Table~\ref{tab:1}). Across phases of care, adding the timeline to clinical data and images gained 0.11
(0.04--0.20); within a phase it gained $-$0.02 ($-$0.13 to $+$0.11) (Figure~\ref{fig:3}A).
Within a phase of care, where the schedule cannot separate the scans, the image pathway gave about as much evidence as
the timeline, with AUCs of 0.68 (0.58--0.77) and 0.64 (0.51--0.78).

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (EXPLORATORY pure imaging; I as run); NUMBERS.md 5 ladder and mismatch 8 (the middle
% step's CI is now NUMBERS.md 12E: +0.050 (-0.003 to +0.106); third step +0.097 (+0.047 to +0.153)).
We built the image model on MU in three steps\expl{} to see which inputs raised its AUC (Figure~\ref{fig:3}C). Adding
change from earlier scans to the current scan's measurements gave the image pathway, 0.05 higher with an interval that included zero (0.00 to 0.11), and adding the three
schedule inputs of the planned imaging model (the interval since the previous scan, a flag for whether there was one
and the smallest volume counted from the end of radiotherapy), which gives that model's image inputs without its
timeline, raised the AUC by a further 0.10 (0.05--0.15) across
phases of care but not within them ($-$0.01, $-$0.06 to $+$0.04).

% Sources: UCSF_AUDIT.md sections 3--6, EXPLORATORY intervals table and Overall verdict; NUMBERS.md 5. Council ruling 2.
% DETECTION_DEEP.md; UCSF_RESULTS.md Part C (Q4); NUMBERS.md mismatch 6. Council ruling 6; resegmentation in RESERVE.md.
At UCSF the progression calls followed enhancement. Enhancing tumour volume alone, the simplest baseline, gave
an AUC of 0.88 (0.79--0.95), higher than the point estimates of the locked image models; UCSF rated enhancement as
increased at 27 of the 29 progression scans, and progression was dated a median of 7 days after the evaluated scan
(Figure~\ref{fig:3}B). Among the 37 scans rated as showing increased enhancement, the single-scan image model reached
0.65 (0.44--0.84), so beyond that rating the measurements added little. At UCSF a model therefore scores well
largely by agreeing with a progression call that was itself made from enhancement at the same scan, which is what the
image pathway measures. Deep networks fine-tuned on UCSF scans learned above a permuted-label control
(Table~\ref{tab:2}), and their scores tracked enhancing volume\expl{} (\todo{Supplementary section: deep networks at
MU and UCSF}). A clinician reading a score also needs to know how far it has been tested in the scan's
situation, and the confidence fields state this.

\subsection{The confidence keeps its coverage on average, and disagreement marks doubtful scans at MU}\label{sec:3.4}

% Sources: SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S1 and Specification; NUMBERS.md 6 and mismatch 13; by outcome NUMBERS.md 12B. Council ruling 4.
The 80\% conformal sets of the combined score covered 78\% to 87\% of scans in the three cohorts, at or above the planned threshold of 78\% (Figure~\ref{fig:4}A). Most sets were informative, holding a single outcome for 62\% to 75\% of scans. At UCSF the
point estimate sat at the threshold and its interval reached below it (68\% to 87\%). By outcome\expl, the sets covered 77\% (67\% to 87\%) of progression scans at LUMIERE and 65\% (51\% to 79\%) of
non-progression scans at UCSF. The other outcome was covered at 94\% and 97\%, and on MU the two outcomes were
similar. Thresholds fitted for each outcome on MU did
not correct this (\todo{Supplementary section: system results}).

% Sources: SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S2, S3; NUMBERS.md mismatches 4 and 5; medians fixed on MU: NUMBERS.md 12A and
% SYSTEM_RESULTS.md review-round-3 section A (MU fold-held-out median gives the same 40% / 0.90 / 0.59).
% Round 5 (audit WRONG 3): the planned own-median result first, the MU-fixed-median rule second and marked.
Disagreement between the pathways marked the less accurate scans at MU. The pathways disagreed on 40\% of MU
scans, and the combined score's AUC was 0.90 where they agreed and 0.59 where they disagreed; setting the disagreeing
scans aside also gained more than removing the same number of scans matched for phase of care. Both results met their planned tests
(Table~\ref{tab:2}; Figure~\ref{fig:4}B). The planned tests at the external centres, which
placed each cohort's medians on its own scores, were not met; at LUMIERE the difference was 0.11 ($-$0.05 to 0.27).
With the MU medians that the system uses, LUMIERE's AUCs were 0.89 where the pathways agreed and 0.67 where they
disagreed (difference 0.22, 0.10--0.34)\expl, while at UCSF the interval still included zero. The rule then flagged
60\% of LUMIERE scans and 48\% of UCSF scans (\todo{Supplementary section: system results}).

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (Reading, lookup table); SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S4; NUMBERS.md mismatch 14 and section 6.
% Round 5 (audit WRONG 6): MU denominator is the 146 intended-use scans of S4 (54 tested, 92 few cases).
Only four situations on the reliability card\expl{} reached the tested tier across the three cohorts, one at MU and
three at LUMIERE; 54 of
the 146 MU scans fell in the tested situation, most LUMIERE scans did, and every situation at UCSF had few cases
(Figure~\ref{fig:4}C). At MU no situation in the first 12 weeks after radiotherapy held more than three progression
scans. Tested situations had lower error than few-case situations at LUMIERE, with overlapping intervals
(Table~\ref{tab:2}); at MU the order was reversed, because the few-case scans there were mostly non-progression scans
early after radiotherapy, which the models score easily. 

\subsection{The report passes an automatic check against its structured record}\label{sec:3.5}

% Sources: REPORT_V3_1.md (S5--S7, ladder); REPORT_V3.md (S5 failure); NUMBERS.md 7 and mismatches 9, 10. Council ruling 5.
Every report written by the local model passed the check of its numbers, flags and wording against its
structured record, 114 reports at MU, 162 at LUMIERE and 130 at UCSF, 406 in total (Figure~\ref{fig:5}B). All 220
reports whose situation had few cases or was untested said so, and when a field of the record was changed, 28 of 30
reports followed the change, against a planned minimum of 27 (Table~\ref{tab:2}). These results follow one revision of the prompt\expl. Before it, every LUMIERE report failed, because a fixed
sentence about the rater's three-month flag quoted a number held in no field. We corrected the prompt, left the
checker unchanged and evaluated again on patients not used for the revision (\todo{Supplementary section: report v3.1}).
The check is lexical and cannot judge emphasis, order or what a clinician would infer.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:4}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 1); NUMBERS.md 3--6.
PRISM is a system for making longitudinal surveillance evidence inspectable. It gives the clinician separate image
and treatment-timeline evidence at each scan, the measurements and context behind those fields, and a report written
inside the hospital that states where the evidence is thin. The component models ranked scans at two external centres
without refitting, but their evidence differed by setting. At the development centre the timeline provided most of the
evidence, largely because of when patients were scanned; within a phase of care the images provided about as much. At
UCSF enhancing volume provided the score, and enhancing volume alone did as well as the image pathway trained there.
The ranking transferred and the risk scale did not, so a group deploying PRISM at a
new centre would recalibrate a probability and set any threshold on local scans.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 2); SYSTEM_PLAN.md. Council ruling 7 (combined score definition).
Because its modules are separate, PRISM can run where treatment dates are missing. The image pathway needs only the scans and
their masks, which is what most public surveillance datasets hold; at LUMIERE and UCSF it ran without treatment
dates. Where dates exist, the timeline is added as a second pathway, and the combined score is shown beside both without changing the image model. The reliability card is a lookup table, so a new cohort adds rows to it, and a situation's tier can
change as evidence accumulates without refitting anything.

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (example card); NUMBERS.md 10 (scenario).
Consider, as an illustration, a scan eight weeks after radiotherapy in which the enhancing tumour has grown. At MU, 1
of the 9 scans in this situation on the card (all grade 4 detection scans) was progression\expl. The timeline pathway scores such a scan
low, because most scans at this point in treatment are not progression, and the image pathway scores it high, because
enhancement has grown. PRISM's report would show both scores, state that they disagree, give a conformal set that
holds both outcomes and record that the card has few cases for this situation. The clinician then sees that the
system has no measured basis for a call at this scan, in the weeks when pseudoprogression is most common, and decides
with information PRISM does not use, such as symptoms, steroid dose and the radiotherapy plan.

% Sources: SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md (1.3, 1.13, 2.1, 3.1, Finding); COMPARABLE_RESULTS.md; RESERVE.md (Christodoulou).
Each of PRISM's components has been described before, but we are not aware of a system that combines them at a
single scan, with each kind of evidence kept apart and the system's own test results attached.
Automatic segmentation of post-treatment glioma with measurement of change between scans is established
\citep{rudie2022}, conformal prediction has been applied to glioma segmentation \citep{elyassirad2025}, clinical and
imaging data have been fused to separate pseudoprogression from progression \citep{gomaa2025}, and model cards report
performance by subgroup \citep{mitchell2019}. The closest system we found combines treatment variables extracted by a
language model with tumour volumes to assign a structured follow-up category at one centre, without an uncertainty
attached to the output \citep{jabal2026}. On MU \citet{christodoulou2026} predicted a label assigned at the patient level, on LUMIERE \todo{cite Tikhonov 2025,
arXiv:2509.06511; full reference not yet verified} classified each scan into four response categories, and
\citet{chen2026} predicted recurrence risk from preoperative and postoperative MRI across centres; none of these is
detection at the scan.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 5); NUMBERS.md 10; UCSF_AUDIT.md (caveat sentence); REPORT_V3_1.md Limits.
PRISM was developed at one centre (Table~\ref{tab:1}), and the two external tests are its only evidence that
its ranking of scans transfers. The label is progression as each centre
documented it; no scan was re-read for this study, and at UCSF most progression scans were also rated as showing
increased enhancement. Subgroups are small, and the analyses marked \textdagger{} need testing on new data. No
clinician has yet read the reports. PRISM forecast progression within 120 days less well than it detected progression at the scan
(AUC 0.60 in the intended use).

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 6); RELATED_WORK.md section 3 (Burdenko, UCSD-PTGBM).
The next step is to pool the rare situations, early progression after radiotherapy and growing enhancement without
it, across cohorts and train on them. The Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM datasets add radiotherapy detail and advanced
sequences, and models fine-tuned on these long-tail cases could be tested on their scans in the card's thin
situations. A reader study in which clinicians use PRISM's
reports at the scan would show whether separate evidence and a stated confidence change their decisions.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim). Round 4: one sentence (round4_trim.md C29 fallback, coordinator ruling).
PRISM keeps image and treatment-timeline evidence apart and shows the clinician both scores at every scan with how
far each has been tested there; its models ranked scans at two other centres without refitting.
