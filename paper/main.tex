\begin{abstract}
After treatment for aggressive glioma, changes on serial MRI must be read alongside the treatment course.
A progression score can rank surveillance scans, but cannot show whether it reflects MRI measurements
or treatment context. We developed PRISM, a local system that makes progression scores inspectable by combining tumour
measurements and treatment context in a checked report. On MU-Glioma-Post, the public development cohort, the clinical-history
model separated scans with recorded progression from other scans (AUC 0.82). Applied without refitting to
external cohorts, prespecified models reached AUCs of 0.77 at LUMIERE (combined) and 0.80 at UCSF-ALPTDG (images from one scan). Audits suggested the
scores followed treatment timing at MU and enhancing volume at UCSF. PRISM shows each score with the measurements, treatment context and provenance behind it. After one revision of the prompt, all 406 reports met predefined automatic checks against their structured records. Future work will
evaluate the reports with clinicians.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Introduction 1); refs taal2008, brandes2008, wen2023.
After surgery and chemoradiotherapy for glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas, serial MRI is read against earlier
scans and the course of treatment. One early challenge is pseudoprogression. Treatment can produce new or increasing
enhancement that resembles tumour growth. Pseudoprogression is concentrated within 12 weeks of completing
chemoradiotherapy \citep{wen2023} and is more common in tumours with MGMT promoter methylation \citep{taal2008,brandes2008}.

Models intended to predict progression assign a score to each surveillance scan. On its own, a score gives an assessment
without showing the information that informed it. A score does not show whether the available information agrees, whether
relevant information is missing, or how the model has performed in comparable clinical situations.

% Sources: STYLE.md section 10 (tagline, 2026-11-03); NOVELTY_SEARCH_2026-11-12.md.
PRISM is a system that makes progression scores inspectable. For each scan, it keeps image measurements, treatment
context, and separate model scores together in a record that is checked before it is written as a report. The name comes from this separation. A prism splits light, and PRISM keeps image measurements and treatment context apart. Earlier systems have generated
brain tumour reports from structured features extracted from segmentation \citep{herasrivera2026,valerio2025}, and
deterministic checks have compared generated clinical text with source records \citep{zeba2026}. PRISM applies these
ideas to glioma surveillance after treatment. Its language model writes the completed record as text, and an automatic checker compares the text with that record.

% Sources: REVISION_2026-11-13.md (advisor's review A1--A3); NUMBERS.md 3--7.
We developed PRISM on the public MU-Glioma-Post cohort and validated it, without refitting, in the LUMIERE and
UCSF-ALPTDG cohorts. Testing it showed why a score should be inspectable, because the same kind of score rested on
different information in different cohorts. This paper makes four contributions. The first two are the methods we
built, and the last two are what testing them showed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item This paper introduces PRISM, a system that builds one record for each follow up scan. The record holds tumour
volumes and their change measured from segmentation masks, three separately fitted scores (a clinical-history model, an
imaging model and a combined model), and three statements of confidence (an 80\% conformal set, whether the
clinical-history and imaging models agree, and how the models performed in comparable situations), each value with its
source. We defined the rules for agreement and for comparable situations after seeing the first results
(Sections~\ref{sec:2.1}--\ref{sec:2.3}, tested in Sections~\ref{sec:4.in}--\ref{sec:4.2}).
\item This paper presents a reporting workflow that runs entirely on local hardware. A language model writes the report
from the record alone, citing for each sentence the record fields it uses, and a deterministic checker tests every number, flag and confidence statement in the report against the record
(Section~\ref{sec:2.4}, validated in Section~\ref{sec:4.3}).
\item This paper shows that what a progression score rests on can differ between cohorts with how each documented
progression. At MU the
scores followed treatment timing, and at UCSF enhancing volume alone ranked scans about as well as the fitted models, which an
AUC alone cannot reveal (Section~\ref{sec:4.4}).
\item This paper tests PRISM in two external cohorts with models fitted once on MU and, for its planned tests, rules
fixed in advance. We
test whether the scores still rank scans, whether the confidence statements keep their stated coverage, and whether
every report passes the checker (Sections~\ref{sec:3.2} and \ref{sec:4.1}--\ref{sec:4.3}).
\end{enumerate}

% Sources: REVISION_2026-11-13.md (A3 roadmap).
Section~\ref{sec:2} describes PRISM, from its inputs and measurements
(Section~\ref{sec:2.1}) and its three models (Section~\ref{sec:2.2}) to its confidence statements
(Section~\ref{sec:2.3}), its record and report (Section~\ref{sec:2.4}), and what it costs to run
(Section~\ref{sec:2.5}). Section~\ref{sec:3} describes the three cohorts, the task, how each cohort was evaluated, which tests were fixed in

the models locked, in the two external cohorts. It ends with the audits of what information carries the scores.
Section~\ref{sec:5} discusses what the results mean for anyone who builds or evaluates such scores, and what remains
untested, above all whether the reports help clinicians.

\section{PRISM}\label{sec:2}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine table); SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 1.
In five stages (Figure~\ref{fig:1}A), PRISM turns one follow up scan and the patient's clinical record into a
structured record and a report. PRISM measures the tumour, scores the scan with three models, attaches three confidence statements,
and writes every value into one record with its source. A local language model then writes the report from that record, and a
checker compares the report with the record. Table~\ref{tab:models} lists every score used in the paper.

\subsection{Inputs and measurements}\label{sec:2.1}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, B1; EXPLORATORY pure imaging); UCSF_IMAGING.md; NUMBERS.md 2.1;
% refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
PRISM takes three inputs for a scan. The first is the segmentation of the current scan and of earlier scans, which gives
the volumes of enhancing tumour, of FLAIR hyperintensity, and of necrosis and the resection cavity. We used the images and masks
as released with each cohort \citep{mahmoud2025,suter2022,fields2024}, already registered to a common space by each
provider, so every volume can be traced to a public file. PRISM does no registration, resampling or segmentation of its
own. From the masks and images PRISM computes 27 measurements. Nineteen describe the current scan. They are the log volumes of the six compartments (enhancing tumour, necrosis, FLAIR hyperintensity, resection cavity, tumour
core and whole lesion) and 13 measures of shape and of T1 contrast and FLAIR intensity, the intensities divided by the median of normal
appearing brain more than 10\,mm from the lesion and cavity (Supplementary Table~S47). The other eight describe change. They are the log ratios of the volumes to those of the previous scan and the enhancing and
lesion volumes relative to the smallest the patient has had up to that scan (Table~\ref{tab:models}). The second input is the treatment context as of the scan day. It records the days since diagnosis and surgery and the start, end and dose of radiotherapy and chemotherapy. From it PRISM places the scan in one of four phases of care, before the end of radiotherapy, early after it (up to 12
weeks after its end), later, or unknown when the dates are missing. The third input is the
patient's age, sex, and IDH mutation and MGMT promoter methylation status, two tumour markers recorded at diagnosis. Every input is taken as of the scan day, so no information from a later
visit enters a score, except IDH and MGMT status at 8 grade 4 scans taken before the diagnosis date
(Supplementary Section~S4).

\subsection{Three models}\label{sec:2.2}

% Sources: prism/models/formulations.py (fit, CS); prism/experiments/c3_radiomics.py (BASE_PRE);
% DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, B1, Tbase); NUMBERS.md 2.1.
PRISM scores each scan with three models. The clinical-history model uses the treatment context and the patient
characteristics, the imaging model uses the 27 measurements and no dates or intervals, and the combined model uses both
sets of inputs. Each is a logistic regression with an L2 (ridge) penalty. We chose it because its coefficients can be read and because deep networks fine tuned on MU added nothing to the clinical-history model (Table~\ref{tab:2}). Missing values are imputed
with the median of the training scans and flagged with an indicator, unknown IDH or MGMT status is coded as the
reference category, and the inputs are standardised. The penalty is chosen from six values of the inverse strength $C$ (0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1 and 3) by
threefold cross validation grouped by patient within the training data, minimising the log loss. Each model outputs a score between 0 and 1 that ranks scans by how closely they resemble progression scans in the training data.

Where a cohort lacks treatment dates, PRISM uses a reduced clinical-history model with the inputs that cohort has
(Table~\ref{tab:models}). The flag for the first weeks after radiotherapy is taken from the rater's note at LUMIERE and
approximated at UCSF as 42 to 125 days after the radiotherapy start, assuming a six week course. LUMIERE's masks have three compartments and no separate cavity, so
for LUMIERE we fitted a separate imaging model on MU with only those three volumes and their change since the previous
scan\expl. UCSF holds two scans per patient, so there the change measurements use the first
scan as the only earlier one. Because the imaging model needs only scans and masks, PRISM still gives an imaging score in a cohort with
no treatment dates.

\subsection{Confidence statements}\label{sec:2.3}

% Sources: prism/audit/conformal.py; prism/analyses/system.py; SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 2; RELIABILITY_CARD.md;
% prism/audit/reliability.py; refs angelopoulos2023, romano2020, mitchell2019.
PRISM attaches three confidence statements to each score and keeps them separate, so that each can be checked on its
own.

The first is a conformal set at the 80\% level \citep{angelopoulos2023}, which names the outcomes the combined score
cannot rule out. As planned, we refitted the combined model on 70\% of the MU training patients and used the other 30\% for
calibration. Write $\hat p(1) = \hat p$ for a scan's score and $\hat p(0) = 1 - \hat p$. For each calibration scan $i$ with outcome
$y_i$, the nonconformity score is $s_i = 1 - \hat p_i(y_i)$, the probability the model did not give to what happened. Because a patient contributes
several scans that are not independent, each scan is weighted by one over the number of that patient's calibration
scans, so that every patient carries the same weight. With $\alpha = 0.2$ and $n$ calibration patients, the threshold $\hat q$ is the smallest $s_i$ whose cumulative weight,
with the scores sorted, reaches $(1-\alpha)(n+1)$. The added one counts the new patient with weight one. If no score reaches it, $\hat q$ is
infinite. A new scan with score $\hat p$ receives the set
\begin{equation}
C(\hat p) = \{\, y \in \{0, 1\} : 1 - \hat p(y) \le \hat q \,\}.
\end{equation}
The set holds one outcome when the score is clear, both when it is not, and in principle none when the threshold is
very small. The refitted model is not the one whose score the clinician sees, and it costs some discrimination (at
UCSF its AUC was 0.08 below that of the displayed score). We therefore also formed the sets from the displayed score
itself\expl. The calibration scores are then the held out predictions of the same model from fivefold cross validation
grouped by patient over the same training data, and the threshold is found in the same way. Because the displayed
model was fitted on the calibration patients too, this version has no finite sample guarantee, unlike the split
version and unlike variants that keep each fold's model \citep{vovk2015,barber2021}, so we judge it only by the coverage it
reached. The reports evaluated here carry the planned sets. We computed sets in the same way for the
clinical-history and imaging models to test them. If a new patient is exchangeable with the calibration patients, meaning drawn from the same population in no
particular order, the set contains the true outcome of about 80\% of
their scans on average, but not necessarily within each subgroup \citep{romano2020}. On MU the calibration split was made inside each training fold.
Externally we used one model and one threshold from MU, unchanged.

The second statement records whether the clinical-history and imaging models agree. The two models agree when both scores fall on the
same side of their medians over the cohort's evaluation scans. A score equal to the median counts as below. This
rule uses no outcome labels. Because a cohort median needs a batch of scans, we later also tested medians fixed on MU, which can be applied to a
single scan\expl{} (a dagger marks an analysis not fixed in advance, Section~\ref{sec:3.3}).

The third statement is a reliability card\expl{} \citep{mitchell2019}, a lookup of how the models performed on scans in
the same situation. A situation is the phase of care crossed with the state of the enhancing tumour. That state is absent when the scan has
less than 0.1\,cc, unknown without a usable segmentation, without comparison when there is no previous scan, increased
when it grew by at least 1\,cc and 40\% from a measurable previous volume or by at least 1\,cc of new enhancement from
none, and not increased otherwise. For each cohort the card counts the scans with and without progression in that situation among the cohort's
held out results. With at least 10 of each it shows the AUC of all three models with 95\% confidence intervals (tested), with fewer it
shows the counts only (few cases), and with none it says the situation was not tested. As run, the card's counts
include the scan being described, which flatters it. We therefore also built each scan's card without any scan of its
patient\expl{} (Section~\ref{sec:4.2}). PRISM also flags a scan whose IDH or MGMT status is
unknown or whose inputs lie outside the 1st to 99th percentile of the training data.

\subsection{Record and report}\label{sec:2.4}

% Sources: prism/report/facts_v3_1.py; prism/report/prompts_v3_1.py; prism/report/checker.py (check_v3);
% REPORT_V3.md (Serving, Checker v3); REPORT_V3_1.md; Supplementary S19.
Every value PRISM computes for a scan enters one structured record under a fixed key, with its source. A local
language model (Qwen3.8-27B, run with greedy decoding) then writes the report from that record alone, so no patient data
are sent to an outside service. The prompt is assembled from the fields present in the record, so the model is never asked
about information the record lacks, and decoding is constrained to a JSON schema in which every sentence carries its
section and the keys of the record fields it uses. The model sees no images and does no arithmetic, and every number it writes must match a field it cites, up to the rounding, sign and percentage forms the record already
implies.

A deterministic checker then accepts or rejects the whole report. The checker rejects a report whose output cannot be parsed, or in
which a sentence cites a field that does not exist, states a number not found in a field it cites, or writes a number
as a word. The checker also rejects a report in which a raised flag is not mentioned, an input is wrongly called out of range, or
the conformal set or the agreement statement is missing or misstated, or the reliability tier is missing where the
evidence is thin. It also rejects a report that leaves out any of the three scores or the enhancing volume, that quotes a performance figure absent from the reliability card, or
that offers a diagnosis, a response category, advice, certainty words such as ``rules out'' or a verbal probability such
as ``likely''. A rejected report is not repaired. No rule ties a number to the label it is written next to or checks the direction of
a change, and the checker cannot judge emphasis or what a clinician would infer (Supplementary Section~S19).

% Sources: Supplementary S24; btrads.com follow up template v1.03 and category guide v1.01; refs weinberg2018,
% gore2019, zhang2020.
No public cohort releases the clinicians' reports for these scans, so we compared the report's content with the follow
up template of the Brain Tumor Reporting and Data System (BT-RADS) \citep{weinberg2018}, which clinicians found less
ambiguous than free prose \citep{gore2019,zhang2020}. Like the template, the report gives the position in treatment and
the change in enhancing tumour and FLAIR abnormality since the previous scan, here as measured volumes. The report leaves out
the template's category and management advice, which PRISM does not give, and steroid and bevacizumab use, which the
cohorts do not record (Supplementary Section~S24).

\subsection{Implementation and computational cost}\label{sec:2.5}

% Sources: .venv package versions (Python 3.11.14, scikit-learn 1.7.2, NumPy 2.2.6, pandas 2.3.3); REPORT_V3.md and
% REPORT_V3_1.md (vLLM 0.31.0, bf16, one H100, 1.08 s per report, 50.2 GiB, model load about 2 min);
% prism/analyses/timing.py (2026-11-13, one CPU core of an Intel Xeon Platinum 8481C; NUMBERS.md 13).
PRISM is written in Python 3.11 with scikit-learn 1.7.2 \citep{pedregosa2011}, and its language model is served by
vLLM 0.31.0 \citep{kwon2023} in bfloat16 precision on one NVIDIA H100 GPU with 80\,GB of memory. The model weights take
50.2\,GiB. On one core of an Intel Xeon Platinum 8481C processor, computing the 27 measurements from a scan's masks and
images took about 2\,s (median of 20 MU scans). Fitting one of the three
models with its penalty search on synthetic data of MU's shape (181 scans) took under 0.1\,s. Scoring took about 2\,\textmu s per
scan when scans were scored together, and computing the conformal threshold took under 1\,ms. Writing reports took 1.08\,s per report when
generated in a batch, after about two minutes to load the model. A scan therefore costs about 2\,s of processor time and
1\,s of GPU time once segmented. These costs leave out segmentation, because we used the masks released with each cohort. A deployment would add the
time of an automatic segmentation model. Every stage is one command of a single package with fixed random seeds, and each run records the hashes of its inputs, code and outputs (Supplementary Section~S16).

\subsection{How each cohort was evaluated}\label{sec:3.2}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, External test); UCSF_PLAN.md (0e7c9fe); SYSTEM_PLAN.md (e7cba80);
% REPORT_V3_1_PLAN.md (4f7e7b9); SPLIT_VARIANCE.md; Supplementary S8 and Figure S2; NUMBERS.md 2.3.
Each cohort played one role (Table~\ref{tab:eval}). MU was the development cohort. On it we evaluated every model by five
repeats of fivefold cross validation grouped by patient, so that no patient contributed to both fitting and testing. We report the mean pooled held out AUC. We used repeated splits because single splits are unstable in small cohorts \citep{an2021,varoquaux2018}
(Supplementary Section~S8). We then
fitted each model once on all 181 grade 4 detection scans of MU, from every phase of care, locked it, and applied it
unchanged to LUMIERE and UCSF under plans committed before any score was compared with an external label. The plans
named all 229 MU detection scans of gliomas of every grade. Fitting on those instead of the grade 4 scans changed the

Discrimination is measured by the AUC, the probability that a scan at which progression was documented scores higher than

\subsection{Audits of what carries a score}\label{sec:3.4}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Cohort by phase; within phase; EXPLORATORY pure imaging); UCSF_AUDIT.md section 3;
% UCSF_RESULTS.md Part C (Q4); Supplementary S22; NUMBERS.md 5.
Each label records how a cohort documented progression, so a score can partly reproduce the way progression was called
rather than what the scan shows. On MU, 48 of the 64 scans with progression were taken on the day progression was
documented. We used four audits to see what information carries a score. The first is the AUC within phases of care,
which pools only pairs of a scan with progression and a scan without progression from the same phase, so that treatment
timing cannot separate them. The second is enhancing tumour volume alone, with no fitted model, as a reference for how
far a label follows enhancement. The third builds the imaging model in three steps, from the current scan's measurements
to change from earlier scans and then three schedule inputs, and compares the AUC across and within phases at each step.
The fourth tests whether deep networks detect more in the images than the measurements capture. We fine tuned SwinUNETR segmentation networks to
score scans directly and compared them with the same networks trained on permuted labels (Supplementary
Section~S22). The fourth audit and the within phase AUCs of the planned models were fixed in advance. The rest were not.

\subsection{The volumes mostly agreed with a second segmentation but changed between close scans}\label{sec:4.in}

% Sources: Supplementary S14 (RESEGMENTATION.md); DIAGNOSTICS_IMAGING.md; NUMBERS.md 15.
The released MU volumes mostly agreed with a second automatic segmentation but changed considerably between scans taken
close together. A second segmentation of all 594 MU scans (BraTS 2021 SwinUNETR) gave a median Dice coefficient (overlap from 0 to 1) of 0.80
for enhancing tumour, with a quarter of scans below 0.49, and 0.85 for the whole lesion. The log volumes correlated at 0.92 and 0.91. A ridge model on the volumes and their change detected progression no better with the new masks

increased, images from one scan reached 0.65 (0.44--0.84), so this sample cannot show whether the measurements add
to that rating. Deep networks tuned on UCSF scans beat the control with permuted labels (Table~\ref{tab:2}), and their
scores tracked enhancing volume\expl.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:5}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 1); NUMBERS.md 3--6.
PRISM's combined and imaging models ranked scans at two other centres without refitting, but the information a score used depended on how
each cohort was scanned and labelled. At MU, the score mostly followed the treatment schedule and at UCSF it followed the enhancement on which
progression was called\expl. An AUC cannot show which evidence a model used. The audits of Section~\ref{sec:4.4} suggested which evidence each model used, and the report shows the clinical-history and imaging scores side by side, so a disagreement between them is visible at
each scan. Process variables can carry much of the apparent skill of an image model
\citep{badgeley2019}, and image models for glioblastoma surveillance have been benchmarked separately at the first and
second scans after radiotherapy \citep{guo2025}. Outside oncology, clinical data were the strongest input in one Alzheimer's cohort and images in another
\citep{brima2026}, and models with equal AUC can behave differently on new data \citep{damour2022}. We found no earlier
study that compared image and treatment context evidence within phases of care in glioma surveillance, or showed that
what carries a locked score differs between externally tested cohorts. The risk scale did not transfer to UCSF, so a new centre would recalibrate the score and set any threshold
on local scans.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 2); SYSTEM_PLAN.md.
Because its modules are separate, PRISM runs on whatever a cohort holds, provided it has scans and masks. The imaging model needs
only those, which most public datasets have, and images from one scan serve where there is no earlier scan. The clinical-history model
is added where dates exist. The reliability card is a lookup table, so a new cohort adds rows to it and a situation's
tier can change as evidence accumulates.

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (example card); NUMBERS.md 10 (scenario).
In the weeks when pseudoprogression is most common, PRISM has too few comparable scans to support a call, and its report
says so. Consider a scan eight weeks after radiotherapy in which the enhancing tumour has grown (Figure~\ref{fig:5}A). The clinical-history
model scores such a scan low, because most scans at this point in treatment are not progression, and the imaging
model scores it high, because enhancement has grown. The report shows both, states that they disagree and that the conformal set holds both outcomes, and records from the card that the situation has few cases (at MU, 1 of 9 such scans was progression\expl). The clinician then decides with information PRISM does not use, such as symptoms, steroid dose and the radiotherapy
plan.

% Sources: SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md; PRIOR_ART_2026-11-01.md; COMPARABLE_RESULTS.md; RESERVE.md (Christodoulou).
Each component of PRISM has been described before, from longitudinal measurement of change \citep{rudie2022} and late
fusion of image and clinical models \citep{boehm2022,gomaa2025} to conformal prediction in neuro oncology imaging
\citep{elyassirad2025,angelopoulos2024}, disagreement between modalities \citep{mahapatra2026}, reliability from similar
cases \citep{habas2007,mitchell2019} and reports written by local language models from tumour measurements
\citep{herasrivera2026,valerio2025}. The nearest systems combine some of these. One assigns a BT-RADS category at follow up from tumour volumes and treatment variables extracted by a language model, at one centre and without an
uncertainty measure for each scan \citep{jabal2026}. Another keeps a ledger of evidence with a confidence for each claim in
longitudinal neuro oncology, but works from reports rather than images and checks its evidence with a language model
\citep{liu2026}. On these cohorts, earlier work predicted a label for each patient on MU \citep{christodoulou2026} and
response categories from longitudinal MRI \citep{tikhonov2025}, and a
model trained on MU was applied unchanged at UCSF to predict survival \citep{ding2026}. \citet{chen2026}
predicted recurrence risk from preoperative and postoperative MRI across centres. None reports image and treatment context evidence separately at each scan, or tests a progression call for each scan locked
at two external centres. For reports, an ECG system already combines a local language model, a verifier and a conformal set \citep{pham2026}, but it
forbids numbers in the text rather than checking each against a typed record.
PRISM therefore combines known parts. It shows image and treatment context evidence at each scan with a confidence from
measured performance, and we tested the combination at two external centres under rules fixed in advance.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 5); NUMBERS.md 10; UCSF_AUDIT.md; REPORT_V3_1.md Limits.
PRISM was developed on one public cohort, and the two external tests are its only evidence that the ranking transfers. The
label is progression as each centre documented it. No scan was read again, and at UCSF most progression scans were also
rated as showing increased enhancement. Of the confidence measures, only the combined score's average coverage met its planned threshold at all three centres, and only on point
estimates. Disagreement\expl{} marked scans the combined score ranked less well at MU alone. That rule needs a batch of a
cohort's scans, and its single scan form (MU medians) was defined after the results had been seen. No UCSF scan fell in a
tested situation. The planned conformal set comes from a refitted copy of the combined model. Sets from the displayed
score kept their coverage at MU and LUMIERE, but they carry no finite sample guarantee and were not rerun at UCSF.
Building the card without the described patient changed one tier, so counting the scan did not change what the card
reported. The checker misses a wrong tier word, a flipped direction word and numbers swapped between labels, and no person has checked a sample of the real
reports for such errors. A fixed template states the same fields and passes the same checks. The language model's
reports are shorter and read more easily by formula, but we have not tested whether they help a reader more. Subgroups are small, the analyses marked
\textdagger{} need testing on new data, and no clinician has yet read the reports. PRISM forecast progression within 120 days less well than it
detected progression at the scan (AUC 0.60 in the intended use).

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 6); RELATED_WORK.md section 3.
The next steps are to pool the rare situations (early progression after radiotherapy, and growing enhancement without
progression) across cohorts, including the Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM datasets, and to test models tuned on them there.
A reader study would show whether separate evidence and a stated confidence change clinicians' decisions.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim).
PRISM keeps evidence from images and treatment context apart and shows the clinician both scores at every scan with how
far each has been tested there. Its combined and imaging models ranked scans at two other centres without refitting. Audits suggested that the scores
followed the treatment schedule at MU and enhancement at UCSF. An AUC alone does not reveal this difference.
