\begin{abstract}
A score for a follow-up MRI after glioma treatment is useful only if the reader can see what it rests on, and a
generated report only if each statement can be checked against the record. PRISM is a local system built for both.
It scores each scan with separate imaging, clinical-history and combined models, keeps every value in one record with
its source, and a local language model writes a report checked against it. The separation exposed what a single
score would hide. On MU-Glioma-Post, the time after radiotherapy and the enhancing tumour carried most of the
ranking, and the combined model added no clear gain over a baseline of the two (AUC 0.80). Applied unchanged, the
reduced combined model reached 0.77 at LUMIERE and 0.86 at UCSF-ALPTDG. All 406 reports for held-out patients passed
the record check, and a verifier rejected every value placed under the wrong region.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

% Sources: wen2010, taal2008, brandes2008, wen2023 (verified 2026-11-18, PubMed abstracts).
After surgery and chemoradiotherapy for glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas, each follow-up MRI is read against
earlier scans and the course of treatment. Enhancement may grow because the tumour has progressed or because of
treatment. Such pseudoprogression follows chemoradiotherapy in 20--30\% of patients with newly diagnosed
glioblastoma \citep{wen2010}. In one cohort scanned four weeks after radiotherapy, half of the patients whose scans
looked progressive had pseudoprogression \citep{taal2008}, and among patients with enlarging lesions it was more
frequent when the MGMT promoter was methylated \citep{brandes2008}. The response criteria therefore treat the first 12
weeks after radiotherapy apart. RANO 2.0 takes the post-radiotherapy scan as the baseline and asks for a repeat scan or
histology before progression is called in that window \citep{wen2023}. The timing of a scan is part of the evidence a
clinician uses to read it.

% Sources: taylor2022, kim2019, leone2023, thust2018, weinberg2018, zhang2020, gore2019 (verified 2026-11-18).
Advanced imaging helps but has not settled the question. In a meta-analysis of 30 studies, diffusion and perfusion
MRI reached pooled AUCs near 0.9 for separating pseudoprogression from progression \citep{taylor2022}, and adding them
to a radiomics model raised its AUC in a cohort of 61 patients \citep{kim2019}. In another cohort of 105 patients,
however, they added no significant gain to a model of clinical data and MGMT status, and radiomics and deep learning
models performed near chance \citep{leone2023}. Practice also varies between centres. Of 220 European centres, 60\%
reported follow-up scans in free text and 27\% applied RANO \citep{thust2018}. Structured reporting systems such as
BT-RADS make the clinical history part of the reading itself \citep{weinberg2018}, and after its adoption reports
more often recorded treatment and MGMT status \citep{zhang2020} and were rated more consistent by referring
clinicians \citep{gore2019}.

% Sources: booth2022, park2020, shi2025, an2021, yu2022, christodoulou2026, gomaa2025, tak2025, tarek2026 (verified 2026-11-18).
Machine learning models proposed for this setting mostly score the image alone and are tested on a single split of
one cohort. A meta-analysis of machine learning studies of treatment response found small samples and a high risk of
bias \citep{booth2022}, and of 51 glioma radiomics studies, 29\% were validated externally \citep{park2020}. A
systematic review of automated response assessment found that 3 of 20 studies explicitly considered clinical
characteristics, 5 of 20 used external test data, and no tool was ready for clinical use \citep{shi2025}. Single
random splits of small radiomics cohorts give unstable estimates \citep{an2021}, and most radiology algorithms lose
performance on external data \citep{yu2022}. On the public MU-Glioma-Post cohort, a radiomics model reported an AUC of 0.80 on a single held out
set of 30 patients \citep{christodoulou2026}. Where clinical inputs were compared with images on an external test set,
they matched the images, and time since the end of treatment was the strongest input \citep{gomaa2025}.
Longitudinal and interpretable designs have begun to appear. A temporal model of serial scans predicted recurrence of
paediatric glioma in data from several institutions \citep{tak2025}, and a concept bottleneck model on LUMIERE passes
predicted tumour measurements through the RANO rules, although the interval between scans strongly affected its
output \citep{tarek2026}.

% Sources: geirhos2020, zech2018, badgeley2019, pencina2008, beaulieujones2021, arun2021, saporta2022, rudin2019 (verified 2026-11-18).
A high AUC in this setting therefore says little until its basis is known. Models learn shortcuts, signals that
predict the label in the development data without being what the reader cares about \citep{geirhos2020}. Image models
have recognised the hospital that acquired a radiograph \citep{zech2018} and predicted hip fracture largely from
patient and healthcare variables \citep{badgeley2019}. Follow-up schedules tie the timing of a scan to its label, so a
score can rank scans well simply by recognising when they were taken. What a model adds beyond the evidence a clinician
already holds is the quantity worth measuring \citep{pencina2008,beaulieujones2021}. Heat maps drawn over a
score after the fact do not show this reliably. In chest radiographs, every saliency method tested failed at least
one test of trustworthiness \citep{arun2021} and all localised findings less accurately than radiologists \citep{saporta2022}.
For high-stakes decisions, a model whose inputs can be read directly is preferable \citep{rudin2019}.

% Sources: angelopoulos2023, mehrtens2023.
A score should also state how sure it is. Conformal prediction turns any score into a set of labels that contains
the true label at a chosen rate without assumptions about the model \citep{angelopoulos2023}. The guarantee holds on
average over patients, however, and in medical image classification it does not hold within subgroups or under
distribution shift \citep{mehrtens2023}.

% Sources: valerio2025, herasrivera2026, mansour2026 (preprints), prucker2025, wiseman2017, gabriel2021, zeba2026,
% rao2025rexerr, mukherjee2023, nowak2025 (verified 2026-11-18).
The written report is the other half of the reading. Language models now draft reports from automated measurements
of brain tumours \citep{valerio2025,herasrivera2026} and of other lesions \citep{mansour2026}. They also make errors
that a reader may not catch. In a prospective trial, 6\% of oncologic CT reports simplified by an open-weight model
contained factual errors \citep{prucker2025}, and systems that write text from tables have long misstated the numbers
they were given \citep{wiseman2017}. Automatic measures of factual accuracy disagree with one another
\citep{gabriel2021}. Checkers built for clinical text test each statement against the patient's record
\citep{zeba2026}, and checkers for radiology reports are tested on planted errors \citep{rao2025rexerr}. A report is
useful only if each statement in it can be traced to the record and checked. Because patient records often cannot
leave the hospital, the model that writes it should run locally, and open-weight models run on site now match closed
models at reading radiology reports \citep{mukherjee2023,nowak2025}.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine 2026-11-18); NUMBERS.md 3--7; METHODS_RESULTS.md E4.
No system for glioma follow-up MRI yet shows what each score rests on, states how sure it is, and checks its written
report against its record. We present PRISM, a local system whose scores and reports can be audited. By auditable we
mean that each score's inputs are reported separately, and that every number in the report is traced to its entry in
the scan's record and checked automatically (Sections~\ref{sec:3.5} and~\ref{sec:4.5}). Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Separate scores.} For each follow-up scan, PRISM scores the imaging and the treatment-course evidence
separately and in combination, with conformal sets and agreement between the models stated beside each score.
\item \textbf{What the separation exposes.} A timing-and-enhancement baseline and an AUC split by time window measure
what each score adds beyond the timing of the scan. On MU-Glioma-Post, the time after radiotherapy and the enhancing
tumour carried most of the ranking.
\item \textbf{Locked transfer.} The models were fitted once on MU-Glioma-Post and applied unchanged at LUMIERE and
UCSF-ALPTDG.
\item \textbf{A checked local report.} A local language model writes the report from the scan's record, and a checker
and a verifier test its numbers and claims against that record.
\end{itemize}

\section{PRISM}\label{sec:2}

% Sources: SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 1.
PRISM turns one follow-up scan and the patient's clinical record into a structured record and a report in five stages
(Figure~\ref{fig:1}). It measures the tumour, scores the scan with three models, attaches the confidence statements,
writes every value into one record with its source, and has a local language model write the report, which is then
checked against the record. Table~\ref{tab:models} lists every score in the paper.

\subsection{Inputs and measurements}\label{sec:2.1}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, B1); UCSF_IMAGING.md; NUMBERS.md 2.1; refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
PRISM takes three inputs for a scan. The first is the segmentation of the current and earlier scans, which gives the
volumes of enhancing tumour, FLAIR hyperintensity, necrosis and the resection cavity. We used the images and masks as
released with each cohort \citep{mahmoud2025,suter2022,fields2024}, so every volume can be traced to a public file.
From the masks and images PRISM computes 27 measurements. Nineteen describe the current scan (the log volumes of the four
compartments, the tumour core and the whole lesion, and 13 measures of shape and of T1 contrast and FLAIR intensity relative to normal appearing brain,
Supplementary Table~S7), and eight describe change since the previous scan and since the patient's smallest volume.
The second input is the treatment course as of the scan day (days since diagnosis and surgery, the start, end and
dose of radiotherapy, and whether chemotherapy had started). From it PRISM places the scan in a time window after radiotherapy (before its
end, within 12 weeks after its end, later, or unknown when the dates are missing). The third input is the patient's
age, sex, and IDH mutation and MGMT promoter methylation status. Every input is taken as of the scan day. IDH and
MGMT status count as unknown at scans taken before the diagnosis date (8 grade 4 scans, used only in training), the duration of radiotherapy and the time since
its end enter only once it has ended, and the prescribed dose enters from its start.

\subsection{Three models}\label{sec:2.2}

% Sources: prism/models/formulations.py; prism/experiments/c3_radiomics.py (BASE_PRE); NUMBERS.md 2.1.
PRISM scores each scan with three models and shows all three. The clinical-history model uses the treatment course
and the patient characteristics, the imaging model uses the 27 measurements and no dates, and the combined model uses
both. Keeping the first two apart is what lets the record show which kind of evidence a score follows. Each is a
logistic regression with an L2 penalty, chosen because it is simple and its inputs are few, and deep networks tuned on
MU did not improve on it (Table~\ref{tab:2}). The inputs are standardised, and the penalty is chosen from six values of
the inverse strength $C$ (0.01 to 3) by threefold cross validation grouped by patient, minimising the log loss. Where a
cohort lacks treatment dates, PRISM uses one reduced clinical-history model, fitted on MU, with six inputs that both
external cohorts provide. LUMIERE's
masks have three compartments, so for LUMIERE we fitted an imaging model on MU with only those three volumes and their
change. Because the imaging model needs only scans and masks, PRISM gives an imaging score in a cohort with no
treatment dates.

\subsection{Record and report}\label{sec:2.3}

% Sources: prism/report/facts_v3_1.py; prism/report/prompts_v3_1.py; REPORT_V3_1.md; Supplementary S8.
Every value PRISM computes for a scan enters one structured record under a fixed key, with its source. A local language
model (Qwen3.8-27B, greedy decoding) writes the report from that record alone, so the record never leaves the computer
that runs PRISM. Decoding is constrained to a JSON schema in which every sentence carries its section and the keys of
the record fields it uses, and the model sees no images and does no arithmetic. The record also carries two
descriptive statements. One says whether the clinical-history and imaging scores fall on the same side of their cohort
medians. The other is a reliability card, after model cards \citep{mitchell2019}, that gives for the scan's situation
(time window and state of the enhancing tumour) how many scans with and without progression the models were tested
on and, with at least 10 of each (the tested tier), their AUCs, and otherwise only the counts (few cases). The card counts the
cohort's tested scans; recomputed without the patient's own scans, it changed the tier of one MU scan. Because the released volumes are noisy (Section~\ref{sec:3.3}),
the report calls them indicative. The report follows the content of the BT-RADS follow-up template
\citep{weinberg2018} without its category or advice, which PRISM does not give (Supplementary Section~S8).

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:3}

\subsection{Cohorts and the task}\label{sec:3.1}

% Sources: TABLE1_COHORT.md; UCSF_DATA.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; DETECTION_TASK.md; INTENDED_USE.md; NUMBERS.md 2.2.
Grade 4 glioma is the primary population in all three cohorts (Table~\ref{tab:1}). The task is to tell the scan at
which a cohort documented progression from scans without progression. At MU and UCSF-ALPTDG such a scan must be
followed by at least 120 days without progression, and at LUMIERE it is a scan rated stable or responding
(Table~\ref{tab:eval}). The 120-day rule drops scans with shorter follow-up, so on MU we also evaluated every score
with all scans before progression as negatives, as the detection plan specified. Each cohort defines
progression in its own way (Table~\ref{tab:eval}), so AUCs are compared between models within a cohort. The design
ties time to the label. On MU most progression scans were taken later than 12 weeks after radiotherapy and most scans
without progression within 12 weeks (Table~\ref{tab:1}), so the time window alone separates many pairs. On MU the
intended use covers scans on or after a documented start of radiotherapy, and MU results refer to it: 146 scans of 80
patients, out of 181 detection scans of 92 patients among the cohort's 126 grade 4 patients.

\subsection{Evaluation design}\label{sec:3.2}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; SYSTEM_PLAN.md; METHODS_PLAN.md.
Each cohort played one role (Table~\ref{tab:eval}). On MU we evaluated every model by five repeats of fivefold cross
validation grouped by patient and report the mean pooled held out AUC, because single splits are unstable in small
cohorts \citep{varoquaux2018}. We then fitted each model once on all 181 grade 4 detection scans of MU, including the
35 without a documented start of radiotherapy, locked it, and applied it unchanged to LUMIERE and UCSF. At UCSF every
model was planned before any score was compared with the label; at LUMIERE the combined model was planned and the
other scores are exploratory. AUCs carry 95\% confidence intervals (CIs) from 1,000 bootstrap samples of
patients (2,000 for the timing-and-enhancement baseline, the AUC split, the group-conditional sets, the mask sensitivity
analyses, the published approach on the second masks, and the calibration, spectrum and marker-coding analyses), which resample the held out predictions without refitting the models. The timing-and-enhancement baseline, the AUC split and group calibration were run under
further plans written before they were coded. UCSF-ALPTDG was no longer on our hardware when they ran, so they were
evaluated on MU and LUMIERE only.

\subsection{Timing-and-enhancement baseline and AUC split}\label{sec:3.3}

% Sources: CHRISTODOULOU_FAITHFUL_PLAN.md (with deviations); CHRISTODOULOU_REPLICATION.md (faithful run);
% ref christodoulou2026.
\paragraph{The published approach.} We ran the published MU radiomics pipeline \citep{christodoulou2026} with the
authors' public code and settings, on the same patients, masks, label and folds as PRISM. It extracts 4,892 radiomic
features from the original, Laplacian of Gaussian and wavelet images of the four sequences of the current scan, keeps
256 of them within each training set by the authors' ranking, and fits a class weighted gradient boosted model tuned
over 400 Bayesian search trials with patient grouped inner folds, followed by Platt calibration. The authors' ranking
adds permutation importance only when an inner fold holds at least 30 scans, so with our 13 to 16 scans it ranked features
by coefficient size alone; we left the code unchanged. The published study gave every scan of a patient who ever
progressed the label of progression. We kept PRISM's label, one scan per documented progression, so the comparison is
of approaches on one task rather than a replication of the published estimate. The plan, committed before the run,
stated that the comparison with PRISM's imaging model would be withdrawn if the pipeline did not rank below it.

% Sources: MASK_SENSITIVITY_PLAN.md (with deviations); METHODS_RESULTS.md E5; ref hatamizadeh2022.
\paragraph{Mask sensitivity.} The masks released with MU are noisy: between scans at most 30 days apart the enhancing
volume changes by a median factor of 2.6. To test whether the MU results depend on them, we replaced every MU mask
with the label map of one public segmenter, a Swin UNETR trained on BraTS 2021 \citep{hatamizadeh2022}, kept the
resection cavity from the released mask, recomputed the 27 measurements, and refitted the imaging and combined models
and the timing-and-enhancement baseline in the same folds. The plan called the results robust if every paired difference had a CI
including zero and the reading against the timing-and-enhancement baseline did not change.

% Sources: CLINICIAN_BASELINE_PLAN.md; METHODS_RESULTS.md E4; ref beaulieujones2021.
\paragraph{The timing-and-enhancement baseline.} To measure what a score adds to evidence a clinician already holds
\citep{beaulieujones2021}, we fitted a ridge logistic regression on the time window after radiotherapy, log enhancing
volume and its change since the previous scan, with the same pipeline, folds and locking as PRISM's models. At LUMIERE the window is
the rater's flag. The plan fixed the reading in advance. A gain whose 95\% CI lies above zero counts as a gain, and
one whose CI includes zero is reported as no clear gain.

% Sources: METHODS_PLAN.md M1; prism/analyses/methods.py; refs kallus2019, janes2008, janes2009.
\paragraph{Splitting the AUC by time window.} Let $P$ and $N$ be the scans with and without progression, $s$ a score,
and $c$ the time window of each scan. The AUC is the share of pairs ranked correctly,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC} = \frac{1}{|P|\,|N|} \sum_{i \in P} \sum_{j \in N} K(s_i, s_j), \qquad
K(a, b) = \mathbb{1}[a > b] + \tfrac12\, \mathbb{1}[a = b].
\end{equation}
The pairs split into $W = \{(i, j) : c_i = c_j\}$, two scans from the same window, and its complement $B$. With
$A_W$ and $A_B$ the same average over $W$ and over $B$, and $w = |W| / (|P|\,|N|)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC} = w\, A_W + (1 - w)\, A_B
\end{equation}
holds exactly \citep{kallus2019}. $A_W$ is the ranking with the window held fixed, a pair weighted analogue of the
covariate adjusted AUC \citep{janes2008,janes2009}. A score with $A_B$ above $A_W$ ranks pairs across windows better
than pairs within one, which can come from the window or from real differences between the tumours of different
windows. To show how much of $A_B$ the window alone could supply, we also score every scan by the progression rate of
its window in the training folds (the window only score). At MU the window is from the treatment dates (during
radiotherapy, within 12 weeks after its end, and later). At LUMIERE it is the rater's flag for scans within three
months of radiotherapy, and the window only score takes MU's rate for its first 12 weeks and for the other windows.
The identity was checked on every bootstrap sample.

\subsection{Confidence statements}\label{sec:3.4}

% Sources: prism/audit/conformal.py; METHODS_PLAN.md M2; refs angelopoulos2023, vovk2022book, romano2020,
% barber2021limits, johansson2015, ding2023, mehrtens2023.
A conformal set names the outcomes a score cannot rule out \citep{angelopoulos2023}. Write $\hat p(1) = \hat p$ for a
scan's score and $\hat p(0) = 1 - \hat p$. For each calibration scan $i$ with outcome $y_i$ the nonconformity score is
$s_i = 1 - \hat p_i(y_i)$. Because a patient contributes several scans, each calibration scan is weighted by one over
the number of that patient's calibration scans. With $\alpha = 0.2$ and $n$ calibration patients, the threshold $\hat
q$ is the smallest $s_i$ whose cumulative weight reaches $(1 - \alpha)(n + 1)$, and a new scan receives
\begin{equation}
C(\hat p) = \{\, y \in \{0, 1\} : 1 - \hat p(y) \le \hat q \,\}.
\end{equation}
As planned, the combined model was refitted on 70\% of the training patients and calibrated on the other 30\%, inside
each training fold on MU and once on all MU training patients for the external cohorts. If a new patient is
exchangeable with the calibration patients, about 80\% of their sets contain the true outcome, on average over scans
but not necessarily within a subgroup \citep{romano2020,barber2021limits}. The weighting by patient makes even the
average coverage approximate.

% Sources: METHODS_PLAN.md M2; Supplementary Section S10.
Group-conditional (Mondrian) sets \citep{vovk2022book} compute $\hat q$ separately within each group. Our groups are
the situations of the reliability card, the time window crossed with the state of the enhancing tumour (absent below
0.1\,cc, without comparison when there is no previous scan, increased when it grew by at least 1\,cc and 40\%, or not
increased). Most groups are small, so each scan is calibrated at the finest of three levels that holds at least 10
calibration patients, its group, then its enhancement state over all windows, then all calibration scans, a fixed
fallback of the kind used for small Mondrian groups \citep{johansson2015,ding2023}. Coverage carries over to an external cohort only if its scans are exchangeable with MU's
\citep{mehrtens2023}. Because group calibration can raise coverage in a group simply by making sets larger, we
compared it after the planned run with marginal sets at the $\alpha$ that gives the same share of sets holding both
outcomes (exploratory).

\subsection{Checking the reports}\label{sec:3.5}

% Sources: prism/report/checker.py; prism/report/verifier.py (v1.1); METHODS_PLAN.md M3; refs zeba2026,
% wiseman2017, kryscinski2020, gabriel2021, rao2025rexerr.
The report is a list of claims, each a sentence with the record fields it cites. The planned checker of PRISM, frozen
before any evaluation report was generated, rejects a report that cannot be parsed, cites a field that does not exist,
states a number found in no cited field, omits a raised flag, misstates the conformal set or the agreement, omits the
card's tier where evidence is thin, leaves out a score, quotes a performance figure absent from the card, or offers a
diagnosis, a response category, advice or certainty words. A rejected report is not repaired. Such rule based checks
compare generated text with a source record \citep{zeba2026}, as extractive evaluations of data to text systems do with
a learned extractor \citep{wiseman2017}. Because the checker accepts a number that equals a number in any cited field,
it accepts a sentence that gives the FLAIR volume as the enhancing volume while citing both fields, or that writes
``larger'' where the record says ``smaller''.

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md M3; METHODS_PLAN.md M3.
A verifier adds two rules that bind a claim to what it refers to. A sentence is split into clauses at commas,
semicolons, colons and the word ``and'', and each number and direction word in a clause is assigned to the nearest
tumour region or score named in it. The first rule requires every such number to equal, up to its written rounding, a
cited value of that region or score. The second requires every direction word on a fixed list (larger, increased,
grew, rose, higher, and their opposites) to agree with the cited direction of its region. The rules as first written
read the region only from the names of cited fields and rejected every evaluation report, because most fields are
sentences such as ``enhancing tumour 3.5\,cc larger than at the previous scan''. We then let the rules read the region
from the text of a cited field as well, tested this change only on synthetic records and the 48 MU and LUMIERE reports
used to develop the prompt, froze it, and ran it again on the evaluation reports. To measure the verifier we planted one error
at a time in correct reports, with 24 kinds of error, as error injection is used to evaluate
factuality checks \citep{gabriel2021,rao2025rexerr}. Three binding errors were held out of rule writing. Because we
wrote both the rules and these errors, a separate language model agent that had not seen the rules then wrote further
errors in its own words, with correct paraphrases as controls (exploratory).

\subsection{Comparison with the published radiomics pipeline on the same task}\label{sec:4.1}

On the same MU scans, masks, label and folds, the published radiomics pipeline run with its authors' code reached an
AUC of 0.58 (0.50--0.66), and PRISM's clinical-history model 0.82 (0.75--0.90), a difference of 0.24 (0.15--0.34).
The pipeline also ranked scans below PRISM's imaging model, which reached 0.71 (0.63--0.78) from 27 measurements, by

the combined model 0.80 and the timing-and-enhancement baseline 0.78. With all MU scans before progression as negatives, adding 89
scans, the clinical-history, imaging and combined models reached 0.81, 0.65 and 0.78.

Clinical data alone, without dates, reached 0.53
(0.45--0.61), and deep networks tuned on MU did not improve on the clinical-history model with image measurements
(Table~\ref{tab:2}). The imaging model's lead over the radiomics pipeline is a comparison of image evidence alone,

\subsection{Timing and the enhancing tumour account for much of the ranking}\label{sec:4.3}

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md E4.

reached 0.80 (0.71--0.88) on MU and, locked, 0.73 (0.64--0.81) at LUMIERE. The combined model ranked scans above it by

scans the rater flagged as within three months of radiotherapy had progression
(Table~\ref{tab:1}), and the reduced models take the flag
as an input, so the reduced clinical-history model's across window AUC of 0.96 against 0.59 within one shows mainly how much the flag carries. There the
imaging model ranked scans better within a window (0.71) than across (0.51).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:5}

% Sources: Sections 4.1--4.3; refs shi2025, booth2022.
PRISM keeps imaging and treatment-course evidence as separate scores, and in these cohorts the separation showed
that the time after radiotherapy and the enhancing tumour account for much of the ranking. Most models offered for this
setting are image-only and tested on one split \citep{booth2022,shi2025}. On identical data the published pipeline
for this cohort ranked scans below both of PRISM's separate models, on two sets of masks, and PRISM's locked combined
and imaging models kept ranking scans at two other centres. PRISM measures timing and enhancement the same way at
every scan, from volumes measured on the masks, and its local report states how many comparable scans the models were
tested on.

Studies of glioma progression models could report two quantities beside the AUC: the gain over a baseline of timing
and enhancement, and the split of the AUC into pairs within and across time windows \citep{kallus2019}, a pair
weighted analogue of the covariate adjusted AUC \citep{janes2008}. Neither needs new images, only the time of each
scan relative to treatment, which most public glioma cohorts record only in part.

In cohorts of this size, calibration within groups mainly redistributed coverage between groups. Calibration pooled
across centres would extend coverage to the smallest groups, and a centre adopting PRISM would calibrate on its own
scans.

The verifier rejected every planted value placed under the wrong region or score, including those written by an
agent that had not seen the rules, and no correct paraphrase. Direction words are bound through a fixed list, which
a wider list and a fresh set of independent errors would extend. Other rule based gates for clinical text forbid
numbers altogether \citep{pham2026} or trace numbers to a measurement file without measuring the check
\citep{mansour2026}. The checks establish that a report states what its record holds; the record itself depends on the
measurements, including the released masks.

PRISM was developed on one public cohort and tested at two external centres. The label is progression as each centre
documented it, and at LUMIERE and UCSF it partly reflects the images the scores use. The timing-and-enhancement baseline, the AUC
split, the group calibration and the verifier were evaluated on MU and LUMIERE, and the published-pipeline comparison
on MU. In the first 12 weeks after radiotherapy MU holds few progression scans, and the report marks such scans as
having few comparable cases. Extending these analyses to UCSF-ALPTDG and pooling rare situations across further
cohorts, such as Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM, would sharpen these estimates. A reader study of PRISM's
clinical value is left to future work.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

On public glioma follow-up data, simple models of the treatment course and of tumour measurements ranked scans better
than the published image-only pipeline run with its authors' code, and the locked combined and imaging models kept
ranking them at two other centres. Because it keeps treatment-course and image evidence as separate scores, PRISM
shows the basis of each, and its local report states both, checked against the scan's record.
