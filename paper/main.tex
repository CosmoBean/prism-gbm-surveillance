\begin{abstract}
Machine learning models for glioma follow-up MRI report high accuracy, but a single AUC cannot show what drives a
model's predictions. We built PRISM (Progression Reading with Independent Scores and Measurements),
running on one local computer: a Swin UNETR network trained on BraTS data segments each scan, three logistic regressions score it from its image measurements, its clinical history and the two
combined, each with a conformal prediction set, and a local large language model writes a
report whose every number is checked against its source. On MU-Glioma-Post, the combined model reached an AUC of
0.82. Without retraining, it reached 0.77 at LUMIERE and 0.86 at UCSF-ALPTDG. For each scan,
PRISM reports what its score rests on: the image and clinical-history scores, whether they agree, and how the models
performed on comparable scans.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
glioma surveillance, brain tumour segmentation, large language models, conformal prediction, external validation
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

Gliomas make up about a quarter of all primary brain tumours, and glioblastoma alone about half of the malignant
ones \citep{ostrom2023}. Even with surgery, radiotherapy and temozolomide, median survival is about 15 months
\citep{stupp2005}. After treatment, patients are followed with MRI every few months, and each scan feeds a decision:
continue treatment, scan again sooner, or change treatment.

Reading these scans is hard because treatment can look like progression. Pseudoprogression follows chemoradiotherapy
in 20--30\% of patients \citep{wen2010}, more often when the MGMT promoter is methylated \citep{brandes2008}, and in
one cohort half of the patients whose first scan after radiotherapy looked progressive had pseudoprogression
\citep{taal2008}. Clinicians therefore read each scan against the time since treatment, and RANO 2.0 asks for a
repeat scan or histology before progression is called within 12 weeks of radiotherapy \citep{wen2023}.

Advanced MRI helps in some studies but has not solved the problem. In a meta-analysis of 30 small studies, 13 at high
risk of bias, diffusion and perfusion MRI reached pooled AUCs of 0.90--0.93 \citep{taylor2022}, and adding them
improved a radiomics model in 61 patients \citep{kim2019}. In 105 patients with suspected pseudoprogression, however,
they did not significantly improve on clinical data and MGMT status \citep{leone2023}. PRISM works from conventional
MRI and the clinical record, both part of every routine follow-up.

Machine learning models for this decision are mostly built on the image alone and tested on a single split of one
cohort. Studies of treatment response have small samples and a high risk of bias \citep{booth2022}, 29\% of 51 glioma
radiomics studies were validated externally \citep{park2020}, and 3 of 20 studies of automated response assessment
considered clinical characteristics \citep{shi2025}. Single splits of small cohorts give unstable estimates
\citep{an2021}, and most deep learning algorithms for radiological diagnosis lose performance on external data
\citep{yu2022}. A radiomics model published on MU-Glioma-Post, the cohort used in this study, was
evaluated on a single held-out set of 30 patients (AUC 0.80) \citep{christodoulou2026}.

A single AUC does not show what drives a model's predictions. Models learn shortcuts, signals that predict the label
without being what the reader cares about \citep{geirhos2020,zech2018,badgeley2019}. In this setting, a model of
clinical data alone matched one using MRI on an external test set \citep{gomaa2025}, and the interval between scans
strongly affected a model built on the RANO rules \citep{tarek2026}. The useful measure is what a model adds beyond
the evidence a clinician already holds \citep{pencina2008,beaulieujones2021}. Saliency maps drawn after the fact do
not show this reliably \citep{arun2021,saporta2022}, so a model that is interpretable by design is preferable
\citep{rudin2019}.

The image measurements themselves depend on how the tumour is outlined. After surgery and radiotherapy the scan
contains a resection cavity and treatment changes, which make segmentation harder, and the BraTS 2024 challenge was
set up for this setting \citep{deverdier2024}. Experts disagree about the quality of the same glioma segmentation
\citep{hoebel2024}, so measured volumes depend on whose outlines are used.

A score is also more useful when it states its uncertainty. Conformal prediction turns a score into a set of possible
outcomes that contains the true one at a chosen rate \citep{angelopoulos2023}, although the guarantee does not hold
within subgroups or under distribution shift \citep{mehrtens2023}.

Finally, the result reaches the clinician as a report, and reports vary. Of 220 European centres, 60\% assessed
follow-up scans qualitatively, 27\% applied RANO and 23\% used a reporting template \citep{thust2018}. After the
structured BT-RADS system was adopted \citep{weinberg2018}, reports mentioned bevacizumab and MGMT status more often
\citep{zhang2020} and were rated more consistent by the clinicians and radiologists caring for these patients
\citep{gore2019}. Language models now draft such reports from automated measurements of brain tumours
\citep{valerio2025,herasrivera2026} and other lesions \citep{mansour2026}.

Reports written by language models can contain errors that a reader may not catch. AI-written chest radiograph reports
contained clinically significant errors \citep{tanno2025}, 6\% of oncology CT reports simplified by an open-weight
model contained factual errors \citep{prucker2025}, and systems that write text from tables misstate numbers
\citep{wiseman2017}. Automatic measures of factual accuracy track human judgement poorly \citep{gabriel2021}, so
checkers test each statement against the patient record \citep{zeba2026}, and errors can be planted in reports to
test such checkers \citep{rao2025rexerr}. Because patient records often cannot leave the hospital, the model should
also run locally, and open-weight models run on site can label radiology reports \citep{mukherjee2023} and extract
their findings as well as closed models \citep{nowak2025}.

We present PRISM (Progression Reading with Independent Scores and Measurements), a system for glioma follow-up MRI
that addresses each of these points and runs on one local computer (Figure~\ref{fig:1}). PRISM outlines the tumour
with a fixed public segmentation network, either a Swin UNETR trained on BraTS 2021 \citep{hatamizadeh2022}, which
marks the enhancing tumour, necrosis and FLAIR hyperintensity, or the first-placed BraTS 2024 post-treatment model
\citep{ferreira2024}, which also marks the resection cavity, or it takes the masks supplied with the dataset. From the
outlines of the current and earlier scans it computes 27 measurements of volume, shape, intensity and change. Three
ridge logistic regressions \citep{pedregosa2011} score the scan: one from the image measurements alone, with no dates,
one from the clinical history, and one from the two combined. Beside each score, PRISM states how sure it is: the outcomes it cannot rule out (progression, no
progression, or both), chosen so that they include the true outcome for 80\% of scans \citep{angelopoulos2023},
whether the image and clinical-history scores agree, and how well the models performed on similar earlier scans. All
of these values are stored in one structured record, each with its source. A local open-weight language model
(Qwen3.8-27B, served with vLLM \citep{kwon2023}) writes the report from this record alone, and each sentence lists the
record entries it uses. Rule-based checks then compare every number and direction in the report with the record.
Unlike systems in which language-model agents read clinical notes and assign a BT-RADS category \citep{jabal2026},
PRISM's language model receives the clinical history as data and makes no decision, so every sentence can be
checked.

In this study, we developed PRISM and validated it on three public cohorts. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A pipeline from MRI to a checked report.} PRISM segments each scan with a BraTS-trained network,
derives 27 longitudinal measurements, scores the scan with three ridge logistic regressions and split-conformal sets,
and writes a schema-constrained report with a 27-billion-parameter open-weight language model. Every stage runs on one
computer, so no patient data leave the hospital.
\item \textbf{Separate evidence streams for every score.} The imaging model never sees dates, the clinical-history
model never sees images, and a combined model sees both. For each scan, a reader can therefore see whether the score
comes from the tumour or from the treatment course.
\item \textbf{An evaluation of what each score adds.} We compare every score with a reference model of the time since
radiotherapy and the enhancing tumour volume, and split its AUC into pairs of scans from the same time window and from
different windows \citep{kallus2019}. This measures what the images add beyond what a clinician already knows.
\item \textbf{External validation without retraining.} Fitted once on 181 scans from 92 patients in MU-Glioma-Post, the
models were applied unchanged to 178 scans from 79 patients in LUMIERE and 69 scans from 69 patients in UCSF-ALPTDG, from the supplied masks and from PRISM's own
segmentation.
\item \textbf{A report checked against its record.} A deterministic checker and a verifier test every number and
direction in the report against the record. We test the checks themselves with 24 kinds of planted error, and all 406
reports written for held-out scans passed them.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:2} describes the cohorts, PRISM's stages and the evaluation. Section~\ref{sec:3} reports the results
at the three centres, what the scores rest on, the effect of segmentation, and the conformal sets and report checks.
Section~\ref{sec:4} discusses what the results mean and their limits.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:2}

\subsection{Cohorts and label}\label{sec:2.1}

% Sources: TABLE1_COHORT.md; UCSF_DATA.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; DETECTION_TASK.md; INTENDED_USE.md; NUMBERS.md 2.2.
We used three public cohorts of patients followed with MRI after treatment for glioma. PRISM was developed on
MU-Glioma-Post \citep{mahmoud2025} and tested without retraining on LUMIERE \citep{suter2022} and UCSF-ALPTDG
\citep{fields2024} (Table~\ref{tab:1}). In all three, the primary population was grade 4 glioma.

The task was to distinguish the scan at which progression was documented from scans without progression. Each cohort
records progression differently, so the label follows each cohort's own data (Table~\ref{tab:eval}). At
MU-Glioma-Post and UCSF-ALPTDG, a scan without progression must be followed by at least 120 days without progression.
LUMIERE rates every scan, and UCSF-ALPTDG supplies two consecutive scans per patient, of which only the second is
labelled. Because the labels differ, models are compared only within a cohort.

At MU-Glioma-Post, the models were fitted on 181 labelled scans from 92 of the cohort's 126 grade 4 patients.
Performance was measured on the 146 of these scans (80 patients) taken after radiotherapy had started, the setting in
which PRISM is meant to be used. The external evaluation sets held 178 scans from 79 patients at LUMIERE and 69 scans
from 69 patients at UCSF-ALPTDG (Figure~\ref{fig:cohorts}). Because the 120-day rule leaves out scans with shorter
follow-up, we also evaluated every score at MU-Glioma-Post with all scans before progression counted as scans without
progression.

\paragraph*{Segmentation.} The tumour masks of each scan come from one of two fixed public segmentation networks,
which PRISM runs itself, or are supplied with the dataset (Table~\ref{tab:1}). The primary results use the supplied masks, so that every volume can be traced to a
public file. At MU-Glioma-Post and UCSF-ALPTDG, the supplied masks mark the enhancing tumour, necrosis, FLAIR
hyperintensity and the resection cavity. At LUMIERE, they are DeepBraTumIA masks with three labels, which count the
cavity as necrosis.

Both networks run with their public weights and settings and were not tuned on our cohorts. The first is the Swin
UNETR \citep{hatamizadeh2022} released for BraTS 2021 (fold 1; Figure~\ref{fig:1}b), a network of 62 million
parameters that marks the enhancing tumour, the tumour core and the whole tumour. It takes FLAIR, T1c, T1 and T2, each
scaled to zero mean and unit variance over the brain, in blocks of $128^3$ voxels that overlap by half. A voxel
belongs to a region when its probability exceeds 0.5. Necrosis is the core outside the enhancing tumour, and FLAIR
hyperintensity is the rest of the whole tumour. BraTS 2021 contains scans taken before surgery, so this network has no
resection-cavity label and supplies 22 of the 27 measurements. The second network is the winning BraTS 2024 model for
adult glioma after treatment \citep{deverdier2024,ferreira2024}, an ensemble of nnU-Net, MedNeXt and Swin UNETR
networks, run from the challenge organisers' container. It also marks the resection cavity and supplies all 27
measurements. \todo{SUPPLEMENT: LUMIERE's images are stored on an atlas grid with a different axis order from the
networks' training data, so each LUMIERE scan is reoriented to that order without resampling, and the label map is

\paragraph*{Three models.} The imaging, clinical-history and combined models are ridge logistic regressions on the
inputs listed in Table~\ref{tab:models}.

Each model is fitted with scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011}. Each missing input is set to its training median,
with an extra input marking that it was missing, and every input is standardised. The inverse penalty strength $C$ is
chosen from 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1 and 3 by the lowest mean log loss in threefold cross validation within the
training data, grouped by patient, and the model is refitted with that value on all training scans. Logistic
regression turns a weighted sum of the inputs into a probability of progression, and the ridge (L2) penalty pulls the
weights towards zero, which keeps them stable with many correlated inputs and few patients. Deep networks fine-tuned on
MU did not add to a ridge model of the clinical history and image measurements (Table~\ref{tab:2}).

At LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG, PRISM applies reduced models, fitted on MU with only the inputs that all three cohorts
record, because neither external centre records the full treatment course (Table~\ref{tab:1}; inputs in
Table~\ref{tab:models}). Both reduced models take a flag for scans in the first 12
weeks after radiotherapy. On MU the flag is
computed from the end of radiotherapy. At LUMIERE it is the rater's mark for scans taken less than three months after
radiotherapy. At UCSF-ALPTDG it marks scans 42 to 125 days after the start of radiotherapy, which assumes a six-week
course. 

At UCSF-ALPTDG PRISM applies the full imaging model, with the one earlier scan in the dataset as the previous scan.
At LUMIERE, whose masks have three labels, the imaging model is fitted on MU with the three volumes and their change
since the previous scan. At the 10 LUMIERE
scans with no previous scan, a model of the three current volumes is used instead.

\paragraph*{Confidence statements.} For each score, PRISM computes an 80\% split-conformal set
\citep{angelopoulos2023}, with its threshold taken from calibration patients that the model was not fitted on. Write $\hat p(1) = \hat p$ for a scan's score and $\hat p(0) = 1 - \hat p$. For each calibration scan $i$
with outcome $y_i$, the nonconformity score $s_i = 1 - \hat p_i(y_i)$ is the probability that the model did not give
to the true outcome. Each calibration scan is weighted by one over the number of its patient's calibration scans, so
every patient counts once. With $\alpha = 0.2$ and $n$ calibration patients, the threshold $\hat q$ is the smallest
$s_i$ at which the cumulative weight, taking the $s_i$ in increasing order, reaches $(1 - \alpha)(n + 1)$. A new scan
receives every outcome to which the model gave enough probability,
\begin{equation}

The agreement statement compares each of the clinical-history and imaging scores with the median of that model's
scores over the cohort's evaluated scans. The two models agree when both scores lie above their medians or both lie
below. The median uses no outcomes, and at MU it is taken in each cross-validation repeat.

CIs from 1,000 bootstrap samples of patients. With fewer, the tier is few cases and the card gives only the counts.
With no scans, the tier is not tested. The record carries the tier of the scan's own cohort. The card fits no model;
it reads results already measured, including those for the scan it describes.

\paragraph*{Record.} PRISM writes everything it computes for a scan into one structured record, a flat list of named
entries. Each entry has a key, such as \code{meas.enhancing\_cc}, and holds one value or one fixed sentence. The
entries give the time after radiotherapy, the volumes and their change since the previous scan, each model's score
with its cohort median and the inputs it is based on, the conformal sets, the agreement statement, the reliability
card and four flags. The flags mark disagreement between the two models, unknown IDH or MGMT status or an input outside
the 1st to 99th percentile of its training values, a card with few cases or none, and an 80\% set that holds both
outcomes. Numbers are rounded as the record is built (scores
to two decimals, volumes to one). Wording that can be fixed in advance, such as the tier, each conformal set, the
agreement, each change in volume and the reason for each flag, is stored as a sentence for the language model to
copy. The record holds no outcome label.

record never leaves that computer. Decoding is greedy (the model always takes its most likely next word), so the report does not depend on random
sampling. The prompt asks for sentences in a fixed order, from the scan's timing to one sentence for each raised flag.

\paragraph*{Splitting the AUC by time window.} We split each AUC by time window, because a difference in AUC does
not show which pairs of scans a score ranks well. The AUC is the share of pairs, one scan with progression and one
without, that a score ranks in the right order. In a pair within a window, both scans were taken in the same window
after radiotherapy, so the window cannot tell them apart and the score must rank them on other information. In a pair
across windows, the window alone can often order the scans, because the share of scans with progression differs
between windows. Let $P$ and $N$ be the scans with and without progression, $s$ a score, and $c$ the time window of
each scan. The AUC is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC} = \frac{1}{|P|\,|N|} \sum_{i \in P} \sum_{j \in N} K(s_i, s_j), \qquad
K(a, b) = \mathbb{1}[a > b] + \tfrac12\, \mathbb{1}[a = b].
\end{equation}
The pairs split into $W = \{(i, j) : c_i = c_j\}$, the pairs within a window, and its complement $B$, the pairs across
windows. With $A_W$ and $A_B$ the same average taken over $W$ and over $B$, and $w = |W| / (|P|\,|N|)$ the share of
pairs within a window,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC} = w\, A_W + (1 - w)\, A_B
\end{equation}
holds exactly. This is the decomposition of Kallus and Zhou \citep{kallus2019} with the pairs grouped into two sets,
and $A_W$ is a pair-weighted analogue of the covariate-adjusted AUC \citep{janes2008,janes2009}. To show how much of
$A_B$ the window alone could supply, we also scored every scan by the share of training scans with progression in its
window (the window-only score), whose $A_W$ is close to 0.5. The window is the time window defined above at MU and the 12-week flag externally. Externally the window-only score
takes MU's share for the matching window, and MU's
overall share for scans without a dated course of radiotherapy. Scans without a value of a score are left out of that
score's pairs. \todo{SUPPLEMENT: The identity was checked on every bootstrap sample.}

\paragraph*{Statistics.} Every AUC carries a 95\% confidence interval (CI) from a bootstrap of patients. Each of 1,000
samples draws patients with replacement and keeps all their scans, so the CI reflects the number of patients, not the
larger number of scans. The CI is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of the AUC over the samples. The bootstrap resamples the held-out predictions without refitting the models, so the CI shows the uncertainty
from the patients evaluated, not from model fitting. At MU each sample averages the AUC over the five repeats of cross
validation, as the point estimate does. Analyses added later used 2,000 samples. \todo{SUPPLEMENT: The analyses with
2,000 samples are the time-and-enhancement model, the AUC split, the group-conditional sets, the segmentation
comparison, and the analyses of calibration, of all scans before progression and of unknown marker status.} A
difference between two scores is computed within the same samples, so variation common to both scores cancels. We
report no P values. A difference whose CI lies above zero counts as a gain, and one whose CI includes zero counts as
no clear difference, which does not show that the scores are equal. Calibration, the match between predicted
probabilities and observed rates, is summarised by the calibration intercept (above 0 when the score predicts too low
on average) and slope (below 1 when predictions are too extreme) \citep{vancalster2019}. \todo{SUPPLEMENT: The slope is
above 1 when the predictions are too compressed.}

\subsection{What the ranking rests on}\label{sec:3.2}

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md E4; NUMBERS.md 3 (single inputs, B0, P - B1); SKELETON_2026-11-20.md timestamp-only check
% (exploratory, 2026-11-20); DETECTION_DEEP.md.
At MU, the time since the end of radiotherapy alone, without any fitted model, ranked scans at 0.77, and the
time-and-enhancement model at 0.80 (0.71--0.88), so the combined model added little there: $+$0.02 ($-$0.03 to
$+$0.08). These timing scores are exploratory. The visit number alone reached 0.78, and a ridge model on every
timestamp reached 0.76 (0.66--0.84). The time since radiotherapy is a legitimate clinical input, which clinicians
already weigh. At MU it carries much of the label, because 56 of the 62 scans with progression were taken later than
12 weeks after radiotherapy and 51 of the 84 scans without progression within 12 weeks, when clinicians ask for
confirmation before they call progression. The patient's characteristics
alone, without dates, reached 0.53 (0.45--0.61), so the clinical-history model's ranking comes from the dates. The
planned test of whether image measurements and schedule inputs add to the clinical-history model was not met, and
deep networks tuned on MU did not add to it either (Table~\ref{tab:2}). At LUMIERE, the time-and-enhancement model,
applied unchanged, reached 0.73 (0.64--0.81), and the combined model ranked scans above it by 0.04 ($-$0.01 to
$+$0.10), again with no clear gain.

% Sources: SKELETON_2026-11-20.md timestamp-only check (LUMIERE days since surgery, UCSF days from RT start, UCSF days
% since previous scan); UCSF_AUDIT.md sections 3--6; UCSF_RESULTS.md Part C; NUMBERS.md 4, 5, 12C; SEGMENTER_STAGE.md P1.
Timing did not carry over to the other centres, yet the combined model held, because there the enhancing tumour did
more of the work. The days since surgery ranked LUMIERE scans at 0.61, and the days since the start of radiotherapy
ranked UCSF-ALPTDG scans at 0.54 (both exploratory). At UCSF-ALPTDG, enhancing volume alone reached 0.88 (0.79--0.95),
and the time-and-enhancement model, applied with the approximated window in the segmentation comparison, 0.87, which
the combined model matched ($-0.01$, $-0.08$ to $0.05$; Figure~\ref{fig:2}c). The
label there follows enhancement. UCSF-ALPTDG rated enhancement as increased at 27 of the 29 scans with progression
and dated progression a median of 7 days after the scan (exploratory). Scans with progression had also been brought
forward. The days since the previous scan ranked them at 0.28, that is 0.72 with shorter intervals ranked as
progression, and the reduced combined model holds this interval as an input. LUMIERE's label is an expert's rating
of the scan itself, and there enhancing volume alone reached 0.66 (exploratory).

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md M1, E1, E4 and UCSF section; SKELETON_2026-11-20.md (all timestamps within window).
Among scans taken at a similar time after radiotherapy, the images ranked progression at about 0.68
(Figure~\ref{fig:3}). At MU, 38\% of pairs fell within one window. The imaging model ranked 0.68 (0.58--0.77) of the
pairs within a window correctly and 0.73 across windows, a difference whose interval includes zero. The ridge model on
timestamps ranked pairs within a window at 0.49 (0.37--0.60), at chance. The clinical-history model ranked 0.93
(0.88--0.97) of the pairs across windows correctly and 0.64 (0.51--0.78) of the pairs within one, and ranking scans by
their window's progression rate alone also ranked 0.93 of the pairs across windows correctly. The clinical-history
score therefore ranks mainly across windows, and the combined model followed it (0.65 within, 0.92 across). Adding
clinical history to images raised the AUC by 0.11 (0.03--0.19) at MU and 0.12 (0.04--0.21) at LUMIERE, and at both
centres the whole gain lay in pairs across windows. Within one window, the imaging model ranked scans above the
time-and-enhancement model by 0.10 ($-$0.04 to $+$0.26) at MU. Nine in ten pairs within a window come from the later
window, so the within window AUC is close to the AUC of scans taken later than 12 weeks after radiotherapy
(exploratory, Supplementary Section~S10).

% Sources: METHODS_RESULTS.md M1 (LUMIERE, UCSF).
At LUMIERE the window is the rater's flag, and only one of the scans the rater flagged as within three months of
radiotherapy had progression (Table~\ref{tab:1}). The reduced models take the flag as an input, so the reduced
clinical-history model's across window AUC of 0.96, against 0.59 within one, shows mainly how much the flag carries.
There the imaging model ranked scans better within a window (0.71) than across (0.51). At UCSF-ALPTDG nearly nine in
ten pairs fell within one window, almost all later than 12 weeks after radiotherapy, so the within window AUCs
(combined 0.87, imaging 0.73) are close to the overall ones and the pairs across windows are too few to read.

% Sources: STRATIFIED_OFFSET.md Q1, Q2; IMAGING_S3.md (exploratory design analyses, 2026-11-20).
No change to the imaging model raised its ranking within a window (exploratory). An imaging model fitted with a
separate intercept for each window, so that only within-window differences inform the image terms, kept the
within-window AUC at MU ($-$0.00, $-$0.09 to $+$0.08) and lowered the across-window AUC. A wider penalty grid changed

\subsection{Effect of segmentation}\label{sec:3.3}

% Sources: SEGMENTER_STAGE.md P2, P3; METHODS_RESULTS.md E5; NUMBERS.md 15.
The enhancing volume is a noisy measurement. Between MU scans at most 30 days apart, during which the tumour should
change little, it changed by a median factor of 2.6 with the supplied masks and 2.2 with Swin UNETR (54 pairs), a
difference whose interval excludes zero. At LUMIERE, where only 52 such pairs exist, the supplied masks were the more
repeatable (2.3 against 3.1), with an interval including zero. Swin UNETR agreed with the supplied masks at a median
enhancing tumour Dice of 0.73 to 0.80 across the centres (Table~\ref{tab:seg}). Many UCSF-ALPTDG scans hold little or

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:4}

% Sources: Sections 3.1--3.3; refs booth2022, shi2025, christodoulou2026, gomaa2025.
Keeping the scores separate showed where a progression model's ranking comes from: the time since radiotherapy at the
development centre, the enhancing tumour elsewhere, and about 0.68 from the images within a time window. A single AUC
measures how well a score ranks scans, and the separate scores show what that ranking rests on. At MU-Glioma-Post the
clinical-history and combined models ranked scans well because the cohort's follow-up schedule ties the time of a scan
to its label, and the time since treatment was the strongest input in an earlier external comparison
\citep{gomaa2025}. The time since radiotherapy is information a clinician already uses, and PRISM keeps it in its own
score so that the reader can see how much of a combined score it supplies. At LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG timing alone ranked
scans far less well, and the combined model kept its ranking because the enhancing volume and its change
carried the signal there. Most models proposed for this decision score the image alone and are tested on one split
\citep{booth2022,shi2025}. On the same data, the published radiomics pipeline ranked scans below PRISM's 27
measurements, and its selected features ranked no better than the enhancing volume, which suggests that in cohorts of
this size a few measurements with a known meaning hold the image signal that thousands of features dilute.

% Sources: Section 3.3; METHODS_RESULTS.md E5; refs hoebel2024, deverdier2024.
The same separation explains why the combined score survived a change of segmenter while the imaging score did not.
The combined and time-and-enhancement models rest on the time window and the enhancing volume, and the enhancing
volume ranks scans similarly whichever network outlines it. The imaging model rests on finer measurements of shape,
intensity and change, which depend on where the outline falls, and at MU it lost ground with Swin UNETR although that
network measured the enhancing volume more repeatably between close scans. Outlines of the same glioma differ between
experts \citep{hoebel2024}, and post-treatment scans are harder to segment than scans before surgery
\citep{deverdier2024}, so an image measurement is defined by its segmenter as much as by the tumour. PRISM records
which masks each measurement came from, and its report calls the volumes indicative. \todo{S4 PENDING: one sentence on
whether the post-treatment BraTS 2024 model narrows the MU imaging loss, written to match Section~\ref{sec:3.3}.}

% Sources: Sections 2.3 and 3.2; refs kallus2019, janes2008, beaulieujones2021.
Studies of progression models could report three things beside the AUC. The first is the gain over a
time-and-enhancement model, which shows what a score adds to the evidence a clinician already holds
\citep{beaulieujones2021}. The second is the AUC within time windows, the split of pairs within and across windows
\citep{kallus2019}, a pair weighted analogue of the covariate adjusted AUC \citep{janes2008}. The third is how the
result changes when another segmenter outlines the tumour. None of these needs new images, only the time of each scan
relative to treatment and a second public segmenter, and the first two need dates that most public glioma cohorts
record only in part.

% Sources: Introduction (jabal2026, herasrivera2026); Section 3.4; refs pham2026, mansour2026, mehrtens2023.
PRISM differs from the systems closest to it in where the clinical history enters. A system that chains language
models over clinical notes to assign a BT-RADS category \citep{jabal2026} reads the history as text, and a system that
phrases automated measurements with a language model \citep{herasrivera2026} reports the images. PRISM enters the
history as data in the record, so every sentence of its report can be checked against a value. Other rule-based gates
for clinical text forbid numbers altogether \citep{pham2026} or trace numbers to a measurement file without measuring
the check \citep{mansour2026}. Here the verifier rejected every planted value placed under the wrong region or score,
including those written by a model that had not seen its rules, and no correct paraphrase, while direction words
outside its fixed list remain for a wider list and a fresh set of independent errors. The conformal sets met their
average coverage at every centre on point estimates, but calibration within groups mainly redistributed coverage in cohorts of this
size, as expected under shift \citep{mehrtens2023}. Calibration pooled across centres would extend coverage to the
smallest groups, and a centre adopting PRISM would calibrate on its own scans.

% Sources: NUMBERS.md 10 (limits); Sections 2.1, 2.3; REPORT_V3_1.md Limits.
This study has limitations. PRISM was developed on one public cohort and tested at two external centres with 69 and
178 evaluation scans, so the external intervals are wide. The label is progression as each centre documented it, not
a pathological or expert consensus reference, and at LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG it partly reflects the images the scores
use. The supplied masks and both segmenters give reference outlines that are not ground truth. At UCSF-ALPTDG the
radiotherapy window was approximated from its start, and the time-and-enhancement model was applied there only
outside the planned comparison. In the first 12 weeks after radiotherapy MU holds few scans with progression, and the report
marks such scans as having few comparable cases. No cohort supplied perfusion or diffusion imaging, which have
separated progression from treatment effects in other studies \citep{taylor2022}. No clinician has read the reports,
and the checks establish agreement with the record, not clinical value. Pooling rare situations across further
cohorts, such as Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM, would sharpen these estimates, and a reader study of PRISM's reports is left
to future work.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

Without retraining, PRISM kept ranking scans at two external centres, and keeping its evidence separate showed what
that ranking rests on. The time since radiotherapy carried the ranking at the development centre and the enhancing
tumour at the external ones, and among scans taken at a similar time the images ranked progression at about 0.68.
PRISM shows each kind of evidence in its own score, and its local report states them, checked against the scan's
record.
