\begin{abstract}
\flag{[PROPOSED, awaiting approval]} Treatment effects can mimic glioma progression on MRI; most surveillance
datasets lack treatment dates, centres document progression differently and the hardest scans are few. We built
PRISM, which scores each scan through separate treatment-timeline and image pathways, so the clinician sees what each
score uses, and adds a confidence and a report from a local language model. On MU-Glioma-Post both pathways detected
the scan where progression was called; the timeline pathway reached an AUC of 0.82. The combined model, applied
unchanged, reached 0.77 on LUMIERE; on UCSF-ALPTDG, single-scan measurements reached 0.80 and the image pathway, with
change between scans, 0.73, against 0.86 when trained there. An audit found the models used mainly the timeline at
MU-Glioma-Post and enhancing tumour volume at UCSF-ALPTDG. Conformal sets for the combined score met their planned
coverage on point estimates at every centre, and after one revision every report passed a check against its record.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Introduction 1); refs taal2008, brandes2008.
After surgery and chemoradiotherapy, patients with glioma are followed with MRI every few months, and at each scan
the team must decide whether the tumour has progressed. The decision is hardest in the months after radiotherapy,
when treatment can increase contrast enhancement without tumour growth. This pseudoprogression is most common in the
first weeks after chemoradiotherapy and more frequent when the promoter of MGMT, a DNA-repair gene, is methylated
\citep{taal2008,brandes2008}. In routine care, progression is therefore documented from the images together with
symptoms and the course of treatment, and the date on which it is recorded depends on when the patient was scanned,
so a model trained on such records can learn from the schedule as well as from the images.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine C1--C5); SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md rows 1.7, 6.3; refs an2021, varoquaux2018, yu2022.
Surveillance data set five challenges for a model of this task. First, most public
surveillance datasets hold images but few treatment dates. Second, centres document progression differently, from
dated clinical decisions to expert ratings of each scan, so a model can score well at two centres for different
reasons. Third, cohorts are small, and one test split can move the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve
(AUC) by more than a change of model
\citep{an2021,varoquaux2018}. Fourth, the situations in which the decision is hardest, early after radiotherapy or
when enhancement grows, hold few cases in any one cohort. Fifth, segmented volumes are noisy, so the clinician
needs to see what was measured; and patient records often cannot leave the hospital, so the report has to be written
there. Repeated cross-validation addresses the third challenge and external tests the second, which 3 of 20 studies in
a recent systematic review of automated response assessment included \citep{shi2025}; we planned two.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (question).
We asked whether a system could give the clinician, at each post-treatment scan, the image evidence and the
treatment-timeline evidence separately, transfer to other centres without refitting, and say how far each can be
trusted there. We also wanted it to explain each score in a short report written inside the hospital.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim); NUMBERS.md sections 3--7.
We present PRISM, a modular system that scores each post-treatment scan through two pathways, one from clinical data
and the treatment timeline and one from measurements of the segmented tumour, and shows a combined score beside
them. The confidence has three parts, a conformal prediction set, a statement of whether the pathways agree and a
reliability card; a local language model writes the report, and a checker tests it against the scan's record.

We developed PRISM on MU-Glioma-Post (MU) and tested it on LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG (UCSF) with models fitted once
and external tests planned in advance. On MU both pathways detected the scan at which progression was documented;
the models fitted there transferred to LUMIERE, and the image measurements to UCSF. When we audited the scores,
the models at MU used mainly the patient's position in treatment, and at UCSF mainly the enhancing tumour volume. The
conformal sets for the combined score met their pre-specified coverage on point estimates at all three centres, at
UCSF exactly at the threshold, and after one revision of the prompt every report passed a check against its record.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:2}

\subsection{PRISM}\label{sec:2.1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine table); SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 1.
PRISM handles each scan in five modules (Figure~\ref{fig:1}). The timeline pathway and the image pathway keep the two
kinds of evidence apart, so that the image pathway can run where no treatment dates exist and the audit can show which
evidence a score used. Segmentation masks supply the measurements and the images the clinician sees. The confidence
gives the clinician a set of plausible outcomes and how many scans like this one the system has been tested on; the
report is written inside the hospital, so the record never leaves it. We meet the third challenge, small cohorts, in
how we evaluate PRISM (Section~\ref{sec:2.3}).

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, B1, B0; External test, Tbase); UCSF_RESULTS.md deviation 3; NUMBERS.md 2.1.
The timeline pathway scores a scan from clinical data and the patient's position in treatment. On MU its inputs are
age, sex, IDH mutation and MGMT promoter methylation, which alone form the clinical-only reference model, together
with the days since diagnosis and since surgery, the start, end and dose of radiotherapy, chemotherapy and the visit
index, each taken as of the scan day. Where a cohort records no treatment dates, PRISM uses a reduced timeline that
keeps the clinical inputs, the days since surgery and a flag for the first weeks after radiotherapy. The flag comes
from the rater's note at LUMIERE and is approximated from the start of radiotherapy at UCSF. Both pathways are ridge
logistic regressions; scaling and any selection were fitted on the training data only.

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (EXPLORATORY pure imaging); UCSF_IMAGING.md; SPLIT_VARIANCE.md; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md Deviations 1.
The image pathway scores a scan from simple measurements of the segmented tumour. On MU it uses 27 inputs: the
volumes, shape and relative intensity of the segmented compartments at the scan, their change from the previous
scan, and the enhancing and total lesion volumes relative to the smallest in the patient's history
(\todo{Supplementary section: image pathway inputs and their harmonisation at LUMIERE and UCSF}). It holds no dates,
intervals or treatment flags. A single-scan version keeps only the 19 measurements of the current scan. We defined
these inputs after finding that the planned imaging model contained three inputs that encode the scan schedule
(Section~\ref{sec:3.3}). Such inputs belong to the timeline pathway, because they carry when a scan was taken, which
the timeline already holds, and the image pathway has to run where no dates exist.

The combined score is one ridge model fitted on both sets of inputs and is shown beside the two pathways. At LUMIERE
and UCSF its inputs are the reduced timeline, the harmonised volumes and their change, and the interval since the
previous scan, its one schedule input.

% Sources: SEG_FIGURE.md; UCSF_IMAGING.md; NUMBERS.md mismatch 6; refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
The measurements come from the segmentation masks supplied with each cohort: the automatic masks released with MU
\citep{mahmoud2025}, the DeepBraTumIA masks of LUMIERE \citep{suter2022} and the masks provided with UCSF
\citep{fields2024}. PRISM renders the report's figure from the same masks by code, and the language model receives
only the record, not the image.

% Sources: SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 2; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md Specification and "cost of the split"; RELIABILITY_CARD.md;
% refs angelopoulos2023, mitchell2019.
Each scan's confidence has three parts, a conformal set, a pathway-agreement statement and a reliability card
\citep{mitchell2019}. The conformal set is a patient-level split conformal prediction set at the 80\% level
\citep{angelopoulos2023}, built to contain the true outcome for at least 80\% of scans; it holds one outcome when
the score is clear and both when it is not. It comes from the combined model refitted on 70\% of the MU patients and calibrated on the remaining 30\%; on
MU this split is made inside each training fold, and for the external cohorts one MU-calibrated set was applied
unchanged, which assumes that their scans are exchangeable with MU's. Because of the refit, the set's model ranks scans slightly differently from the displayed combined score
(\todo{Supplementary section: system results}). The two pathways agree when both scores lie on the same side of that
pathway's median score over the cohort's evaluation scans; the rule uses scores only, never labels, but as
evaluated it needs the scores of a whole cohort (\todo{round 4: agreement rule with medians fixed on MU and applied
unchanged externally; NUMBERS.md 12A}). The reliability
card looks up the scan's situation, its phase of care crossed with the change in enhancing tumour since the previous
scan, in a table of results measured in each cohort. It states the situation's tier: tested, with AUCs and
intervals, when the situation holds at least 10 progression and 10 non-progression scans; few cases, with counts
only, when it holds fewer; and not tested when it holds none.

% Sources: REPORT_V3.md (Design, Checker v3); REPORT_V3_1.md; Supplementary S19.
A local language model writes the report from the scan's record, and a deterministic checker tests the report
against that record. The model (Qwen3.8-27B) runs offline at temperature 0 and receives only the record; the checker
rejects any number, flag or confidence statement that is not in the record, any omission of required content, and any
wording that offers a diagnosis, advice or a probability (Supplementary Section~S19).

\subsection{Cohorts and the task}\label{sec:2.2}

% Sources: TABLE1_COHORT.md; UCSF_DATA.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; refs mahmoud2025, suter2022, fields2024.
PRISM was developed on MU and tested on LUMIERE and UCSF, in each case on grade 4 glioma as the primary population,
with models fitted once on MU and applied unchanged, which we call locked (Table~\ref{tab:1}). MU records the dates of surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy \citep{mahmoud2025}. LUMIERE records no treatment dates, and the rater notes whether the scan falls within three months of radiotherapy \citep{suter2022}. UCSF
holds one pair of consecutive post-treatment scans per patient, with the start of radiotherapy \citep{fields2024}.
Progression is documented in each cohort's own record (Table~\ref{tab:1}): as a dated clinical decision at MU, as an
expert rating of response at each scan at LUMIERE and as a progression date supplied with the dataset at UCSF. Because the first scan of each pair has no earlier scan, we evaluated the second scan
only.

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, Cohort, External test); UCSF_PLAN.md; INTENDED_USE.md.
The task is to decide, at each scan, whether this is the scan at which progression is documented. On MU a positive is the first
scan on or after the documented progression date, and a negative is a scan followed by at least 120 days without
progression. At LUMIERE a positive is the first scan rated as progressive disease, and a negative is an earlier scan
rated stable or responding. At UCSF a positive is a second scan with progression dated within 30 days of it and
nearer to it than to the first scan; a negative is a second scan with progression at least 120 days later, or with at
least 120 days of follow-up without progression. On MU the intended use is scans from the start of radiotherapy
onwards, and unless stated MU results are for this population; LUMIERE and UCSF are evaluated on all scans in their
detection sets. We divide the intended use into phases of care: during radiotherapy, early after radiotherapy (up to
84 days, 12 weeks, after its end) and later. Predicting progression within 120 days of a scan is reported as a
secondary task.

\subsection{Evaluation}\label{sec:2.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan); SPLIT_VARIANCE.md (Results table 1); Supplementary S8 and Figure S2.
% OUTLINE open question (split variance): settled here as one paragraph + supplementary pointer (council round 1
% recommendation, pending author). UCSF own-split result moved to RESERVE.md.
On MU, every model was evaluated by repeated patient-grouped cross-validation. We used five repeats of five folds,
with all scans of a patient in the same fold, and report the mean over repeats of the pooled held-out AUC with a 95\%
confidence interval (CI) from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap draws. In the analyses of Sections~\ref{sec:3.1}
to~\ref{sec:3.4} no patient appears in more than one of fitting, calibration and testing. We chose this design because in cohorts of this size one split can move an
AUC by more than a change of model. We saw this in our first evaluation, in which a radiomics model reached an AUC of
0.80 on one test split and averaged 0.72 when re-selected and re-tested over 500 splits of the same design
(Supplementary Section~S8 and Figure~S2).

% Sources: UCSF_PLAN.md (0e7c9fe); SYSTEM_PLAN.md (e7cba80); REPORT_V3_1_PLAN.md (4f7e7b9); UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A, B.
For the external tests, each model was fitted once on MU and applied once, under plans committed before any external
label was read. The locked models were fitted on all grade 4 detection scans of MU, from every phase of care. The
UCSF plan named all MU detection scans; fitting on all gliomas gave the same AUC at LUMIERE, and at UCSF the
all-glioma models were tested on all-glioma scans (\todo{Supplementary section: deviations from the plans}).
External AUCs have 95\% CIs from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap draws. As a reference, we also fitted each
UCSF model on UCSF itself by repeated cross-validation. Table~\ref{tab:2} lists the questions fixed in advance and
their answers.

% Sources: NUMBERS.md (status column); OUTLINE.md decision 8.
Some analyses were not planned, and we list them here once. They are the definition of the image pathway on MU
(its use at UCSF and in the system tests was planned); the three-step comparison of image models; the clinical-only,
image-only and timeline-only models at LUMIERE; the single-scan comparison with models trained at UCSF; the audits of
what each score used; the agreement rule and the reliability card, both defined post hoc, after the cohort results
had been seen; a stricter flag rule; the second evaluation of the reports, which followed a failure seen in the
first; and the comparison of report models (\todo{round 4: add the new exploratory analyses: MU-fixed agreement,
class-conditional coverage, enhancing-volume baselines, operating points, calibration}). In the Results each is marked
``(exploratory)'' once, where it first appears in a paragraph, and the figure and table notes mark exploratory panels
and rows. Comparisons within a phase of care or within an enhancement category are diagnostics of what a score
depends on; none of them is a decision rule.

\section{Results}\label{sec:3}

\subsection{Both pathways detect the progression scan on MU}\label{sec:3.1}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Primary table; EXPLORATORY pure imaging, images + clinical); INTENDED_USE.md; NUMBERS.md 3.
In the intended use on MU (146 scans; Table~\ref{tab:1}), the image pathway (exploratory) detected the progression
scan with an AUC of 0.71 (95\% CI 0.63--0.78), and the timeline pathway with 0.82 (0.75--0.90), mostly by separating
phases of care, within which it did no better than the images (Section~\ref{sec:3.3}). Clinical data alone, without the timeline, scored 0.53, close to chance. The combined score
also reached 0.82; on MU the images did not raise the timeline's AUC, and the planned imaging model fell short of the
pre-specified gain of 0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:2}). Predicting progression within 120 days of a scan was harder;
the model reached 0.60 (0.52--0.69) in the intended use (Supplementary Section~S23). In one secondary evaluation
set, all scans of all gliomas, the planned model did gain 0.04 (Table~\ref{tab:2}), which may reflect image inputs
that encode the scan schedule (Section~\ref{sec:3.3}). A timeline pathway needs treatment dates, which the two
external cohorts largely lack, so we next tested how each pathway transfers to them.

\subsection{The models transfer to two external centres without refitting}\label{sec:3.2}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (External test; EXPLORATORY clinical alone on LUMIERE); UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A; NUMBERS.md 4.
The locked combined model reached 0.77 (0.68--0.86) on LUMIERE (Figure~\ref{fig:2}) and met its planned transfer
rule (Table~\ref{tab:2}). At LUMIERE the combined score was above either pathway alone, images 0.65 and the reduced
timeline 0.69 (exploratory; the gain over the timeline alone had an interval that included zero). Clinical data alone
stayed close to chance in all three cohorts (Figure~\ref{fig:2}). \todo{round 4: enhancing tumour volume alone as a
reference at MU and LUMIERE (UCSF in Section 3.3), as a bar in Figure 2 (NUMBERS.md 12C); sensitivity and
specificity at one MU-fixed threshold (12D) and calibration externally (12F), one sentence here and a supplementary
table.}

% Sources: UCSF_RESULTS.md (Part A, Part B, Q1--Q3, contrasts); NUMBERS.md mismatch 1. Council ruling 1.
At UCSF, both planned image models transferred. Measurements of the single scan reached 0.80 (0.69--0.89), and the
image pathway, which adds change from the previous scan, reached 0.73 (0.60--0.84), meeting its planned transfer rule
(Table~\ref{tab:2}). Training at UCSF raised the image pathway to 0.86 (difference 0.14, 0.03--0.26), while the
single-scan model scored about the same when trained there (exploratory). The change inputs therefore transferred
less well than the measurements of the scan itself. The reduced timeline, whose early-post-radiotherapy flag UCSF
can only approximate (Section~\ref{sec:2.1}), transferred worst of the pathways, at 0.56. The combined score at UCSF
scored above the image pathway (Figure~\ref{fig:2}), partly through the interval between the two scans, which was
shorter before progression. Because similar AUCs at different centres can come from different
evidence, we audited what each score used.

\subsection{What each score rests on depends on the cohort}\label{sec:3.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Cohort by phase; within phase; EXPLORATORY images + clinical); STORY.md gap 3.
The audit (exploratory) shows that at MU the scores depend mainly on where the patient is in treatment, and at UCSF
on how much the tumour enhances (Figure~\ref{fig:3}). At MU the scan schedule alone separates most
progression scans from most non-progression scans, because most progression scans came more than 12 weeks after
radiotherapy and most non-progression scans came within those 12 weeks (Table~\ref{tab:1}). Across phases of care, adding the timeline to clinical data and images gained 0.11
(0.04--0.20); within a phase it gained $-$0.02 ($-$0.13 to $+$0.11) (Figure~\ref{fig:3}A). Within a phase the images
also gave about as much evidence as the timeline, 0.68 (0.58--0.77) against 0.64 (0.51--0.78).

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (EXPLORATORY pure imaging; I as run); NUMBERS.md 5 ladder and mismatch 8 (the middle
% step has a verified point difference, +0.050, but no verified CI; none is quoted).
We built the image model on MU in three steps (exploratory) to see which inputs raised its AUC (Figure~\ref{fig:3}C). Adding
change from earlier scans to the current scan's measurements gave the image pathway, 0.05 higher
(\todo{round 4: bootstrap CI for this step; NUMBERS.md 12E}), and adding the three schedule inputs of the planned imaging model (the
interval since the previous scan, a flag for whether there was one and the smallest volume counted from the end of
radiotherapy) raised the AUC to 0.81 across phases of care but not within them ($-$0.01, $-$0.06 to $+$0.04).

% Sources: UCSF_AUDIT.md sections 3--6, EXPLORATORY intervals table and Overall verdict; NUMBERS.md 5. Council ruling 2.
% DETECTION_DEEP.md; UCSF_RESULTS.md Part C (Q4); NUMBERS.md mismatch 6. Council ruling 6; resegmentation in RESERVE.md.
At UCSF the progression calls follow enhancement closely. Enhancing tumour volume alone, the simplest baseline, gave
an AUC of 0.88 (0.79--0.95), higher than the point estimates of the locked image models; UCSF rated enhancement as
increased at 27 of the 29 progression scans, and progression was dated a median of 7 days after the evaluated scan
(Figure~\ref{fig:3}B). Among the 37 scans rated as showing increased enhancement, the single-scan image model reached
0.65 (0.44--0.84), so beyond that rating the measurements added little. At UCSF an AUC therefore largely measures
agreement with a progression call made from enhancement at the same scan, which the image pathway measures directly.
Deep networks fine-tuned to score UCSF scans directly learned from the images, above a permuted-label control
(Table~\ref{tab:2}), and their scores tracked enhancing volume (correlation about 0.8; \todo{Supplementary section:
deep networks at MU and UCSF}). Because the evidence behind a score differs by cohort and by situation, PRISM attaches
a confidence to each score.

\subsection{The conformal sets of the combined score meet their coverage threshold on point estimates at all three centres}\label{sec:3.4}

% Sources: SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S1 and Specification; NUMBERS.md 6 and mismatch 13. Council ruling 4.
At the 80\% level, the conformal sets of the combined score covered between 0.78 and 0.87 of scans in the three
cohorts, meeting on point estimates the threshold of 0.78 fixed in advance, a tolerance of 0.02 below the
nominal level (Figure~\ref{fig:4}A). Most sets were informative, holding a single outcome for 62\% to 75\% of scans. At UCSF the
point estimate sat at the threshold and its interval reached below it (0.68--0.87). Coverage fell short for the
image pathway alone at LUMIERE and the timeline alone at UCSF, and it was uneven across situations within each
cohort (\todo{Supplementary section: system results}). \todo{round 4: coverage by true class (progression and
non-progression) in each cohort, with the single-outcome share by class; NUMBERS.md 12B.}

% Sources: SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S2, S3 and EXPLORATORY reduced design (3d20c41); NUMBERS.md mismatches 4 and 5.
On MU, the warning that the two pathways disagree (exploratory) picked out the less accurate scans. The pathways disagreed on 40\% of MU scans; the combined score's AUC was 0.90 where they agreed and
0.59 where they disagreed, and setting the disagreeing scans aside gained more than removing the same number matched
for phase of care (Table~\ref{tab:2}; Figure~\ref{fig:4}B). At LUMIERE and UCSF disagreement did not pick out the
less accurate scans. \todo{round 4: restate with the MU-fixed agreement rule (NUMBERS.md 12A), whose LUMIERE
result differs.} A stricter rule chosen after these results, flagging a scan only when the pathways disagree and
its set holds both outcomes, is described in the \todo{Supplementary section: system results}.

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (Reading, lookup table); SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S4; NUMBERS.md mismatch 14 and section 6.
The reliability card (exploratory) states, for each scan, how many tested cases its situation holds, and this
differs by cohort. Across the three cohorts only four situations held at least 10 progression and 10
non-progression scans, one at MU and three at LUMIERE; 35 of the 249 MU scans fell in a tested situation, most
LUMIERE scans did, and every situation at UCSF had few cases (Figure~\ref{fig:4}C). At MU no situation in the first
12 weeks after radiotherapy held more than three progression scans. Tested situations had lower error than few-case
situations at LUMIERE, with overlapping intervals (Table~\ref{tab:2}); at MU the order was reversed, with a Brier
score of 0.222 in tested situations against 0.147 in few-case ones, because the few-case scans there were mostly
non-progression scans early after radiotherapy, which the models score easily. The clinician
reads these statements in the report, which we evaluated next.

\subsection{Every report passes a check of its numbers, flags and wording against the record}\label{sec:3.5}

% Sources: REPORT_V3_1.md (S5--S7, ladder); REPORT_V3.md (S5 failure); NUMBERS.md 7 and mismatches 9, 10. Council ruling 5.
Every evaluation report written by the local model passed a lexical check of its numbers, flags and wording
against its record (114, 162 and 130 reports at MU, LUMIERE and UCSF; Figure~\ref{fig:5}); the check cannot judge
emphasis, order or what a clinician would infer from the wording. These counts come from a second evaluation
(exploratory), after one revision of the prompt; its records exclude the 10 LUMIERE and 10 UCSF patients used to
develop the revision and are otherwise those of the first evaluation. The first evaluation had failed at LUMIERE (0 of 178) because one sentence quoted a
number that was in no cited field; we corrected the prompt and left the checker unchanged. The reports stated the
evidence tier wherever the card had few or no cases, and followed a changed field in 28 of 30 perturbed records
(Table~\ref{tab:2}). The smallest open-weight model that passed in every cohort had 27 billion parameters and ran on
one GPU (\todo{Supplementary section: second report evaluation and model comparison}). The report therefore gives the
clinician the volumes, the two scores and the confidence exactly as the record holds them.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:4}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 1); NUMBERS.md 3--6.
PRISM gives the clinician separate image and treatment-timeline evidence at each scan, transfers to two external
centres without refitting, and states how far each score has been tested in the scan's situation, which for most
scans at MU and UCSF is little. At the development centre the
timeline provided most of the evidence, largely because of when patients were scanned, and within a phase of care
the images provided about as much; at UCSF the image measurements provided the score, and training there raised it
further; the locked results therefore measure transfer, and a model fitted at the centre can do better. Because the pathways were kept
apart, we could measure these differences at each centre, and each report states them through the confidence and the
card.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 2); SYSTEM_PLAN.md. Council ruling 7 (combined score definition).
Because its modules are separate, PRISM runs where data are incomplete. The image pathway needs only the scans and
their masks, which is what most public surveillance datasets hold; at LUMIERE and UCSF it ran without treatment
dates. Where dates exist, the timeline is added as a second pathway, and the combined score, one ridge model on both
sets of inputs, is shown beside both without changing the image model. A new cohort adds rows to the reliability card,
so the tiers change as evidence accumulates and no confidence model has to be refitted.

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (example card); NUMBERS.md 10 (scenario).
Consider, as an illustration, a scan eight weeks after radiotherapy in which the enhancing tumour has grown. At MU, 1
of the 9 scans in this situation on the card (all grade 4 detection scans) was progression (exploratory). The timeline pathway scores such a scan
low, because most scans at this point in treatment are not progression, and the image pathway scores it high, because
enhancement has grown. PRISM's report would show both scores, state that they disagree, give a conformal set that
holds both outcomes and record that the card has few cases for this situation. The clinician then sees that the
system has no measured basis for a call at this scan, in the weeks when pseudoprogression is most common, and decides
with information PRISM does not use, such as symptoms, steroid dose and the radiotherapy plan.

% Sources: SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md (1.3, 1.13, 2.1, 3.1, Finding); COMPARABLE_RESULTS.md; RESERVE.md (Christodoulou).
Each of PRISM's components has been described before, and we are not aware of a system that combines them.
Automatic segmentation of post-treatment glioma with measurement of change between scans is established
\citep{rudie2022}, conformal prediction has been applied to glioma segmentation \citep{elyassirad2025}, clinical and
imaging data have been fused to separate pseudoprogression from progression \citep{gomaa2025}, and model cards report
performance by subgroup \citep{mitchell2019}. The closest system we found combines treatment variables extracted by a
language model with tumour volumes to assign a structured follow-up category at one centre, without an uncertainty
attached to the output \citep{jabal2026}. On MU, \citet{christodoulou2026} predicted a label assigned at the patient
level with a radiomics model, a different question from detection at the scan. On LUMIERE, \todo{cite Tikhonov 2025,
arXiv:2509.06511; full reference not yet verified} classified each scan into four response categories, and
\citet{chen2026} predicted recurrence risk from preoperative and postoperative MRI across centres; both address other
labels.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 5); NUMBERS.md 10; UCSF_AUDIT.md (caveat sentence); REPORT_V3_1.md Limits.
PRISM was developed at one centre, with 126 grade 4 patients (80 in the intended-use detection set), and the two
external tests are its only evidence of transfer. The label is progression as each centre
documented it; no scan was re-read for this study, and at UCSF the label agrees closely with an enhancement call at
the same scan. Subgroups are
small, and UCSF contributes 29 progression scans. The coverage threshold was met on point estimates, and at UCSF the interval reached below it; the
disagreement warning picked out less accurate scans only at MU. The exploratory analyses listed in Section~\ref{sec:2.3}
need testing on new data. The report checker is lexical, and no
clinician has yet read the reports. PRISM forecast progression within 120 days less well than it detected it
at the scan, so we present it as a detection system and report the forecast as a secondary task.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 6); RELATED_WORK.md section 3 (Burdenko, UCSD-PTGBM).
The next step is to pool the rare situations across cohorts and train on them. Early progression after radiotherapy
and growing enhancement without progression are rare at any one centre. The Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM datasets add
radiotherapy detail and advanced sequences, and each new cohort adds cases to the card's thin situations, which is
where models fine-tuned on these long-tail cases could be tested. A reader study in which clinicians use PRISM's
reports at the scan would show whether separate evidence and a stated confidence change their decisions.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim).
PRISM keeps image and treatment-timeline evidence apart, and its models held at two other centres without
refitting. In our three cohorts the images and the timeline each contributed, in different proportions at each
centre, and PRISM shows the clinician both scores at every scan, with how far each has been tested there.
