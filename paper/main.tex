\begin{abstract}
Surveillance MRI after treatment for glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas must be interpreted with prior imaging
and the treatment course. A progression score alone does not show the clinician the evidence that produced it or what
it leaves out. We built PRISM, a modular system that combines image-derived measurements, treatment context, separate
image and timeline pathways, uncertainty and provenance in a locally generated, checked report. On MU-Glioma-Post,
the timeline and image pathways reached AUCs of 0.82 and 0.71. Applied unchanged, the combined score reached 0.77 on
LUMIERE; at UCSF-ALPTDG, single-scan image measurements reached 0.80 and the image pathway 0.73. The evidence behind
the scores differed by cohort: treatment timing at MU-Glioma-Post and enhancing volume at UCSF-ALPTDG. In a second,
cross-cohort report evaluation, 406 of 406 reports passed a lexical check against their structured records. PRISM is
designed to make evidence inspectable, not to replace clinical judgement; its effect on clinical decisions needs a
reader study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Introduction 1); refs taal2008, brandes2008.
Glioblastoma and other aggressive gliomas are followed with repeated MRI after surgery and chemoradiotherapy, and
each scan is read against earlier scans and the course of treatment. The call is hardest soon after radiotherapy,
when treatment can increase contrast enhancement without tumour growth. This pseudoprogression is most common in the
first weeks after chemoradiotherapy, and more so when the promoter of the DNA-repair gene MGMT is methylated
\citep{taal2008,brandes2008}.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (spine C1--C5); refs an2021, varoquaux2018, shi2025.
A model's probability alone does not show the treatment context, the
measurements behind it, its uncertainty or what it left out. Surveillance data make these gaps hard to close, because
public datasets often hold images but few treatment dates, centres document progression differently, and the hardest
situations are rare. Only 3 of 20 studies in a recent review of automated response assessment tested
their models externally \citep{shi2025}.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (question, claim); NUMBERS.md sections 3--7.
We built PRISM to give the clinician, at each post-treatment scan, the image evidence and the treatment-timeline
evidence separately, with a statement of how far each has been tested, in a report written inside the hospital. We
developed it on MU-Glioma-Post (MU) and applied its models unchanged to LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG (UCSF). The models
ranked scans at both external centres, but what drove a score differed by cohort, mostly the position in treatment at MU and enhancement at UCSF.

The timeline pathway scores a scan from clinical data and the patient's position in treatment, both taken as of the
scan day. Where a cohort lacks treatment dates, a reduced timeline keeps the inputs it has
(Table~\ref{tab:models}), with the flag for the first weeks after radiotherapy taken from the rater's note at LUMIERE
and approximated at UCSF. The image pathway scores the scan from 27 measurements of the segmented
tumour and its change (Table~\ref{tab:models}; \todo{Supplementary section: image pathway inputs and their
harmonisation at LUMIERE and UCSF}) and uses no dates or intervals. The measurements come from the masks released with each cohort
\citep{mahmoud2025,suter2022,fields2024}.

% Sources: SYSTEM_PLAN.md section 2; SYSTEM_RESULTS.md Specification; prism/analyses/system.py (derive: medians over
% each cohort's evaluation scans); NUMBERS.md 12A (MU-fixed medians, EXPLORATORY); RELIABILITY_CARD.md;
% refs angelopoulos2023, mitchell2019.
The confidence has three parts. A patient-level split conformal set at the 80\% level \citep{angelopoulos2023}
holds one outcome when the score is clear and both when it is not. It comes from the combined model refitted on 70\%
of the MU patients and calibrated on the other 30\% (within each training fold on MU) and is applied unchanged
elsewhere, which assumes that external scans are exchangeable
with MU's (\todo{Supplementary section: system results}). The two pathways agree when both scores lie on the same
side of their medians over the cohort's evaluation scans; because those medians need a batch of scans, we also tested
medians fixed on MU, which a single scan can use (exploratory). The reliability card\expl{} \citep{mitchell2019} looks up the scan's situation, its phase of
care crossed with the change in enhancing tumour, in a record of that cohort's held-out results, which includes the scan
itself and so describes it slightly optimistically. A situation with at least 10 scans of each outcome is tested, and the card gives its AUCs; with fewer it has few
cases, and the card gives counts only; with none it is not tested.

% Sources: REPORT_V3.md (Design, Checker v3); REPORT_V3_1.md; Supplementary S19.
A local language model (Qwen3.8-27B, temperature 0) writes the report from the record alone, so patient data do not
leave the hospital. A
deterministic checker rejects any number, flag or confidence statement that is not in the record, any missing
required content, and any wording that offers a diagnosis, advice or a probability (Supplementary Section~S19).

\subsection{Cohorts and the task}\label{sec:2.2}

% Sources: TABLE1_COHORT.md; UCSF_DATA.md; UCSF_PLAN.md; DETECTION_TASK.md (Plan, Cohort, External test); INTENDED_USE.md.
Grade 4 glioma is the primary population in all three cohorts (Table~\ref{tab:1}); we fitted each model once on MU
and applied it unchanged, or locked, to the others. The task is to decide at each scan whether it is the scan
at which progression was documented, as each cohort documents it. On MU a positive is the first scan on or after the
documented progression date, and a negative is a scan followed by at least 120 days without progression. At LUMIERE
a positive is the first scan rated as progressive disease, and a negative is an earlier scan rated stable or
responding. UCSF holds one pair of consecutive scans per patient, and we evaluated the second: a positive is a second scan whose
progression date lies within 30 days of it and closer to it than to the first scan, and a negative is followed by at least 120
days without progression. On MU the intended use is scans from the start of radiotherapy, divided into three phases
of care: during radiotherapy, early after it (up to 12 weeks after its end) and later; MU results are for the intended
use unless stated. Predicting progression within 120 days of a scan is a secondary task.

\subsection{The timeline carries most of the evidence at the development centre}\label{sec:3.1}

In the intended use on MU (146 scans), the timeline pathway detected the progression scan with an AUC of 0.82 (95\%
CI 0.75--0.90) and the image pathway with 0.71 (0.63--0.78)\expl; clinical data alone scored 0.53

planned model adding image measurements and three schedule inputs to the timeline fell short of its target gain of

Forecasting progression within 120 days was harder, at 0.60 (0.52--0.69) in the intended use (Supplementary

\subsection{The models rank scans at two external centres without refitting}\label{sec:3.2}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (External test); UCSF_RESULTS.md Part A, B, Q1--Q3, contrasts; NUMBERS.md 4 and 12C.
The locked combined model, in the reduced form used externally (0.80, 0.73--0.89, on MU by cross-validation), reached 0.77 (0.68--0.86)
at LUMIERE and met its planned transfer rule (Table~\ref{tab:2}), above the image pathway and the reduced timeline\expl{} (Figure~\ref{fig:2}), although its gain over the
timeline had an interval that included zero. At UCSF both planned image models transferred: the single-scan
measurements reached 0.80 (0.69--0.89) and the image pathway 0.73 (0.60--0.84). Locked, the image pathway scored 0.07 below the single-scan model ($-$0.13 to $-$0.01), so its change inputs
transferred less well than the measurements of the scan itself. Trained at UCSF, it gained 0.14 over its locked version (0.03--0.26), while the single-scan model stayed about the same\expl. The reduced timeline scored 0.56 (0.42--0.69) at UCSF, where its radiotherapy flag is approximated, and the locked
combined score reached 0.86 (0.76--0.93), partly from the interval between the two scans, which was shorter before progression\expl{} and so carries the
schedule rather than the tumour. 

% Sources: NUMBERS.md 12D (operating point, EXPLORATORY) and 12F (calibration).
As a probability, the combined score was calibrated in the large at LUMIERE\expl{} but overpredicted progression at UCSF
(observed over expected 0.72, 0.55--0.91), and a threshold at the MU median\expl{} called 90\% of UCSF scans
positive (\todo{Supplementary section: calibration and thresholds}).

\subsection{What carries a score differs by cohort}\label{sec:3.3}

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (Cohort by phase; within phase; EXPLORATORY images + clinical); STORY.md gap 3.
At MU the scores depended mainly on where the patient was in treatment, and at UCSF on how much the tumour
enhanced\expl{} (Figure~\ref{fig:3}). At MU most progression scans came more than 12 weeks after radiotherapy and most
non-progression scans within those 12 weeks (Table~\ref{tab:1}), so the schedule alone could separate most of them. A
within-phase AUC pools only the pairs of a progression and a non-progression scan from the same phase of care; most
such pairs come from the later phase (56 progression and 32 non-progression scans). Adding the timeline to clinical
data and images\expl{} gained 0.11 (0.04--0.20) across phases of care and $-$0.02 ($-$0.13
to $+$0.11) within them (Figure~\ref{fig:3}A). Within a phase, the image pathway scored 0.68 (0.58--0.77) and the
timeline 0.64 (0.51--0.78).

% Sources: DETECTION_TASK.md (EXPLORATORY pure imaging; I as run); NUMBERS.md 5 ladder and 12E.
On MU the image model gained most from inputs that carry timing\expl. Adding change from earlier scans to the
current scan's measurements gave the image pathway, 0.05 higher with an interval that included zero ($-$0.003 to
$+$0.106). Adding the planned model's three schedule inputs (Table~\ref{tab:models}) raised the AUC a further
0.10 (0.05--0.15) across phases of care but not within them ($-$0.01, $-$0.06 to $+$0.04)
(Figure~\ref{fig:3}C).

% Sources: UCSF_AUDIT.md sections 3--6 and Overall verdict; UCSF_RESULTS.md Part C (Q4); NUMBERS.md 5.
At UCSF the progression call followed enhancement\expl, so a model could score well there by tracking it. UCSF rated
enhancement as increased at 27 of the 29 progression scans, and progression was dated a median of 7 days after the
scan (Figure~\ref{fig:3}B). Enhancing volume alone reached 0.88 (0.79--0.95), against 0.62 at MU and 0.66 at LUMIERE
(Figure~\ref{fig:2}), and above the point estimates of the locked image models at UCSF. Among the 37 scans rated as increased, the single-scan
model reached 0.65 (0.44--0.84), so beyond that rating the measurements added little that this sample could detect. Deep
networks fine-tuned on UCSF scans beat a permuted-label control (Table~\ref{tab:2}), and their scores tracked
enhancing volume\expl{} (\todo{Supplementary section: deep networks at MU and UCSF}).

\subsection{Coverage holds on average; disagreement marks the less accurate scans at MU}\label{sec:3.4}

% Sources: SYSTEM_RESULTS.md S1; NUMBERS.md 6 and 12B.

planned threshold of 78\%, and held a single outcome for 62\% to 75\% of scans (Figure~\ref{fig:4}A). At UCSF its interval (68\% to 87\%) reached below the threshold. At the external centres coverage was uneven by
outcome\expl: 77\% (67\% to 87\%) for
progression scans at LUMIERE and 65\% (51\% to 79\%) for non-progression scans at UCSF, against 94\% and 97\% for the
other outcome. Calibrating the sets separately for each outcome did not correct this.

scans that the models score easily (Table~\ref{tab:2}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:4}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 1); NUMBERS.md 3--6.
PRISM's models ranked scans at two other centres without refitting, but what carried a score depended on how each
cohort was scanned and labelled. At MU it was mostly when patients were scanned, and within a phase of care the images
gave about as much as the timeline\expl; at UCSF, enhancing volume alone did at least as well as the locked models,
and 27 of 29 progression scans were rated as showing increased enhancement. An AUC cannot show which evidence a model
used; the separate pathways and the audits of Section~\ref{sec:3.3} can. The risk scale did not transfer to UCSF, so a new centre would recalibrate the score and set any threshold on local scans.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 2); SYSTEM_PLAN.md.
Because its modules are separate, PRISM runs on what a cohort holds: the image pathway needs only scans and masks, which most public datasets have
(and a single-scan version serves where there is no earlier scan); the timeline is added where dates exist; and the reliability card is a lookup table
to which a new cohort adds rows, so a situation's tier can change as evidence accumulates.

% Sources: RELIABILITY_CARD.md (example card); NUMBERS.md 10 (scenario).
Consider a scan eight weeks after radiotherapy in which the enhancing tumour has grown (Figure~\ref{fig:5}A).  The timeline
pathway scores such a scan low, because most scans at this point in treatment are not progression, and the image
pathway scores it high, because enhancement has grown. The report shows both, states that they disagree and that the conformal set holds both outcomes, and records from the card that the situation has few cases (at MU, 1 of 9 such scans was progression\expl). PRISM
therefore has too few tested cases to support a call here, in the weeks when pseudoprogression is most common, and the
clinician decides with information it does not use, such as symptoms, steroid dose and the radiotherapy plan.

% Sources: SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE.md; COMPARABLE_RESULTS.md; RESERVE.md (Christodoulou).
Each component has been described before: longitudinal segmentation with measurement of change
\citep{rudie2022}, conformal prediction for glioma segmentation \citep{elyassirad2025}, fusion of clinical and imaging
data to separate pseudoprogression from progression \citep{gomaa2025} and model cards \citep{mitchell2019}. The closest system assigns a structured follow-up category from treatment variables
extracted by a language model and tumour volumes, at one centre and without an uncertainty \citep{jabal2026}. On these
cohorts, earlier work predicted a patient-level label on MU \citep{christodoulou2026} and response categories on
LUMIERE (\todo{cite Tikhonov 2025, arXiv:2509.06511; full reference not yet verified}); separately, \citet{chen2026}
predicted recurrence risk from preoperative and postoperative MRI across centres. None detects progression at the
scan, and we are not aware of a system that combines these components with each kind of evidence kept apart and the
system's own test results attached.

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 5); NUMBERS.md 10; UCSF_AUDIT.md; REPORT_V3_1.md Limits.
PRISM was developed at one centre, and the two external tests are its only evidence that the ranking transfers. The
label is progression as each centre documented it; no scan was re-read, and at UCSF most progression scans were also
rated as showing increased enhancement. Of the confidence, only average coverage met its planned threshold at all three centres, on point estimates: under the planned rule, disagreement marked the
less accurate scans at MU alone, and no UCSF scan fell in a tested situation. Subgroups are small, the analyses marked
\textdagger{} need testing on new data, and no clinician has yet read the reports. PRISM forecast progression within 120 days less well than it
detected progression at the scan (AUC 0.60, 0.52--0.69, in the intended use).

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (Discussion 6); RELATED_WORK.md section 3.
The next steps are to pool the rare situations (early progression after radiotherapy, and growing enhancement without
progression) across cohorts, including the Burdenko and UCSD-PTGBM datasets, and test models fine-tuned on them there;
and a reader study to show whether separate evidence and a stated confidence change clinicians' decisions.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

% Sources: OUTLINE.md (claim).
PRISM keeps image and treatment-timeline evidence apart and shows the clinician both scores at every scan with how
far each has been tested there. Its models ranked scans at two other centres without refitting, but what carried a
score differed by cohort, which is why the evidence is shown apart.
