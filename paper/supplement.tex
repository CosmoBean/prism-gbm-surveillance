\section{Estimator}\label{sec:S9}

Source: \code{docs/results/FIX\_EVALUATION.md}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Headline estimator.} The mean over the 5 repeats of the AUC of the pooled held-out
predictions of each repeat.
\item \textbf{Bootstrap.} Patient-cluster bootstrap, 1,000 draws; each draw resamples patients once and
recomputes the mean-over-repeats statistic of every model on that draw, so differences are paired. $\Delta$AUC
is the point estimate (not the bootstrap mean) with the bootstrap CI.
\item \textbf{Analyses with 2,000 draws.} The time-and-enhancement model, the AUC split, the group-conditional sets,
the segmentation comparison, and the analyses of calibration, of all scans before progression and of unknown marker
status use 2,000 draws.
\item \textbf{Selection inside folds.} Standardisation, imputation and the choice of penalty are all inside the
training fold, the penalty by inner patient-grouped cross validation
\citep{varma2006,cawley2010,demircioglu2021}.
\end{itemize}

\section{SwinUNETR encoder tuned on the task}\label{sec:S22}

\textbf{Plan.} Written into \code{docs/results/FINETUNE\_DEEP.md} and committed before any network saw a
real label; a dated copy (\code{PLAN\_snapshot.md}) was saved at 22:06 on 2026-10-15, and the first real-label job
finished at 22:08. Code: \code{prism/analyses/finetune\_deep.py} (stage \code{finetune}).

\textbf{Splits.} Outer: the shared 5 $\times$ 5 folds. Inside each outer training set, the scans with a known
120 day outcome were split into 4 inner folds grouped by patient. For each inner fold one network was tuned on the
other three, with early stopping on 20\% of their patients, and predicted the inner fold (out-of-fold score) and the
outer test fold. The test score is the mean logit of the 4 networks; the out-of-fold scores, z-scored, were stacked
onto the core model's predictors and the core ridge was refitted. Patients never overlap between fitting, stopping,
inner and outer test sets (unit test). Censored scans were excluded from the training loss and scored as usual.

\textbf{Network.} MONAI \citep{cardoso2022monai} SwinUNETR encoder (feature size 48; FLAIR, T1c, T1, T2) from BraTS 2021 or BrainSegFounder
weights. Input: a brain z-scored cube centred on the lesion and cavity, side $\max(128, \text{extent} + 16)$ mm,
resampled to 128$^3$ when larger (37 of 308 scans). Mask-weighted means of stages 2 to 4 within the lesion plus the
global mean of stage 4 (2,112 features), LayerNorm, dropout 0.3 and one linear output. Patch embedding and stages 1--2
frozen; stages 3--4 and the head trained with AdamW (encoder 2e-5, head 1e-3, weight decay 0.01), one warm-up epoch
and cosine decay over 25 epochs, batch 4, bf16, binary cross-entropy; early stopping with patience 5 and at least 3
epochs. Augmentation: flips, translation of $\pm$8 voxels, intensity scale 0.9--1.1 and shift $\pm$0.1. Prediction:
mean over 8 flips. Seeds fixed per job and deterministic kernels; repeated runs are bit identical. No hyperparameter
was tuned.

\textbf{Arms.} (1) BraTS 2021, grade 4 (primary); (2) the same with outcomes permuted within each outer training set
(control; the stacking ridge uses the real outcomes); (3) BraTS 2021, all gliomas; (4) BrainSegFounder, grade 4; (5)
optional, a siamese network that also sees the previous scan, cropped at the same atlas box. Each arm has 100
fine-tunes.

\textbf{Rule.} Fine-tuning helps if, in grade 4, the core model plus the arm 1 score gains at least 0.03 with a paired
95\% CI excluding 0. Validity: the permuted arm's image-only AUC near 0.5 with a CI covering 0.5, and its stack gain
$\leq$ 0. Other arms are secondary, without multiplicity correction.

\section{Report content against the BT-RADS follow-up template}\label{sec:S24}

Source: the BT-RADS follow-up reporting template (sample v1.03) and category guide (version 1.01)
\citep{btradsweb,weinberg2018}, and the report fields in \code{prism/report/facts\_v3.py} and \code{prism/report/sections\_v3.py}.
No patient record was used for this comparison.

\textbf{Why this template.} No public cohort releases clinician reports for glioma follow-up MRI after treatment,
including the three used here, so the report could not be compared with the reports written for the same scans. We
compared its content instead with the structured follow-up template of the Brain Tumor Reporting and Data System
(BT-RADS) \citep{weinberg2018}. In surveys of clinicians and radiologists at the centre that introduced it, taken before and
nine months after its adoption, reports were rated more consistent and less ambiguous after the change
\citep{gore2019}, and an analysis of the reports themselves found that they recorded more clinical history and used
fewer hedging words \citep{zhang2020}. In a survey of 220 European
centres, 60\% issued qualitative follow-up reports and 23\% used a template \citep{thust2018}.

and it gives the two findings BT-RADS asks of every follow-up, the change in FLAIR and in enhancement, as measured

and none was compared with a report written for the same scan.

\subsection{Group-conditional conformal sets}\label{sec:S26.2}

We calibrated the conformal sets within groups defined by the time window after radiotherapy and the enhancement
state, which are the situations of the reliability card, and compared them with the marginal sets of the plan. Each scan was calibrated at the finest level with at least 10 calibration patients, level 1 being its group,
level 2 its enhancement state over all time windows and level 3 all calibration scans, which is the marginal set. Most
groups hold few calibration patients, and small calibration sets give overly wide prediction regions \citep{johansson2015}, which is
why a group with fewer than 10 calibration patients falls back to a coarser level. Clustered conformal prediction
likewise gives classes with too few calibration examples the threshold pooled over all classes \citep{ding2023}. The
time after radiotherapy is unknown for every LUMIERE scan, so LUMIERE scans start at level 2. The nominal level was
80\% throughout. Under exchangeability within the group, level 1 and level 2 sets are conditional on the group, and
nothing holds at LUMIERE unless its scans are exchangeable with the MU calibration scans.

\subsection{Bound verifier}\label{sec:S26.3}

We added two rules to the frozen checker, which itself was not edited. The first binds each number
that sits next to a named quantity (enhancing tumour, FLAIR hyperintensity, necrosis, cavity, or one of the three
model scores) to a cited field for that quantity. The second binds each direction word (larger, increased, smaller,
decreased and similar) to every cited field that carries a direction. The rules of version 1.0 were fixed before any
report was checked. The verifier splits each sentence into clauses at commas, semicolons, colons and the word ``and''. The rules of
version 1.0 bound a number only to cited fields whose key names the quantity, and they
rejected every real report (276 of 276 from the language model and 276 of 276 from the template) and 24 of the 52
correct synthetic reports, because the statement fields name the quantity in their text and not in their key, as in
``enhancing tumour 3.5\,cc larger than at the previous scan''. This
pre-specified rule failed, and we report it as such. Version 1.1 also binds numbers and direction
words inside a cited text field to the quantity named in their clause. It was developed on the synthetic records
(52 of 52 correct reports passed) and on the development reports (47 of 48 from the language model and 48 of 48 from
the template), frozen, and then applied once to the evaluation reports.

The 52 synthetic records cover two tiers, both sides of each model's median, each of the three possible conformal
sets and the out-of-range flag.

Every evaluation report passed the frozen checker. Version 1.1 rejected 1 of the 114 MU reports of the language model
(by the direction rule), none of its 162 LUMIERE reports and none of the 276 template reports. These rejections were
not adjudicated, because the reports are not read in a hosted context. Table S15 gives the share of planted errors
rejected. The plan counts the verifier as closing a blind spot when it rejects at least 95\% of the synthetic mutants

The verifier closes the two blind spots known before (B1 and B2) and the held out rewrite of the direction (B3).
Diagnostics added after the run, which are exploratory, qualify the other rows. The frozen checker caught B4 and B5
only through its rule on required content, because each mutant replaces the sentence that stated the score or the
volume, and not through any binding of the number. The two binding rules alone reject every B1 to B5 mutant in every
set (52 of 52 synthetic mutants for each operator, 194 of 194 real B1 and B3 mutants for each writer, and 260 of 260
and 261 of 261 real B5 mutants for the language model and the template). C2 is not a blind spot. The operator removes
only the sentences that cite the statement field of the conformal set. In the 111 real reports per writer whose C2
mutant passes, another sentence still states the combined 80\% set by citing its fields or its flag, so the mutant is
a correct report, and in the other 165 the mutant is rejected. One blind spot remains, a tier word swapped to
``tested'' where the card has few cases (T2), which passes in 33\% to 45\% of real mutants because no rule names that
word. The template and the language model were caught at the same rate on every operator.

The planted errors above were written by us alongside the verifier. As an exploratory check, not in the plan, a
separate agent that had not seen the code of the checker or the verifier wrote new errors on the 52 synthetic records
(Table S16). Binding a value to its quantity generalised to these errors, with every value placed under the wrong
quantity rejected and no correct paraphrase rejected. Binding the direction catches only the direction words on its
list, so two thirds of the direction errors written in other words passed. Neither rule covers the side of a score
beyond the above or below pattern of the checker. We did not widen the word list, because doing so on this set would

\section{Details of PRISM's stages}\label{sec:S29}

% Sources: main.tex Methods (former SUPPLEMENT notes); prism/features/reseg.py; prism/audit/conformal.py;
% prism/report/prompts_v3_1.py, checker.py; REPORT_V3_1.md.
\subsection{Segmentation at LUMIERE}\label{sec:S29.1}

LUMIERE's images are stored on an atlas grid whose axis order differs from that of the networks' training data. PRISM
reorients each LUMIERE scan to the training order without resampling, runs the network, and reorients the label map
back.

\subsection{Measurements}\label{sec:S29.2}

Supplementary Table~S7 defines the 27 measurements. Nineteen describe the current scan: the log volumes of the
enhancing tumour, necrosis, FLAIR hyperintensity, resection cavity, tumour core and whole lesion; eight measures of
composition, shape and position relative to the cavity; and five measures of T1c and FLAIR intensity relative to
normal-appearing brain, taken as the brain more than 10\,mm from the lesion and the cavity. Eight describe change: the
log ratio of each of the six volumes to the previous scan, and of the enhancing tumour and whole lesion to the
patient's smallest volume so far.

Five measurements need the resection cavity: its volume, whether there is one, the share of enhancing tumour within
5\,mm of it, the distance of the enhancing tumour from it, and its change since the previous scan.

\subsection{Conformal sets}\label{sec:S29.3}

If no nonconformity score $s_i$ reaches the cumulative weight $(1-\alpha)(n+1)$, the threshold $\hat q$ is infinite
and every set holds both outcomes. When $\hat q < 0.5$, a score between $\hat q$ and $1 - \hat q$ gives an empty set.

\subsection{Report}\label{sec:S29.4}

The prompt asks for sentences in this order: the scan's timing; the volumes, called indicative; their change; each
model's score and what it is based on; whether the two models agree; the combined score and what a score means; its
80\% conformal set; the card's tier; and one sentence for each raised flag. Flags are raised for nearly every scan, so
the report presents the agreement and the card as descriptions of what the score rests on rather than as warnings.

The first prompt asked for the rater's three-month mark at LUMIERE in words that contain the number 3, but the record
entry held only ``yes'' or ``no''. The 3 was therefore in no cited entry, and the checker rejected every LUMIERE
report. We changed this entry to hold the full sentence with its number, wrote every number word in the record as
digits, made each line of the prompt appear only when its entry is in the record, and removed the mention of RANO.

Of six other local open-weight models of 2 to 35 billion parameters given the same prompt unchanged (main
Figure~8), only Gemma-4 31B also had every report pass. The reports regenerated with this paper's model
names are in Supplementary Section~S9.

\subsection{Checks}\label{sec:S29.5}

Besides the rules in main Section 2.2, the checker rejects a report that writes a number as a word. It rejects a
report that leaves out a raised flag, any of the three scores, the enhancing volume, or the tier where the card has
few cases or none. It also rejects a report that calls an input out of range when no flag says so, misstates a
conformal set or the agreement, or quotes a performance figure that is not on the card.

Because the checker accepts a number that equals a number in any cited entry and does not read direction words
(main Section 2.2), it accepts a sentence that gives the FLAIR volume as the enhancing volume while citing both
entries, or that writes ``larger'' where the record says ``smaller''. The verifier (Supplementary Section~S10.3) adds rules for both
cases.

\section{Details of the evaluation}\label{sec:S30}

% Sources: main.tex Methods (former SUPPLEMENT notes); UCSF_PLAN.md; CLINICIAN_BASELINE_PLAN.md;
% CHRISTODOULOU_REPLICATION.md (faithful run); MASK_SENSITIVITY_PLAN.md.
\subsection{What was fixed in advance at each external centre}\label{sec:S30.1}

At UCSF-ALPTDG every model was fixed in a written plan before any score was compared with the label. At LUMIERE the
combined model and its comparison with the time-and-enhancement model were fixed in advance, and the other scores are
exploratory.

\subsection{Time-and-enhancement model and timing alone}\label{sec:S30.2}

At MU the time window enters the time-and-enhancement model as indicators, with scans later than 12 weeks after
radiotherapy as the reference. Besides the days since the end of radiotherapy and the visit number used directly,
timing alone includes a ridge model on the days since the end of radiotherapy, the visit number, the days since
diagnosis and the 12-week flag. Externally, where these dates are missing, the unfitted timing score is the days
since surgery at LUMIERE and the days since the start of radiotherapy at UCSF-ALPTDG.

\subsection{Published radiomics pipeline}\label{sec:S30.3}

We ran the authors' extraction on all 594 MU scans with four sequences and a mask: N4 bias correction \citep{tustison2010} within the mask,
resampling to 1\,mm with B-spline interpolation, scaling to zero mean and unit variance within the mask, and one
region, the whole mask. The 4,892 features per scan are taken from each of the four sequences, from the original image
and from Laplacian of Gaussian ($\sigma$ 0.5 to 3\,mm) and wavelet filtered versions of it \citep{vangriethuysen2017}.

The tree model weights the two classes equally. Its settings are tuned over 400 trials of a Bayesian search (Optuna; \citealp{akiba2019}),
each trial scored by fivefold cross validation grouped by patient within the training set. The model's output is
mapped to a probability by Platt calibration \citep{platt1999}, a logistic fit to the held-out predictions of that cross validation.
The authors' ranking combines the size of L1 penalised logistic regression coefficients with permutation importance,
but their code computes permutation importance only in inner folds of at least 30 scans. Our inner folds held 13 to 16
scans, so the features were ranked by coefficient size alone. We left the code unchanged
\citep{christodoulou2026}.

\subsection{Rules of the segmentation comparison}\label{sec:S30.4}

The first analysis (Swin UNETR at MU, with the supplied cavity kept) required every paired CI to include zero. The
second and third (Swin UNETR and the BraTS 2024 model at all three centres) required every paired CI to include zero
or lie above it, so that a segmenter may raise a score but not lower it. All three required the combined model's gain
over the time-and-enhancement model to be read the same way under both mask sets, at MU in the first analysis and at
MU and LUMIERE in the others. The published pipeline also had to rank below PRISM's imaging model on the Swin UNETR
masks, or the comparison would be limited to the supplied masks.

\section{Further results}\label{sec:S31}

\subsection{Published radiomics pipeline}\label{sec:S31.1}

The pipeline's tuning AUC within the training folds averaged 0.70 and did not carry to the held-out folds. An earlier
re-implementation from the published description used original-image features only, selected the 256 by univariate
AUC and ran 40 search trials. It also reached 0.58 (0.50 to 0.66) in the intended use, and a logistic regression on
its 256 features reached 0.64 (exploratory).

We also ran the earlier re-implementation with the authors' patient-level label, which marks every scan of a patient
who ever progressed as progression (exploratory). Under repeated cross validation it reached 0.44 (0.38 to
0.50) in patients with glioblastoma, below age, sex, IDH and MGMT status alone (0.57). With patients of all diagnoses
labelled as the authors' code labels them, it reached 0.73 (0.66 to 0.78), against 0.81 (0.74 to 0.87) for the same
four clinical inputs. Over 100 held-out sets of 30 patients, it averaged 0.75.
