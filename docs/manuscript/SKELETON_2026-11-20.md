## 1 Introduction
1. **After treatment for glioblastoma, each follow-up MRI feeds a decision: continue, scan sooner, or change
   treatment, and progression and treatment effects can look alike on it.** [RANO and BT-RADS as other people's
   criteria; free-text practice in Europe (thust2018); the 12-week problem.]
2. **Machine learning models for this setting mostly score the image alone, and a high AUC can come from when a scan
   was taken rather than what it shows.** [Single-split evaluations; pseudoprogression clusters early after RT;
   shortcut learning; the published MU pipeline.]
3. **The tumour measurements these models use depend on a segmentation, and released masks and automatic segmenters
   do not agree closely, even between scans a few weeks apart.** [Post-treatment segmentation is harder than
   pre-operative (cavity, treatment change); the BraTS 2024 post-treatment challenge (deverdier2024); experts
   disagree on segmentation quality (hoebel2024); volumetry improves reproducibility (vollmuth2023).]
4. **A reader can use a score only if they can see what it rests on and how sure it is, and can trust a written
   report only as far as it can be checked.** [Structured reports with tables plus a short narrative are preferred
   (kowalczyk2025, plumb2009, gore2019); wrong AI categories mislead readers (dratsch2023); AI-written reports carry
   clinically significant errors (tanno2025); conformal sets (cresswell2024).]
5. **We present PRISM, a local system for glioma follow-up MRI that segments each scan, keeps imaging and
   clinical-history evidence as separate scores, states their uncertainty, and checks its written report against the
   scan's record.** ["Auditable" defined; four contributions.]
6. **PRISM is a software system rather than a new model: a chain of deterministic stages around a public
   segmentation network and one language model, running on one local computer.** [Tools underneath; the nearest
   systems, the Duke multi-agent BT-RADS system and BTReport, and how PRISM differs.]

## 2 Methods
### 2.1 Cohorts, label and intended use
7. **We developed PRISM on MU-Glioma-Post and tested it, locked, on LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG (Table 1).** [Grade 4;
   intended use; label definition; evaluation sets of 146, 178 and 69 scans; the masks each cohort releases (MU and
   UCSF: four post-treatment labels with cavity, refined by neuroradiologists; LUMIERE: three DeepBraTumIA labels,
   automatic); cohort figure.]

### 2.2 PRISM
8. **PRISM turns one follow-up scan and the patient's record into a checked report in six stages (Figure 1).**
9. **PRISM's first stage segments each scan into enhancing tumour, necrosis, FLAIR hyperintensity and resection
   cavity with a fixed public network, applied unchanged to every cohort (Figure 1b).**
   - Two segmenters: the BraTS 2021 Swin UNETR, which has no cavity class, and the BraTS 2024 post-treatment
     first-place ensemble, which has one.
   - The masks released with each cohort can be used in place of the segmenter; the primary results use them.
   - Inference settings; the LUMIERE reorientation.
10. **From the masks of the current and earlier scans, PRISM computes 27 measurements of the tumour and its change.**
    [Volumes, shape, intensity relative to normal brain, change since the previous scan and the smallest volume; with
    Swin UNETR, the five cavity measurements are dropped (22).]
11. **Three ridge logistic regressions score each scan: one on the images, one on the clinical history, one on both.**
    [Inputs; reduced form externally; penalty; "no dates" for imaging.]
12. **Three statements describe the evidence behind each score: a split-conformal set, the agreement of the imaging
    and clinical-history scores, and a reliability card.**
13. **Collation gathers every value into one record under a fixed key, with its source, and the language model writes
    the report from that record alone.** [Qwen3.8-27B, schema-constrained, no tools, no images; the segmentation
    slices are attached to the report deterministically.]
14. **A deterministic checker accepts the report or rejects it without repair, and a verifier ties each number and
    direction to its region.**
15. **The report's layout follows what referring clinicians ask for: tables of measurements with a short narrative,
    numbers instead of verbal probability, segmentation images beside the volumes, and no diagnosis or category.**
    [Evidence map from REPORT_DESIGN_EVIDENCE.md; volumes called indicative because segmentation quality varies
    (hoebel2024); BT-RADS elements carried, its category not.]

### 2.3 Evaluation
16. **On MU we estimated performance by five repeats of fivefold cross validation grouped by patient, then fitted each
    model once and applied it unchanged at both external centres.**
17. **Two reference points show what a score adds: a timing-and-enhancement baseline, and the published MU radiomics
    pipeline run with its authors' code.**
18. **We split each AUC into pairs of scans from the same time window and pairs from different windows, which
    separates ranking by the images from ranking by timing.**
19. **To test the segmentation stage, we rebuilt every measurement from each segmenter's masks, refitted the models on
    MU and applied them locked, and compared them with the released-mask results.** [Paired AUC differences
    (imaging, reduced combined, baseline) with the rule fixed in advance; Dice against the released masks;
    repeatability as the fold change of the enhancing volume between scans at most 30 days apart.]
20. **The tests, their rules and their outcomes were fixed in advance (Table of planned tests).** [Bootstrap;
    planned and exploratory labelling.]

## 3 Results
### 3.1 PRISM ranked scans at the development centre and at two external centres
21. **We first asked whether models fitted on one public cohort would keep ranking scans elsewhere.** [MU: combined
    0.82, clinical history 0.82, imaging 0.71. Locked reduced combined: 0.77 at LUMIERE and 0.86 at UCSF; imaging
    0.65 and 0.73 (Figure 2). Meaning: ranking transferred; probabilities need local recalibration.]

### 3.2 Timing and the enhancing tumour carry much of the ranking
22. **Because the scores are kept apart, we could ask what the ranking rests on.** [Baseline of the time window and
    enhancing tumour alone: 0.80 on MU, 0.73 locked at LUMIERE; combined gain +0.02 (−0.03 to +0.08).]
23. **Splitting the AUC showed that the clinical-history score ranks mainly by when the scan was taken, while the
    imaging score ranks within and across windows (Figure 3).**
24. **Holding the time window fixed, the image measurements ranked scans at about 0.68 within a window, and neither a
    stratified fit nor a wider penalty or window-specific enhancement raised it.** [A/B and S3; the across-window
    credit of 0.73 comes from timing.]

### 3.3 Against the published radiomics pipeline on the same task
25. **On the same scans, masks, label and folds, the published radiomics pipeline reached 0.58, against 0.82 for
    PRISM's clinical-history model.** [A second mask set; clinical data alone 0.53; deep networks.]

### 3.4 PRISM from the images alone
26. **The measurements PRISM relies on are noisy: between MU scans at most 30 days apart, the enhancing volume of the
    released masks changed by a median factor of 2.6.**
    - Swin UNETR: 2.2 at MU, more repeatable than the released masks (difference −0.17, −0.46 to −0.02).
    - At LUMIERE: 3.1 against 2.3, not confirmed, few pairs.

27. **With Swin UNETR in place of the released masks, PRISM's combined model and timing-and-enhancement baseline stayed

## 4 Discussion
33. **In this study we built PRISM and found that, once the scores are kept apart, timing and the enhancing tumour
    account for much of what a progression model ranks.**
34. **The same separation explains why PRISM's combined score held when its own segmenter replaced the released masks,
    while the imaging score moved: what the images add depends on how the tumour is outlined.** [Segmentation as
    the weakest link for image-based scores; a post-treatment segmenter with a cavity class (BraTS24 TBD); volumes
    stay indicative.]
35. **For a clinician, a PRISM report states what the scan shows, what each score rests on, and how far the evidence
    reaches at this scan.**
36. **Studies of glioma progression models could report the gain over a timing-and-enhancement baseline, the AUC
    split by time window, and how the result changes with the segmentation, beside the AUC.**
37. **PRISM differs from recent agent-based systems in where the language model sits: it writes from a fixed record
    and decides nothing.** [Duke, BTReport; why a checker is possible.]
38. **This study has limitations.**
    - First, one public development cohort.
    - Second, a documentation-based label.
    - Third, segmentation agreement is measured against released masks, which are themselves automatic or refined
      outputs, not a truth (LUMIERE's are fully automatic).
    - Fourth, small external samples and an approximated RT window at UCSF.
    - Fifth, no clinician has read the reports.
39. **A reader study and prospective use are left to future work.**

### Conclusion
40. **Keeping evidence separate and reports checkable showed what a progression score rests on, and the locked system,
    run from its own segmentation or from released masks, kept ranking scans at two external centres.**
