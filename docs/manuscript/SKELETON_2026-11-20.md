## 1 Introduction
1. **After treatment for glioblastoma, each follow-up MRI feeds a decision: continue, scan sooner, or change
   treatment, and progression and treatment effects can look alike on it.** [RANO and BT-RADS as other people's
   criteria; free-text practice in Europe (thust2018); the 12-week problem.]
2. **Machine learning models for this setting mostly score the image alone, and a high AUC can come from when a scan
   was taken rather than what it shows.** [Single-split evaluations; pseudoprogression clusters early after RT;
   shortcut learning; the published MU pipeline.]
3. **A reader can use a score only if they can see what it rests on and how sure it is, and can trust a written
   report only as far as it can be checked.** [Evidence: structured reports with tables plus a short narrative are
   preferred (kowalczyk2025, plumb2009, gore2019); wrong AI categories mislead readers (dratsch2023); AI-written
   reports carry clinically significant errors (tanno2025); conformal sets (cresswell2024).]
4. **We present PRISM, a local system for glioma follow-up MRI that keeps imaging and clinical-history evidence as
   separate scores, states their uncertainty, and checks its written report against the scan's record.**
   ["Auditable" defined; four contributions.]
5. **PRISM is a software system rather than a new model: a chain of deterministic stages around one language model,
   running on one local computer.** [Tools underneath; nearest systems, the Duke multi-agent BT-RADS system and
   BTReport, and how PRISM differs.]

## 2 Methods
### 2.1 Cohorts, label and intended use
6. **We developed PRISM on MU-Glioma-Post and tested it, locked, on LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG (Table 1).** [Grade 4;
   intended use; label definition; evaluation sets of 146, 178 and 69 scans; cohort figure.]

### 2.2 PRISM
7. **PRISM turns one follow-up scan and the patient's record into a checked report in six stages (Figure 1).**
8. **PRISM segments each scan, or takes the masks released with the cohort, and measures the tumour and its change.**
   [Swin UNETR (and BraTS 2024, TBD); released masks primary; 27 measurements.]
9. **Three ridge logistic regressions score each scan: one on the images, one on the clinical history, one on both.**
   [Inputs; reduced form externally; penalty; "no dates" for imaging.]
10. **Three statements describe the evidence behind each score: a split-conformal set, the agreement of the imaging
    and clinical-history scores, and a reliability card.**
11. **Collation gathers every value into one record under a fixed key, with its source, and the language model writes
    the report from that record alone.** [Qwen3.8-27B, schema-constrained, no tools, no images.]
12. **A deterministic checker accepts the report or rejects it without repair, and a verifier ties each number and
    direction to its region.**
13. **The report's layout follows what referring clinicians ask for: tables of measurements with a short narrative,
    numbers instead of verbal probability, and no diagnosis or category.** [Evidence map from
    REPORT_DESIGN_EVIDENCE.md; BT-RADS elements carried, its category not.]

### 2.3 Evaluation
14. **On MU we estimated performance by five repeats of fivefold cross validation grouped by patient, then fitted each
    model once and applied it unchanged at both external centres.**
15. **Two reference points show what a score adds: a timing-and-enhancement baseline, and the published MU radiomics
    pipeline run with its authors' code.**
16. **We split each AUC into pairs of scans from the same time window and pairs from different windows, which
    separates ranking by the images from ranking by timing.**
17. **The tests, their rules and their outcomes were fixed in advance (Table of planned tests).** [Bootstrap;
    planned and exploratory labelling.]

## 3 Results
### 3.1 PRISM ranked scans at the development centre and at two external centres
18. **We first asked whether models fitted on one public cohort would keep ranking scans elsewhere.** [MU: combined
    0.82, clinical history 0.82, imaging 0.71. Locked reduced combined: 0.77 at LUMIERE and 0.86 at UCSF; imaging
    0.65 and 0.73 (Figure 2). Meaning: ranking transferred; probabilities need local recalibration.]

### 3.2 Timing and the enhancing tumour carry much of the ranking
19. **Because the scores are kept apart, we could ask what the ranking rests on.** [Baseline of the time window and
    enhancing tumour alone: 0.80 on MU, 0.73 locked at LUMIERE; combined gain +0.02 (−0.03 to +0.08).]
20. **Splitting the AUC showed that the clinical-history score ranks mainly by when the scan was taken, while the
    imaging score ranks within and across windows (Figure 3).**
21. **Holding the time window fixed, the image measurements ranked scans at about 0.68 within a window, and neither a
    stratified fit nor a wider penalty or window-specific enhancement raised it.** [A/B and S3; the across-window
    credit of 0.73 comes from timing.]

### 3.3 Against the published radiomics pipeline on the same task
22. **On the same scans, masks, label and folds, the published radiomics pipeline reached 0.58, against 0.82 for
    PRISM's clinical-history model.** [A second mask set; clinical data alone 0.53; deep networks.]

### 3.4 PRISM from the images alone
23. **With its own segmenter in place of the released masks, PRISM's combined model and baseline stayed within 0.04 of
    their released-mask values at every centre.** [Swin UNETR: MU imaging 0.71 → 0.63; Dice ET 0.80 / 0.74 / 0.73;
    BraTS 2024 TBD.]

### 3.5 What the confidence statements say at each scan
24. **The combined score's 80% sets covered 82%, 87% and 78% of scans at MU, LUMIERE and UCSF, and named a single
    outcome for most.** [Figure 4; group-conditional sets.]
25. **At individual scans, the statements show when the evidence is thin (case strips).**

### 3.6 The reports and what the checker and verifier catch
26. **All 406 held-out reports passed the checker at the three centres.** [Prompt revised once; planned test.]
27. **The verifier rejected every planted direction error and misplaced value, and missed only errors outside its
    rules (verifier table; Figure 5).**

## 4 Discussion
28. **In this study we built PRISM and found that, once the scores are kept apart, timing and the enhancing tumour
    account for much of what a progression model ranks.**
29. **For a clinician, a PRISM report states what the scan shows, what each score rests on, and how far the evidence
    reaches at this scan.**
30. **Studies of glioma progression models could report the gain over a timing-and-enhancement baseline and the AUC
    split by time window beside the AUC.**
31. **PRISM differs from recent agent-based systems in where the language model sits: it writes from a fixed record
    and decides nothing.** [Duke, BTReport; why a checker is possible.]
32. **This study has limitations.** [First, one public development cohort. Second, a documentation-based label.
    Third, small external samples and an approximated RT window at UCSF. Fourth, no clinician has read the reports.]
33. **A reader study and prospective use are left to future work.**

### Conclusion
34. **Keeping evidence separate and reports checkable showed what a progression score rests on, and the locked system
    kept ranking scans at two external centres.**
