## 1 Introduction
1. **After treatment for glioblastoma, each follow-up MRI feeds a decision (continue, scan sooner, or change
   treatment), and early after radiotherapy, treatment effects can look like progression.** [The 12-week rule
   clinicians already apply; free-text reports.]
2. **Models proposed for this decision mostly score the image alone and report one AUC, which cannot show whether
   the ranking comes from the image or from facts the reader already has, such as when the scan was taken.**
3. **The image measurements themselves depend on how the tumour is outlined, and outlines from different sources
   disagree, even between scans a few weeks apart.** [Post-treatment segmentation; BraTS 2024.]
4. **We present PRISM, a local system that outlines the tumour on each scan, scores the scan separately from the
   images and from the clinical history, states how certain each score is, and writes a report that is checked
   against its source data.** [Contributions in one list; PRISM is fixed software around a public segmentation
   network and one language model that writes from a fixed record and decides nothing, unlike recent agent-based
   systems (Duke, BTReport).]

## 2 Methods
5. **We developed PRISM on MU-Glioma-Post and tested it without retraining on LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG, with the tests
   and their rules fixed in advance (Table 1, Table of planned tests).** [Label from clinical documentation, defined
   exactly; evaluation sets of 146, 178 and 69 scans.]
6. **PRISM outlines the tumour with a fixed public network, or uses the masks supplied with each dataset, and computes
   27 measurements of the tumour and its change (Figure 1).** [Swin UNETR (22 measurements, no cavity); BraTS 2024
   post-treatment ensemble (27); the primary results use the supplied masks.]
7. **Three logistic regression models score each scan: one on the images, one on the clinical history, and one on
   both. The imaging model is never given dates.** [Inputs; where a centre lacks treatment dates, a reduced
   clinical history is used.]
8. **Each score comes with an 80% set of possible outcomes, a statement of whether the imaging and clinical-history
   scores agree, and the measured performance in comparable scans.**
9. **All values go into one record with their sources. A local language model writes the report from that record
   alone, and two rule-based programs check every number and every direction against it.** [Layout: tables of
   measurements with a short narrative, no diagnosis or category; evidence for the design in the supplement.]
10. **We judged each score against a simple model of the time since radiotherapy and the enhancing tumour volume, and
    split its AUC into scans taken at a similar time and scans taken at different times.** [The published MU
    radiomics pipeline as a second reference; how each segmenter changes the results.]

## 3 Results
### 3.1 Performance at three centres
11. **Without retraining, the combined model ranked scans at 0.77 at LUMIERE and 0.86 at UCSF, against 0.82 at MU.**
    [Imaging 0.71 / 0.65 / 0.73; the probabilities need local recalibration (Figure 2).]
12. **The published radiomics pipeline, run with its authors' code on the same MU scans, masks and folds, reached 0.58,
    below PRISM's imaging model (0.71).**

### 3.2 What the ranking rests on
13. **At MU, the time since radiotherapy alone ranked scans at 0.77, and the time-and-enhancement model at 0.80, so
    the combined model added little there (+0.02, −0.03 to +0.08).** [56 of 62 progression scans fell later after
    radiotherapy; part of this follows from how the cohort was followed and labelled.]
14. **Timing did not carry over to the other centres (0.61 at LUMIERE, 0.54 at UCSF), yet the combined model held,
    because there the enhancing tumour did more of the work.** [UCSF: enhancing volume alone 0.88; scans brought
    forward ranked as progression.]
15. **Among scans taken at a similar time after radiotherapy, the images ranked progression at about 0.68, and no
    change to the model raised it (Figure 3).** [Timing alone ranks at chance within a window; the clinical-history
    score ranks mainly across windows.]

### 3.3 Effect of segmentation
16. **The enhancing volume is a noisy measurement: between MU scans at most 30 days apart it changed by a median
    factor of 2.6 with the supplied masks and 2.2 with Swin UNETR.**
17. **With PRISM's own Swin UNETR outlines, the combined and time-and-enhancement models changed by at most 0.04 at
    every centre, but the imaging model fell from 0.71 to 0.63 at MU.** [Dice in a table; BraTS24 TBD: whether the
    post-treatment segmenter, which also outlines the cavity, closes the gap.]

### 3.4 Certainty and reports
18. **The 80% sets contained the true outcome in 82%, 87% and 78% of scans at the three centres, and named a single
    outcome in 75%, 62% and [x]%.** [Figure 4; case strips show single scans.]
19. **All 406 reports passed the format check, and the second check caught every planted wrong number and wrong
    direction. Both checks test agreement with the record, not clinical accuracy.**

## 4 Discussion
20. **Keeping the scores separate showed where a progression model's ranking comes from: the time since radiotherapy
    at the development centre, the enhancing tumour elsewhere, and about 0.68 from the images within a time window.**
21. **The same separation explains why the combined score survived a change of segmenter while the imaging score did
    not.**
22. **Studies of progression models could report three things beside the AUC: the gain over a time-and-enhancement
    model, the AUC within time windows, and how the result changes with the segmenter.**
23. **This study has limitations.** [One public development cohort; a label from clinical documentation; reference
    masks that are not ground truth; small external samples; no perfusion or diffusion imaging; no clinician has
    read the reports, so a reader study comes next.]

### Conclusion
24. **Without retraining, PRISM kept ranking scans at two external centres, and keeping its evidence separate showed
    what that ranking rests on.**
