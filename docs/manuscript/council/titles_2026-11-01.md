# Title council (2026-11-01)

Four personas (neuroradiologist, hostile methods reviewer, journal editor, science writer) each proposed six titles
from the same brief (contributions as narrowed by docs/literature/PRIOR_ART_2026-11-01.md). Previous title, judged a
mouthful by the author: "Which evidence carries a glioma progression score? PRISM separates image and treatment
evidence on surveillance MRI".

Where they agreed
- Drop PRISM from the title (no search value; collides with PRISMA; reads as a software announcement). Name it in the
  abstract and keywords.
- The question "timing (or scan/schedule) or tumour?" is the clinician's question and cannot be overclaimed.
- The newest result is that the evidence behind the same locked score differs between centres.
- Keep "language model", "first", "explainable", "trustworthy", "drives", "external validation", RANO out of the title.

Shortlist (word counts)
1. Timing or tumour? The evidence behind glioma progression scores on post-treatment MRI (12) -- adopted, pending author approval
2. Scan or schedule? Image and timeline evidence for glioma progression on surveillance MRI (13) -- neuroradiologist's pick
3. The evidence behind a locked glioma progression score shifts between centres (11) -- methods reviewer's pick
4. Separating image and treatment-timing evidence of glioma progression versus pseudoprogression on MRI (12) -- editor's pick for indexing
5. What carries a glioma progression score depends on how the cohort was scanned and labelled (15) -- the thesis line; vague without the abstract

Cautions raised: "separating" can read as causal disentanglement (methods); "pseudoprogression" in the title promises
pathology-confirmed labels, which we do not have; "shifts between centres" is two external centres, so name them early.
