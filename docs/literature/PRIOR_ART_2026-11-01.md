### 0. What is clinical knowledge (do not claim it)

- Pseudoprogression is concentrated in the first ~12 weeks after chemoradiotherapy, and more common with MGMT
  methylation (Taal 2008, Brandes 2008; RANO 2010/2.0). Already cited.
- Label definition depends on phase: RANO 2.0 needs confirmation of progression within 12 weeks of RT (Wen 2023). Cited.
- Reference-standard heterogeneity in PsP ML studies (follow-up imaging vs histology; 0-12 wk vs up to 6 mo windows) is
  documented by Booth's reviews (booth2022 is cited; see 1.5).
So "timing matters clinically" and "labels differ between studies" are NOT ours. What could be ours is a measurement on
ML scores: how much of a score's AUC is the schedule, and how image vs timeline evidence compare inside a phase.

### 3. Verdict

**C1 (the finding): PARTIAL.**
- The *ingredients* exist: timing as the top feature of a PsP model (Gomaa 2025), scan interval flagged as dataset-level
  signal in LUMIERE (TRACE 2026), evaluation at separate follow-up stages (Guo & Mirzaei 2025), process variables
  explaining image-model skill (Badgeley 2019), within-stratum evaluation (Janes & Pepe 2008; Oakden-Rayner 2020),
  site-specific labelling (Guo 2024; Castro 2020; Lasko 2024), incorporation bias (Worster 2008).
- Not found: any glioma progression/PsP study that (a) compares image vs timeline evidence *within* phases of care, (b)
  shows on locked external cohorts that the dominant driver of the same score differs between centres, or (c) links one
  centre's apparent image signal to the label definition (enhancement-called progression). Strength: moderate (focused
  web search; Gomaa/TRACE show the field is close).

**Wording we can defend**
- "Timing is known to carry much of a pseudoprogression model's output (Gomaa 2025) and scan interval has been flagged as
  dataset-level signal in LUMIERE (Tarek 2026). We measure it: within a phase of care at MU, images matched the timeline
  (0.68 vs 0.64), and the evidence that carried the score differed between centres."
- "We found no study of post-treatment glioma progression that reports image and timeline evidence within phases of care,
  or compares what carries the score across externally tested centres."
- Present the within-phase audit as a covariate-specific AUC (Janes & Pepe) / hidden-stratification audit
  (Oakden-Rayner) applied to phase of care; present the UCSF result as an instance of incorporation bias / annotation shift.

**Wording to avoid**
- "first to show that timing drives progression models" (Gomaa, TRACE); "we discover a new shortcut"; "timing confounding
  is unrecognised"; "within-phase evaluation is new" (Guo & Mirzaei did stage-wise evaluation; Janes & Pepe is the method).
- Any implication that pseudoprogression's early concentration is our finding.
- "labels are wrong at UCSF" - say progression was called on enhancement, so an enhancement-based score is partly
  scored against itself.

**Must cite (missing from paper/refs.bib)**
1. Tarek et al. 2026, TRACE, arXiv:2606.30313 - closest statement that scan timing is dataset-level signal on LUMIERE.
2. Guo & Mirzaei 2025, *Cancers* 18:36 - stage-specific evaluation in GBM follow-up.
3. Janes & Pepe 2008, *Am J Epidemiol* - the method behind within-phase AUC; or Oakden-Rayner 2020 CHIL (treated-subgroup audit).
4. Badgeley 2019 (and/or Agniel 2018) - process variables explain image-model skill; listed in SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE but absent from refs.bib.
Optional: Worster & Carpenter 2008 (incorporation bias) for the UCSF sentence; Castro 2020 or Lasko 2024 for label/annotation shift.

### 2. What the adversarial search establishes

- **Separate modality models plus a fused score** are standard in oncology (Boehm 2022, Vanguri 2022) and in glioma PsP
  nomograms (Zhou 2026). **Per-patient modality attribution** exists (Chen 2022, including glioma; HAIM 2022). Keeping
  pathways apart is therefore not new. The defensible point is narrower: PRISM's second pathway is the **treatment timeline**,
  each pathway's score is **shown at each scan** next to the combined score, and the gap between them is used as a flag.
- **Disagreement between modalities as uncertainty** is published (EnTrust 2026), but for segmentation across image
  sequences. We found no image-versus-timeline disagreement flag for a scan-level outcome.
- **Per-case confidence parts** each exist separately: conformal triage at deployment (Angelopoulos 2024), stratum-
  conditional conformal (Lu 2022, in the landscape), reliability from similar cases (Habas 2007), and claim-level calibrated
  confidence with deferral (TumorBoard 2026). We found **no system that combines conformal set, pathway disagreement and a
  measured situation-specific record into one per-case statement**, in glioma or in medical imaging generally.
- **Provenance**: TumorBoard (2026) already has a per-claim evidence ledger in longitudinal neuro-oncology, so "records the
  source of every value" cannot be claimed as new. It differs from PRISM in using reports rather than images and LLM
  entailment rather than a deterministic check.
- **Locked external tests on these cohorts**: CLARITY (ECCV 2026) applied an MU-trained model to UCSF-ALPTDG without
  fine-tuning, and an uncited repository (Bio-SSM-Flow) ran a frozen-plan external test on UCSF and MU. Both concern survival
  or image forecasting, not progression. We found no scan-level progression/PsP model tested locked at **two** external
  centres against transfer rules committed in advance.

### 3. Verdicts

**Contribution 2 (system): PARTIAL.** Every element has a precedent. Only the combination, applied to glioma
surveillance with a timeline pathway, was not found.
- Defensible: "PRISM keeps an image pathway and a treatment-timeline pathway as separate scores shown with the combined
  score at each scan, and attaches a confidence built from three parts (a conformal set, the disagreement between pathways,
  and the measured record in that scan's phase of care and enhancement state). We found no glioma surveillance system that
  does this." Credit each component: late fusion (Boehm 2022), modality-conflict uncertainty (EnTrust 2026), conformal
  triage (Angelopoulos 2024), case-level reliability (Habas 2007), evidence ledger (TumorBoard 2026).
- Avoid: "first system", "novel architecture", "unlike existing multimodal models, which fuse..." (late fusion with
  visible components is common). Do not present "records the source of every value" or "runs on whatever a centre
  records" as new (TumorBoard, HAIM, the late-fusion literature). Keep them as design properties, not contributions.

**Contribution 3 (locked external tests): PARTIAL, but narrow and well supported.**
- Defensible: "Models fitted once on MU were applied unchanged at LUMIERE and UCSF-ALPTDG under transfer rules committed in
  advance. To our knowledge, external tests of scan-level progression models after glioma treatment are rare
  (\citealp{shi2025}: 3 of 20) and usually use one centre."
- Avoid: "first locked/zero-shot evaluation on UCSF-ALPTDG" (CLARITY did MU→UCSF without fine-tuning), and "first
  pre-specified external validation on these public cohorts" (Bio-SSM-Flow has a frozen-plan repository).

### Adversarial points a reviewer can raise

1. **The architecture is not new.** BTReport (A1) already does segmentation -> structured features -> local open-weight LLM (no images) for glioma, with a "use only the metadata" instruction; Valerio 2025 (A2) does the same with JSON and several open model families. We cannot claim the design.
2. **The deterministic check is not new in principle.** Zeba 2026 and PROVE 2026 (already in SYSTEM_LANDSCAPE) check LLM numbers against source by rule; Wiseman 2017 established record-matching of generated numbers. What we have not found is a deterministic, accept/reject checker applied to every report at run time **in imaging/neuro-oncology**, plus a perturbation test (28/30 reports followed a changed field).
3. **Model comparison is confounded.** The prompt was revised for Qwen3.8-27B, then reused for the six others; "only the two largest dense models passed" may partly reflect prompt fit. The dense-vs-MoE contrast is one MoE model (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, 3B active). State it as an observation, not a size/architecture law. C1 gives an independent, human-rated MoE-worse data point.
4. **"Passed" is lexical.** As main.tex already says, the check cannot judge emphasis or inference (Thomson & Reiter's context errors).

### Verdict

**C4: PARTIAL.** Local open-weight LLMs writing neuro-oncology reports from segmentation-derived structured records,
without images, already exist (BTReport 2026; Valerio 2025), and rule-based numeric checking of LLM text exists outside
imaging (Zeba 2026, PROVE 2026) with roots in data-to-text (Wiseman 2017). Not found: (i) such a report for
post-treatment surveillance carrying scores and confidence, (ii) a deterministic checker that gates **every** delivered
report against its record, with a field-perturbation test, (iii) evaluation across three cohorts, (iv) a seven-model
local comparison under one fixed checker.

Defensible wording:
- "As in earlier work that had local language models write brain-tumour reports from extracted measurements \citep{herasrivera2026,valerio2025}, the model sees no images; PRISM adds a deterministic check of every report against its record, which all 406 reports passed after one prompt revision."
- "Of seven local models given the same prompt, written for Qwen3.8-27B, only the two largest dense models passed in every cohort; a single mixture-of-experts model did not, consistent with more errors from a mixture-of-experts model in radiology report simplification \citep{proff2026}."

Avoid: "first LLM report grounded in model outputs"; "hallucination-free reports" (lexical check only); "dense models are more faithful than MoE" or "model size determines faithfulness" (n=7, one MoE, prompt tuned for one model); "the LLM is verified" (the report is checked, not the model).
