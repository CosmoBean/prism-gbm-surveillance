# Related Work: Post-Treatment Glioma Progression Prediction

As of 2026-10-11. Exported from the shared related-work doc; the doc is the editable copy.

## Scope

The project predicts whether a glioma (mostly glioblastoma) will progress within 120 days of a post-treatment MRI. It uses clinical, timing, treatment and imaging features from MU-Glioma-Post, with LUMIERE as an external test. Its main finding is that conventional radiomics adds no measurable value over a treatment-aware clinical baseline.

The papers are grouped by how they relate to that question. Each group lists up to 10, ranked by how directly they bear on it: same task and data first, then adjacent tasks, then general methods. Each entry is in APA 7 format with a DOI or publisher link. The citation details were checked against the source page, so verify the final formatting with your reference manager before you submit.

## 1. Pseudoprogression vs true progression classifiers

This is the closest task in the literature. Meta-analyses put pooled ML AUC near 0.77 with high risk of bias, and externally validated models typically lose 0.05–0.15 AUC.

1. Booth, T. C., Grzeda, M., Chelliah, A., Roman, A., Al Busaidi, A., Dragos, C., Shuaib, H., Luis, A., Mirchandani, A., Alparslan, B., Mansoor, N., Lavrador, J., Vergani, F., Ashkan, K., Modat, M., & Ourselin, S. (2022). Imaging biomarkers of glioblastoma treatment response: A systematic review and meta-analysis of recent machine learning studies. *Frontiers in Oncology, 12*, Article 799662. [link](https://doi.org/10.3389/fonc.2022.799662)
   - *Why it matters:* Across 18 ML studies, pooled sensitivity 0.77 and specificity 0.65 (AUC ≈ 0.77); high risk of bias, a third without a hold-out test set.
2. Bhandari, A., Marwah, R., Smith, J., Nguyen, D., Bhatti, A., Lim, C. P., & Lasocki, A. (2022). Machine learning imaging applications in the differentiation of true tumour progression from treatment-related effects in brain tumours: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Medical Imaging and Radiation Oncology, 66*(6), 781–797. [link](https://doi.org/10.1111/1754-9485.13436)
   - *Why it matters:* Across 25 studies, pooled sensitivity 95.2% and specificity 82.4% for glioma, but quality problems and little external validation block clinical use.
3. Kim, J. Y., Park, J. E., Jo, Y., Shim, W. H., Nam, S. J., Kim, J. H., Yoo, R.-E., Choi, S. H., & Kim, H. S. (2019). Incorporating diffusion- and perfusion-weighted MRI into a radiomics model improves diagnostic performance for pseudoprogression in glioblastoma patients. *Neuro-Oncology, 21*(3), 404–414. [link](https://doi.org/10.1093/neuonc/noy133)
   - *Why it matters:* Multiparametric radiomics scored AUC 0.90 in training (n = 61) and 0.85 externally (n = 34), a rare externally validated benchmark.
4. Akbari, H., Rathore, S., Bakas, S., Nasrallah, M. P., Shukla, G., Mamourian, E., Rozycki, M., Bagley, S. J., Rudie, J. D., Flanders, A. E., Dicker, A. P., Desai, A. S., O'Rourke, D. M., Brem, S., Lustig, R., Mohan, S., Wolf, R. L., Bilello, M., Martinez-Lage, M., & Davatzikos, C. (2020). Histopathology-validated machine learning radiographic biomarker for noninvasive discrimination between true progression and pseudo-progression in glioblastoma. *Cancer, 126*(11), 2625–2636. [link](https://doi.org/10.1002/cncr.32790)
   - *Why it matters:* Fell from AUC 0.92 (leave-one-out) to 0.80 in an inter-institutional cohort (n = 20), showing the internal-to-external drop.
5. Taylor, C., Ekert, J. O., Sefcikova, V., Fersht, N., & Samandouras, G. (2022). Discriminators of pseudoprogression and true progression in high-grade gliomas: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Scientific Reports, 12*, Article 13258. [link](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-16726-x)
   - *Why it matters:* Across 30 studies (1,372 patients), DWI and DSC perfusion reach summary AUC 0.93, and DCE 0.90; these are sequences MU-Glioma-Post lacks.
6. Elshafeey, N., Kotrotsou, A., Hassan, A., Elshafei, N., Hassan, I., Ahmed, S., Abrol, S., Agarwal, A., El Salek, K., Bergamaschi, S., Acharya, J., Moron, F. E., Law, M., Fuller, G. N., Huse, J. T., Zinn, P. O., & Colen, R. R. (2019). Multicenter study demonstrates radiomic features derived from magnetic resonance perfusion images identify pseudoprogression in glioblastoma. *Nature Communications, 10*, Article 3170. [link](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11007-0)
   - *Why it matters:* Perfusion radiomics reached AUC 0.89 across three centres (n = 98), but the prospective validation cohort had only 7 patients.
7. Jang, B.-S., Jeon, S. H., Kim, I. H., & Kim, I. A. (2018). Prediction of pseudoprogression versus progression using machine learning algorithm in glioblastoma. *Scientific Reports, 8*, Article 12516. [link](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-31007-2)
   - *Why it matters:* A CNN-LSTM on post-contrast MRI plus clinical features reached AUC 0.83 at a second site (test n = 19) and beat MRI-only and clinical-only models.
8. van Dijken, B. R. J., van Laar, P. J., Holtman, G. A., & van der Hoorn, A. (2017). Diagnostic accuracy of magnetic resonance imaging techniques for treatment response evaluation in patients with high-grade glioma, a systematic review and meta-analysis. *European Radiology, 27*(10), 4129–4144. [link](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-017-4789-9)
   - *Why it matters:* Anatomical MRI pooled only 68% sensitivity / 77% specificity vs 87% / 86% for DSC perfusion, explaining why conventional-MRI features add little.
9. Moassefi, M., Faghani, S., Conte, G. M., Kowalchuk, R. O., Vahdati, S., Crompton, D. J., Perez-Vega, C., Cabreja, R. A. D., Vora, S. A., Quiñones-Hinojosa, A., Parney, I. F., Trifiletti, D. M., & Erickson, B. J. (2022). A deep learning model for discriminating true progression from pseudoprogression in glioblastoma patients. *Journal of Neuro-Oncology, 159*(2), 447–455. [link](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11060-022-04080-x)
   - *Why it matters:* A 3D DenseNet on T1c and T2 (n = 124) reached a mean cross-validated AUC of only 0.76 with no external test, close to our numbers.
10. Patel, P., Baradaran, H., Delgado, D., Askin, G., Christos, P., John Tsiouris, A., & Gupta, A. (2017). MR perfusion-weighted imaging in the evaluation of high-grade gliomas after treatment: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Neuro-Oncology, 19*(1), 118–127. [link](https://doi.org/10.1093/neuonc/now148)
   - *Why it matters:* Across 28 studies, DSC and DCE perfusion pooled about 90% / 88% and 89% / 85% sensitivity / specificity, a non-ML reference standard.

## 2. Predicting progression from post-treatment MRI surveillance

These are the most direct comparators. Christodoulou et al. (2026) work on the same MU-Glioma-Post task (AUC 0.80, one split, no external test). The only longitudinal study on LUMIERE-type data, Suter et al. (2024), tops out at AUC 0.75.

1. Christodoulou, R. C., Vamvouras, G., Pitsillos, R., Solomou, E. E., & Georgiou, M. F. (2026). Explainable radiomics with probability calibration for postoperative glioblastoma surveillance. *European Journal of Radiology Artificial Intelligence, 5*, Article 100074. [link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrai.2026.100074)
   - *Why it matters:* Scan-level progression radiomics on MU-Glioma-Post follow-up MRI; AUC 0.80 on one held-out patient split, no external validation.
2. Suter, Y., Schuhmacher, F., Ermis, E., Knecht, U., Schucht, P., Wiest, R., & Reyes, M. (2024). Towards radiomics-based automated disease progression assessment for glioblastoma patients. In *Brainlesion: Glioma, multiple sclerosis, stroke and traumatic brain injuries* (Lecture Notes in Computer Science, Vol. 14668, pp. 36–47). Springer. [link](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-76160-7_4)
   - *Why it matters:* Current plus change radiomics for progression on LUMIERE-type data; best ROC-AUC 0.748 (random forest).
3. Kickingereder, P., Isensee, F., Tursunova, I., Petersen, J., Neuberger, U., Bonekamp, D., Brugnara, G., Schell, M., Kessler, T., Foltyn, M., Harting, I., Sahm, F., Prager, M., Nowosielski, M., Wick, A., Nolden, M., Radbruch, A., Debus, J., Schlemmer, H. P., . . . Maier-Hein, K. H. (2019). Automated quantitative tumour response assessment of MRI in neuro-oncology with artificial neural networks: A multicentre, retrospective study. *The Lancet Oncology, 20*(5), 728–740. [link](<https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(19)30098-1>)
   - *Why it matters:* Deep-learning volumetric time to progression, externally tested on 2,034 scans (532 patients); a better survival surrogate than manual RANO.
4. Chen, C., Guo, F., Huang, D., Zheng, Y., Feng, Y., Liu, T., Yao, Y., Wei, J., Zheng, M., & Liu, Y. (2026). Predicting recurrence risk of glioblastoma based on preoperative-postoperative longitudinal MRI: A multicenter study. *Bioengineering, 13*(6), Article 668. [link](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering13060668)
   - *Why it matters:* Longitudinal deep model for 6-month recurrence (n = 139); AUC fell from 0.887 internal to 0.762 external, the same drop pattern as ours.
5. Hashim, U., Jalal, A., Jabali, A. N., & Rana, H. (2026). Radiomics outperforms conventional tumor volume in prognostic stratification across independent glioma cohorts. *Cureus, 18*(7), Article e112298. [link](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.112298)
   - *Why it matters:* Uses MU-Glioma-Post (n = 194) for post-treatment PFS; volume C-index 0.676, with the volume–PFS link inconsistent externally.
6. Cepeda, S., Luppino, L. T., Pérez-Núñez, A., Solheim, O., García-García, S., Velasco-Casares, M., Karlberg, A., Eikenes, L., Sarabia, R., Arrese, I., Zamora, T., Gonzalez, P., Jiménez-Roldán, L., & Kuttner, S. (2023). Predicting regions of local recurrence in glioblastomas using voxel-based radiomic features of multiparametric postoperative MRI. *Cancers, 15*(6), Article 1894. [link](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers15061894)
   - *Why it matters:* Voxel radiomics on postoperative MRI predicts where recurrence appears (n = 55); test AUC 0.81 on only 15 patients.
7. Kwak, S., Akbari, H., Garcia, J. A., Mohan, S., Dicker, Y., Sako, C., Matsumoto, Y., Nasrallah, M. P., Shalaby, M., O'Rourke, D. M., Shinohara, R. T., Liu, F., Badve, C., Barnholtz-Sloan, J. S., Sloan, A. E., Lee, M., Jain, R., Cepeda, S., Chakravarti, A., . . . Davatzikos, C. (2024). Predicting peritumoral glioblastoma infiltration and subsequent recurrence using deep-learning-based analysis of multi-parametric magnetic resonance imaging. *Journal of Medical Imaging, 11*(5), Article 054001. [link](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JMI.11.5.054001)
   - *Why it matters:* Leave-one-site-out validation across six institutions; an example of the multi-site validation design reviewers expect.
8. Zimmer, L., Weidner, J., Balcerak, M., Kofler, F., Krupa, M., Ezhov, I., Cepeda, S., Zhang, R. Z., Lowengrub, J. S., Menze, B., & Wiestler, B. (2026). [link](https://doi.org/10.1227/NEU.0000000000001202)
   - *Why it matters:* Foundational ML prediction of where GBM recurs from preoperative MRI (31 patients); mean AUC 0.84 in replication.

## 5. Clinical, molecular and treatment baselines

## 6. Censoring and validation methodology

These papers back the methods: IPCW labels, AUC(120 d), discrete-time hazards, nested CV, calibration and reporting.

1. Vock, D. M., Wolfson, J., Bandyopadhyay, S., Adomavicius, G., Johnson, P. E., Vazquez-Benitez, G., & O'Connor, P. J. (2016). [link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2016.03.009)
   - *Why it matters:* The reference for IPCW-weighted binary classifiers on censored data, the paper's main fix for censored 120-day labels.
2. Blanche, P., Dartigues, J.-F., & Jacqmin-Gadda, H. (2013). Estimating and comparing time-dependent areas under receiver operating characteristic curves for censored event times with competing risks. *Statistics in Medicine, 32*(30), 5381–5397. [link](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.5958)
   - *Why it matters:* Gives the IPCW estimator of AUC(t) and its inference (timeROC), the paper's main evaluation metric.
3. Uno, H., Cai, T., Tian, L., & Wei, L. J. (2007). Evaluating prediction rules for t-year survivors with censored regression models. *Journal of the American Statistical Association, 102*(478), 527–537. [link](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214507000000149)
   - *Why it matters:* Sets out IPCW-based accuracy measures for t-year risk prediction under censoring, the same setup as fixed 120-day progression.
4. Heagerty, P. J., Lumley, T., & Pepe, M. S. (2000). Time-dependent ROC curves for censored survival data and a diagnostic marker. *Biometrics, 56*(2), 337–344. [link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0006-341X.2000.00337.x)
   - *Why it matters:* Introduced cumulative/dynamic time-dependent ROC curves for censored outcomes, the basis of AUC(t) evaluation.
5. Tutz, G., & Schmid, M. (2016). *Modeling discrete time-to-event data*. Springer. [link](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-28158-2)
   - *Why it matters:* The standard reference for discrete-time hazard models, the paper's alternative to binary labels at a fixed horizon.
6. Varma, S., & Simon, R. (2006). Bias in error estimation when using cross-validation for model selection. *BMC Bioinformatics, 7*, Article 91. [link](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91)

7. Cawley, G. C., & Talbot, N. L. C. (2010). On over-fitting in model selection and subsequent selection bias in performance evaluation. *Journal of Machine Learning Research, 11*(70), 2079–2107. [link](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html)
   - *Why it matters:* Shows that over-fitting during model selection biases estimates, supporting the nested, repeated grouped CV design.
8. Van Calster, B., McLernon, D. J., van Smeden, M., Wynants, L., & Steyerberg, E. W. (2019). Calibration: The Achilles heel of predictive analytics. *BMC Medicine, 17*, Article 230. [link](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7)

## 7. Radiomics reproducibility, validation pitfalls and reporting

These papers frame the negative result. Single splits, leaky feature selection and missing volume or clinical baselines are known ways radiomics performance gets inflated.

   - *Why it matters:* Shows published radiomic signatures can be surrogates for tumour volume; motivates volume/clinical baselines and random-feature controls.
2. An, C., Park, Y. W., Ahn, S. S., Han, K., Kim, H., & Lee, S.-K. (2021). Radiomics machine learning study with a small sample size: Single random training-test set split may lead to unreliable results. *PLOS ONE, 16*(8), Article e0256152. [link](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256152)

3. Demircioğlu, A. (2021). Measuring the bias of incorrect application of feature selection when using cross-validation in radiomics. *Insights into Imaging, 12*(1), Article 172. [link](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13244-021-01115-1)
